## About Nurador

Set in the foothills of the Kaltera Mountains within the rugged Matahal Valley, Nurador appears as a graceful cluster of white-stone buildings with tall roofs and carved spires, surrounded by terraced gardens and winding paths. The architecture blends elven elegance with stout half-orc stonework—a visible reminder of the settlement’s founding by elven scholars and the half-orc outcasts who later found sanctuary and purpose here. Visitors sense the careful balance that governs life: elves and half-orcs working side by side in governance, craft, and defense. Those who stay can trade in finely wrought arms and illuminated texts, seek insight or solace at the blended shrines (neutral ground for all), or explore the old ruins left by the Awakening. 

### **The People**

Nurador is home to roughly 750–800 inhabitants, split nearly evenly between elves and half-orcs with a small number of humans and others. The population respects its blended heritage without pretense: elves contribute artistry, magical learning, and measured governance, while half-orcs supply strength in defense, construction, and practical craft. Outsiders are treated fairly if they abide by the laws and the settlement’s emphasis on shared responsibility.

### **Beliefs**

Elves traditionally follow Myrdris, god of magic and knowledge, and Sylunara, goddess of transformation and the moons. Half-orcs honor Muerg, primal god of the earth, and Brault, god of might and battle. Over time these practices merged: elven shrines now carry stone carvings of strength, and half-orc rites often occur beneath moonlight. The four shrines stand as neutral ground; violence or theft there is judged a crime against the entire village. Traditions stress balance, personal transformation, and the idea that real power comes from standing together rather than apart.

### **Economy and Trade**

The foothill location limits large-scale farming, yet Nurador has adapted through terraced gardens that yield grains, herbs, and hardy vegetables alongside orchards of mountain fruit and nut trees. Half-orcs drive much of the labor and heavy craft—stonecutting from the Kaltera foothills, skilled masonry, sturdy woodwork, and blacksmithing that pairs durability with elven artistry. Elves produce finer goods such as carved furniture, illuminated texts, and woven garments. Exports center on cut stone and crafted arms; imports bring grain, fish, fabrics, and reagents. 

### **Holidays and Traditions**

Traditions in Nurador actively renew the bonds between elves and half-orcs rather than simply marking time. The Word of Nurador, held each spring, gathers the community for public forums where grievances are resolved by mixed panels and paired residents exchange carved tokens buried together in the terraces to root their shared pact. Four times a year on Salvus, when both moons are full, the Observance of Moon and Mountain draws villagers to moonlit rites. Personal stories of growth and endurance are shared aloud, and offerings are left at the blended shrines. These observances, along with smaller seasonal celebrations tied to the founding, keep cooperation a living force.

# Dungeon Master’s Reference

Set in the foothills of the Kaltera Mountains, within the rugged expanse of the Matahal Valley, Nurador is a village known for its order and resilience. Founded by elven scholars and strengthened by half-orc outcasts who found sanctuary here, the settlement has grown into a rare blending of cultures. Its architecture reflects this union: graceful elven arches and carved spires are reinforced with stout stonework and practical designs favored by the half-orcs. Predominantly home to elves and half-orcs, with a scattering of humans and others, Nurador has earned a reputation as a community that thrives on cooperation, balance, and shared strength despite the harsh lands that surround it.

## History

### **The Founding of Nurador**

Nurador was founded in 1187 by a circle of high elf scholars who sought seclusion in the Kaltera foothills to pursue their studies. Numerous orc clans roamed the Matahal Valley, and the new settlement quickly drew their attention. Raids were frequent in the early years, and the elves struggled to defend their community. Over time, half-orc outcasts—children of violence, exile, or broken ties—sought refuge in Nurador. In exchange for sanctuary, they offered their strength and martial skills, becoming protectors of the settlement and finding purpose in service.  The Onn’bane family, early elven scholars, rose to prominence during this time, regarded as the founders and natural leaders of Nurador with their willingness to accept the half-orcs and help them prosper.

### **The Awakening and Concord of Nurador**

The Awakening brought violent upheaval to the foothills of Kaltera and parts of Nurador crumbled, leaving ruins that would later be rebuilt.  Many pushed back on the effort, abandoning the cause and dissent festered. In the wake of this chaos, elves and half-orcs forged a pact in Kesero of 1229 known as the Concord of Nurador, declaring equal voice and shared responsibility in the settlement’s future. 

### **The Lightgarde Ascension and Intrigue**

The Lightgarde family began to rise to prominence, offering a different vision for Nurador and promising greater prosperity for Nurador.  In Paramor of 1229, the Onn’banes were removed from power, and the Lightgarde family was chosen to guide the village into a new era. Lady Phéhldra Lightgarde became ruler with her husband Zelesel at her side. Their reign was short-lived as Zelesel died under suspicious circumstances shortly after 1230\.  Lady Phéhldra has since ruled alone—assisted by her trusted counselors Ranomir Runesworn, Nalu Hodras, and Hektu Naskug. 

### **Unity Through Celebration**

In the years since, Nurador has embraced cultural blending not only in governance and faith but also in celebration. The elves and half-orcs established holidays and festivals that tied back to Nurador’s founding.

## Citizenry

Nurador is home to roughly 750–800 inhabitants, with a nearly even split between elves and half-orcs, and a small number of humans and others. The population respects its blended culture: elves provide artistry, magical learning, and governance, while half-orcs offer strength in defense, construction, and craft.

## Holidays and Traditions

Traditions in Nurador keep the hard-won union of elves and half-orcs alive and balanced, giving every voice equal weight. They are Nurador’s way of saying: we survive because we choose each other, again and again.

### **Word of Nurador**

The Word of Nurador is the village’s central celebration of governance and unity, held each spring. For two days, the 7th and 8th of Kesero, the community gathers to speak the living promises of the Concord aloud.  At dusk on the second day, paired residents—one elf, one half-orc—exchange small carved tokens: delicate runes from the elf, sturdy geometric marks from the half-orc. These tokens are buried together in the terraced gardens, a literal planting of the pact. The nights fill with blended feasts while stories of cooperation are retold. 

### **Observance of Moon and Mountain**

The Observance of Moon and Mountain is held four times each year on Salvus, whenever both moons stand full. On those nights the people climb to the stone platforms above the valley where elves chant and half-orcs provide deep earth-pounding rhythms. Offerings of moon-blooms and mountain stones are left at the blended shrines. The rite binds the soaring insight of the moons to the unyielding endurance of the earth, a symbol that Nurador stands beneath both.

Nurador is ruled by Lady Phéhldra Lightgarde, with the assistance of her three counselors, for a governance that blends elven and half-orc will and acceptance.

## Laws

The Concord of Nurador (1229) continues to shape governance, ensuring that rulings are viewed as balanced between communities. Enforcement falls primarily to the village guard, led by Ranomir Runesworn, though serious disputes are brought before Lady Phéhldra Lightgarde and her counselors.

Here are some key ordinances:

* **Equal Voice Statute** – Every resident, elf or half-orc, has the right to speak in open forum and petition the Lady or her counselors.  
* **Weapons Within the Walls** – Carrying a weapon in town is permitted, but it must be peace-bonded (tied or sheathed) unless in defense of life.  Unbonded weapons in the marketplace or at festivals incur fines.  
* **Blended Property Rights** – Disputes over land or craft are settled by a joint panel of an elf and a half-orc chosen from the community.  
* **Sanctuary Oath** – Outcasts who seek refuge in Nurador cannot be turned away if they swear to uphold its laws and contribute to the community.    
* **Respect of Shrines** – Shrines to Myrdris, Sylunara, Muerg, and Brault are considered neutral ground. Violence or theft at these sites is judged as a crime against the entire village.  
* **Market Fairness Ordinance** – Traders must set prices in accordance with the council’s seasonal rates to prevent exploitation during lean harvests.  
* **Concord Enforcement** – Any attempt to undermine the Concord of Nurador, whether through intimidation, violence, or secret alliance with outside clans, is treated as treason.  Punishment may include exile or even execution.

Enforcement

* **Nurador Guard** – Led by Ranomir Runesworn, the guard patrols the streets, enforces day-to-day ordinances, and investigates crimes.  
* **Council Mediation** – Lesser disputes are resolved by community panels or through counselor mediation (Nalu Hodras often takes lead in half-orc disputes, while Ranomir handles elven matters).  
* **Lady’s Judgment** – Serious crimes—treason, murder, or violations of the Concord—are judged by Lady Phéhldra herself, with her three counselors advising.

## Economy and Trade

Its foothill location in the Matahal Valley makes large-scale farming difficult, but the community has adapted.  Terraced gardens along the slopes provide grains, herbs, and hardy vegetables.  Orchards of mountain fruit and nut trees are also layered around Nurador.

The half-orcs of Nurador supply strength in craft and labor: stonecutting from the Kaltera foothills, skilled masonry, and sturdy woodwork. Blacksmiths and armorers bear both durability and a touch of elven artistry. Elves contribute finely crafted goods—carved furniture, illuminated texts, and woven garments. Small workshops produce trinkets and other reliable crafts.

Trade is conducted mostly with neighboring settlements of the Matahal Valley and passing caravans that cross the foothills. Nurador’s exports include cut stone and crafted arms, while its imports consist of grain, fish, fabrics, and reagents that cannot be sourced locally. 

## Religion

## Religion reflected Nurador’s divided heritage. The elves built shrines to Myrdris, god of magic and knowledge, and Sylunara, goddess of transformation and the moons, weaving ritual into the scholarly life of the settlement. Meanwhile, the half-orcs honored Muerg, primal god of the earth, and Brault, god of might and battle. Over time, elements of these devotions began to merge—elven shrines took on carvings of stone and strength, while half-orc rites were performed beneath moonlight.

# NPC Info

# Lady Phéhldra Lightgarde

*Female High-elf Noble, Lawful Neutral*  
*Gear**.***  Silver circlet, signet ring of House Lightgarde, finely crafted longsword (family heirloom, rarely worn in public), small vial of moonwater (used in private rituals of remembrance).

### **Appearance**

A statuesque elf with a commanding presence, Phéhldra carries herself with quiet authority. Her silver-blonde hair, once worn loose in youth, is now always braided and pinned with polished moonstone clasps. Her eyes are pale green, calm but ever-watchful, and fine lines at their corners mark both grief and sleepless vigilance. She dresses in layered gowns of muted silver and deep green, embroidered with motifs of vines and mountain lilies—the sigil of her house. She always wears a slender silver circlet, more a sign of burden than of pride.

### **Personality**

Reserved, pragmatic, and deliberate. Phéhldra rarely speaks hastily and prefers to listen, weighing counsel before offering judgment. Though she appears aloof to strangers, her composure masks the grief of losing her husband, Zelesel, under suspicious circumstances. Her outlook is shaped by resilience—order and fairness above sentiment—and she has earned a reputation as a ruler who does not flinch in the face of unrest. Yet beneath her measured tone is a woman deeply protective of her people and quietly fearful of hidden threats to her rule.

### **Background**

Phéhldra Lightgarde rose to prominence during the upheaval of the Awakening. In 1229, Phéhldra and her late husband Zelesel were chosen to lead under the newly signed Concord of Nurador. Zelesel’s sudden death in 1230, rumored to be linked to an attempted coup, left Phéhldra as the sole ruler. 

### **Motivations**

Phéhldra’s primary motivation is preserving stability in Nurador. She views the Concord not only as law but as the settlement’s soul, and she has sworn to keep it intact at all costs. Her cautious nature means she often sees threats before opportunities, and her mistrust of ambitious families (especially surviving Onn’bane loyalists) borders on obsession. Despite her stoicism, she deeply values loyalty and rewards those who prove themselves steadfast.

### **Secrets**

Phéhldra keeps her grief locked behind layers of discipline, but she has not abandoned the suspicion that her husband was murdered. She quietly investigates potential traitors in Nurador, using Ranomir’s guard to root out whispers of disloyalty. More dangerously, she is torn between her lawful pragmatism and a growing temptation to use harsher methods of control should unrest return. Though respected by most, her solitude and grief leave her vulnerable to influence—something her enemies may one day exploit.
