# **Overview**

Shademoor is a quiet yet resilient town that thrives in the shadow of its tragic past. With a population of around 1,000, the town has managed to rebuild after the horrors of the Fracturing. The town itself is modest, with cobblestone streets lined by weather-worn homes, many of which house multiple generations under one roof. 

The fields to the east stretch wide and are lush with crops, the most notable being the evergreen-colored Mottleberry. These berries are the town's lifeblood, harvested for food, dye, and the locally prized Mottleberry wine. Large families tend these fields, their communal efforts lending the town a sense of stability and purpose.

To the west, the Shademoor Forest looms dark and foreboding, its twisted trees shrouded in a persistent mist that drifts out into the town. At twilight, this fog thickens, spreading from The Garden—a sprawling cemetery set in the woods and rumored to be haunted.

## **History**

Shademoor was once a thriving town before the Fracturing, benefitting from its fertile lands and bustling trade. However, its fortunes turned when the demonic hordes emerged from the Crungess, transforming the town into a frontline battlefield for the Bathaen Empire. The conflict decimated the population, leaving the town nearly abandoned and strewn with the graves of soldiers and civilians.

After the war shifted to Brimhaven, Shademoor slowly began to recover. The fertile eastern fields provided a foundation for regrowth, with families returning to the area and cultivating crops—most notably the versatile Mottleberry. This berry became the cornerstone of Shademoor’s economy, allowing the town to stabilize at a steady population of about 1,000.

Yet the scars of the past remain. The Garden, a cemetery in the western forest, stands as a grim reminder of the war’s horrors. Over the years, it has become shrouded in an unnatural mist, giving rise to rumors of hauntings and curses. Despite these tales, the people of Shademoor have learned to live with the shadows of their history, creating a unique blend of agricultural vitality and somber reflection.

### **Holidays and Traditions**

Shademoor’s **Mottlebright Festival** is a cherished celebration held on All-bright, the one day of the year when the mists of the Shademoor Forest mysteriously fail to settle over the town. The festival centers around the Mottleberry, the pride of Shademoor, and begins at dawn with a ceremonial gathering in the eastern fields. The town’s eldest farmer leads a blessing of the crops, thanking the land and the light for their bounty.  Throughout the day, the streets are alive with music, dancing, and vibrant stalls offering Mottleberry-inspired goods, including pies, preserves, dyed fabrics, and, of course, the famous Mottleberry wine.  In the afternoon, a friendly competition known as the Berry Dash takes place, where participants race to collect the most berries from a designated field. The winner is crowned Mottlebright Champion and given a ceremonial basket filled with the freshest harvest. The festival concludes with families and visitors gathering for a grand feast in the town square, sharing Mottleberry dishes under the open sky.

## **Government**

Shademoor is run by **Countess Sapphira Legore**, an enigmatic and composed leader who has served as Shademoor’s appointed ruler for the past 15 years. Handpicked by Queen Breya Talward of Basctdelm, Sapphira is known for her striking beauty and poised demeanor, which complement her sharp intellect and commanding presence. She is a skilled diplomat who values order and pragmatism, traits she uses to navigate the complexities of governing a town with a dark history and a mistrustful populace.

Though Sapphira is warm and welcoming to those who visit her, she maintains an air of mystery. Her preference for working late into the evening feeds into whispers among the townsfolk, who speculate about her peculiar habits. Sapphira is a keen observer, rarely missing subtle cues in conversation, and she has a way of making people feel at ease while still maintaining authority. When roleplaying Sapphira, emphasize her eloquent speech and measured tone. She exudes calm confidence, but her piercing gaze and ability to subtly steer conversations reveal a woman who is always in control. Her warmth is genuine, but her commanding nature reminds everyone that she is not to be underestimated.

Sapphira is protective of Shademoor’s reputation and prosperity, actively working to maintain its stability while discouraging outside interference in the town’s affairs. She is wary of those who dig too deeply into the mysteries of the Shademoor Forest and The Garden, deflecting questions with grace but rarely providing clear answers.

## **Economy and Trade**

Shademoor’s economy thrives on two primary resources: the **Mottleberry** and the **Shadewood** lumber. The Mottleberry uses range from food production to dye creation, and the plentiful Mottleberry wine is often available in Halesworth. The Shadewood lumber, harvested from the dense and eerie Shademoor Forest, is a dark, exceptionally hard wood sought for its durability and beauty. Often favored by artisans and furniture makers, Shadewood is often considered a luxury material in the nearby cities.

The town trades these resources to neighboring villages, towns, and cities in exchange for goods they cannot produce locally. Much of this trade occurs along the main road connecting Shademoor to the larger settlements of Halesworth. While Mottleberry products are shipped regularly, the harvesting of Shadewood is more cautious and selective, respecting the forest’s ominous reputation. The combination of these resources has ensured Shademoor’s survival and modest prosperity, even as it remains haunted by its tragic past.

## **Religion**

While many people of Shademoor worship all the different gods of Myrdae, there are some deities that are more often worshipped. 

### **Crael, the Soulbearer**

Crael is revered by the people of Shademoor to ensure the proper rest of those who perished in the town’s tragic past. With Shademoor’s history steeped in death and lingering mysteries, many pray to Crael for peace for the dead and protection from any restless spirits that might disturb the living.

A modest oratory dedicated to Crael stands near a weathered statue of him at the center of town. The statue depicts an elderly man in a hooded robe, holding a staff and accompanied by a raven. Here, townsfolk gather to offer prayers and tokens to honor the dead and seek Crael’s guidance in safeguarding the town from lingering hauntings.

### **Phoe, the Hooded Lady**

Phoe is honored by those who value secrecy and the protection of privacy, as well as those who work or travel under the cover of night. Shademoor’s foggy evenings and the mysteries of the forest make her an appealing deity for townsfolk who find solace and safety in the darkness.

Phoe shares a small oratory with Sylunara in the center of town. This shared sacred space, adorned with deep violet drapery, is illuminated by faint, flickering lanterns that create an ambiance of quiet reflection. Worshippers visit during twilight to offer silent prayers and seek Phoe’s blessing for discretion and safety.

### **Sylunara, the Evening Radiance**

Sylunara is worshiped by those drawn to the cycles of the moons and the transformative nature of their phases. Her guidance is sought for personal growth, self-reflection, and the balance of light and shadow in one’s life. The frequent mists and the beauty of moonlit nights in Shademoor make her a natural choice for worship.

Sylunara’s oratory is shared with Phoe, with offerings of moon-shaped tokens and silver-adorned candles decorating the altars. Her followers often congregate here during the full moons to celebrate her transformative guidance.

### **Vallari, the Dawn Princess**

Farmers and those who work the fields worship Vallari, seeking her blessings to ensure bountiful crops and the natural harmony of life and death in their work. Her connection to life and nature resonates deeply with those who rely on the land for survival.

Vallari’s worshippers do so in small, private shrines scattered across the fields. These shrines are simple yet reverent, often adorned with flowers, small wooden carvings, and tokens of gratitude for a healthy harvest. Gatherings are rare, as her worship is more personal and intimate.

### **Nigrum, the Netherlord**

A small subset of Shademoor’s population reveres Nigrum, drawn by their fascination with the mysteries of The Garden and their belief in the balance he brings to life and undeath. These individuals see Nigrum as a guardian of the boundary between life and death, ensuring that such mysteries remain undisturbed.

Nigrum’s followers gather in secrecy, often within The Garden itself or in hidden clearings in the Shademoor Forest. These gatherings are quiet and solemn, marked by whispered prayers and symbolic gestures to honor the Netherlord and seek his protection for the balance of the natural and supernatural realms.

## **Garden, The**

The Garden is a large cemetery on the southwest side of Shademoor, surrounded by the dense Shademoor Forest.  It is the most significant location in the town, both historically and supernaturally. The Garden sits atop a large hill and holds hundreds of graves, many of which belong to those who perished during the Battle of Shademoor after the Fracturing. The cemetery is encircled by an imposing black iron fence with stone pillars, giving it an aura of both dignity and unease.

On the western side of the Garden stands the Old Cathedral, a grand and well-preserved structure of imposing grey stone with tall arched windows and a prominent bell tower that looms over the cemetery. Though it is no longer used as a place of worship, the cathedral remains intact and exudes a haunting beauty. Its interior was repurposed as a somber hall for remembrance, housing relics and artifacts tied to the Battle of Shademoor.

At the center of the Garden is a massive tree, nearly petrified, with sparse, brittle leaves that refuse to fall. This tree, called the **Heart of the Garden**, is a source of superstition, as some believe it binds the spirits of those buried there.

The Hero’s Tomb on the northern edge of the Garden is a small collection of mausoleums and memorials honoring the greatest defenders of Shademoor who fell during the battle. Their names are etched into stone plaques, though time and neglect have eroded many of them.

A cemetery guardhouse sits at the eastern entrance of the Garden, at the top of the road leading from the town square. Though it appears vacant, the tools inside, such as spades, lanterns, and pruning shears, show signs of nightly use. The Keeper of the Garden, a mysterious figure seen only as a silhouette during the misty nights, is believed to tend to the graves in secret. While many dismiss this as myth, locals find comfort in the belief that the Garden is still watched over.

### **About the Keeper of the Garden**

Now, he remains as its eternal caretaker, tending the graves and ensuring no harm befalls the resting souls. Othwode appears during the misty haze that envelops the town, continuing his quiet vigil. ## **Stock House, The (pub and general store)**

The Stock House is a cornerstone establishment of Shademoor, combining a general store and a pub under one roof. The two-story building is constructed of dark Shadewood and stone, with the ground floor built entirely of stone to provide a cool, stable base for the pub, while the upper floor, made of polished Shadewood, houses the store. Large double doors open the pub area to the street, inviting patrons to sit and enjoy a drink or meal with a view of the town square.

The general store, accessed via a wooden staircase on the side of the building, caters to adventurers and locals alike, selling standard goods such as rope, tools, lanterns, rations, and basic adventuring gear. Trades are also welcome, and the store keeps a small supply of higher-quality equipment for well-paying customers. Below, the pub offers Mottleberry ale and wine alongside two staple dishes: Mottleberry Meat Stuffing, a savory and tangy dish made from local ingredients, and Hollow Root Crisps, a crunchy, spicy snack. The Stock House is run by the shrewd and friendly Kole Utheram, a human man with a knack for business, who personally oversees both the pub and store operations.

## **Town Hall**

The Town Hall of Shademoor is the administrative heart of the town and serves as the central location for governance and public services. Constructed entirely of wood, the structure showcases the rich, dark tones of Shadewood with contrasting accents of pale birch. The main hall occupies the entire central section, extending two stories high and running from the front entrance to the back of the building. To either side of the main hall are stacked rooms, with the upper rooms accessible via staircases in the back corners of the hall. A lofted balcony runs along the second floor on each side, providing a view of the main hall below.

The Town Hall has a guard stationed at the entrance to maintain order. The Countess Sapphira Legore often holds meetings and oversees town affairs from her office, located on the upper floor to the right. The master-of-coin, Trinetta Willowbend, manages finances and trade agreements, while her husband, Furnin Willowbend, oversees tax collection. Jural Swell, the town’s notary, is responsible for maintaining records, witnessing agreements, and drafting legal documents. The building also hosts town meetings, court hearings, and other public gatherings.

## Standing prominently on the town square, the Town Hall is a striking two-story building crafted from richly textured Shadewood, its deep hues accented with lighter tones of birch. The structure has a symmetrical design with carefully crafted wooden details. Wide double doors, polished to a soft sheen, sit at the center of the building, flanked by tall, shuttered windows. A small wooden sign bearing the town crest hangs above the entryway. A single guard stands at attention near the entrance.
