## About Dunduar

Dunduar is a dwarven kingdom carved deep inside the Distancion Mountains in the region known as the Severed Crease. Founded centuries ago as a mining village, today Dunduar is known across Myrdae for its Great Foundry, its rich veins of iron and silver, and the rare magical metal called Dunduar Ore.  King Ky’meag Umberfist currently rules from within the mountain.

### **The People**

Dunduar’s population is mostly dwarven.  Silver-scaled Nelcrath dragonborn comprise about 10% of the citizens and are known for their emphasis on mercy and guardianship. A small but important community of Mipswaddle svirfneblin (less than 10%) lives in Dunduar after being rescued. The remaining population consists of various other races who have come to the mountain for trade, craft, or opportunity.

### **Beliefs**

Dunduar’s culture is shaped by the mountain itself and the hard lessons of survival. The people revere three primary deities: Muerg, who provides the ores and riches of the earth; Goldraen, who grants mastery of craft and making; and Ol’farium, the platinum wyrm whose spirit watches over and protects those of good intent beneath the mountain. Society values endurance, structural integrity, and the slow, deliberate “Pace of Stone.”  Most citizens believe the mountain must be actively protected and maintained rather than simply exploited.

### **Economy and Trade**

Dunduar’s economy centers on mining and metalworking. Its main exports are iron and silver, which it produces in greater quantity than any other settlement in Myrdae thanks to the highly efficient Great Foundry. The kingdom is also famous for Dunduar Ore, a magically potent metal that attracts smiths and mages from across the continent. Amberstone was once a major source of wealth but has become much rarer in recent generations. Gold and platinum are mined in smaller but steady amounts. 

### **Holidays and Traditions**

Dunduar observes several important holidays tied to its history and survival. Morn of Dunduar (Hemist 18th) is a somber day of remembrance for the great collapse. Refounding Vigil (Baegrum 7th–8th) honors the decision to move inside the mountain and build the deep foundations. Ore Discovery Day celebrates the finding of Dunduar Ore and the mountain’s gifts, while the Foundry’s Blessing gives thanks for the Great Foundry and the alliance with the Mipswaddle. Night of Ol’farium’s Watch is a solemn vigil focused on protection and good intent. Recurring traditions include the Pillar Renewal, where citizens help inspect and reinforce the deep foundations, and public Ancestral Ledger Readings that reinforce the importance of history and slow, careful decision-making.

## History

Dunduar was founded on Kesero the 11th, year 49, by clan elders Dundrim Umberfist and Uarmil Cragfury as a mining village in the eastern foothills of the Distancion Mountains (Severed Crease, southeastern span of the range on the Mainland). Rich iron ore and gem pockets drove rapid expansion; mines soon pushed deep beneath the mountain and eventually breached the Underdark, triggering prolonged battles with drow and illithids.

Illithid forces spent years dominating umber hulks to tunnel beneath the village. On Hemist the 18th, year 59 (Morn of Dunduar), the ground collapsed into a 100-foot chasm. Only a dozen survivors remained. The surviving dwarves relocated deeper into the Distancion Mountains and refounded Dunduar inside the mountain itself on Baegrum the 7th, year 59\. They engineered massive interlocking foundational columns and beams rooted deep into the rock. Structural work finished by year 69; mining resumed and the population exceeded 5,000 by the turn of the century.

On Talil the 30th, year 81, miners discovered Dunduar Ore, a metal with innate magical properties. Smiths, jewelers, and mages from across Myrdae came to work it.

Leadership remained tied to the founding clans. Dundrim Umberfist died in year 378; his son Boreth succeeded him as Umberfist elder. Uarmil Cragfury died in year 317; his son Saeviul (disappeared with family in year 271\) had already taken Cragfury leadership. Over the centuries the settlement evolved from clan stronghold into the recognized dwarven kingdom of Dunduar.

### **The Awakening and Geographic Shift**

After the Awakening, massive land changes closed the original northeast entrance into the Distancion Mountains. A new gate was established on the opposite side of the range, opening a road called the New Climb that leads into Orbansia. Travel and trade now route through this newer path.

### **Current Leadership (Year 1211–present)**

Ky’meag Umberfist became king of Dunduar in year 1211 at age 147\. He leans heavily on his mother, Eringar, for counsel following his father’s death. Ky’meag strives to be a level-headed ruler but feels he inherited a raw hand—centuries of bureaucratic precedent and unresolved problems from the transition to kingship now rest on his shoulders. Governance has consolidated under the Umberfist line as a formal kingdom while Cragfury influence remains significant in mining and clan affairs.

## Citizenry

Dunduar’s citizenry is shaped by shared history: the Collapse, the deep foundations, the discovery of Dunduar Ore, and the post-Awakening rerouting of trade through Orbansia. Cragfury mining interests and Umberfist royal authority create natural political weight, while Nelcrath and Mipswaddle minorities add layers of mercy, guardianship, and Underdark insight. Lingering clan fractures (especially Umberfist–Cragfury tensions over sealed depths and ley flows) and the Mipswaddle’s gratitude to Boreth’s line offer ready hooks for intrigue, loyalty tests, or moral dilemmas. Most citizens take pride in Dunduar’s endurance and view the mountain itself as both protector and burden.

Dunduar is a resilient, mountain-forged kingdom whose people reflect centuries of endurance, craft, and quiet alliances. The population is approximately 70% dwarven, 10% Nelcrath (silver dragonborn), less than 10% svirfneblin of the Mipswaddle clan, and roughly 10% other races.

### **Dwarves (70% of population)**

The dwarven majority is split between the two founding clans and later migrants. Cragfury dwarves (Barren type) comprise roughly 50% of Dunduar’s total citizens and remain heavily tied to the deep mines and Dunduar Ore extraction. Umberfist dwarves (Mountain type) and other migrant dwarves make up the remaining \~20%; the Umberfist line holds the throne, with King Ky’meag Umberfist (r. 1211–present) leaning on his mother Eringar for counsel amid inherited bureaucratic pressures. Dwarves here prize disciplined craft, structural mastery, and the hard-won lesson that “stone endures.”

### **Nelcrath (10% of population)**

Silver-scaled dragonborn trace their line to Nelcradia the Bright, the ancient silver dragon who watched over Dunduar from its earliest days. Integrated shortly after the year 59 refounding, they embody the creed that mercy is not weakness and true strength refuses to become cruel. They serve as quiet guardians, healers, and voices of restrained compassion, often viewed by outsiders as idealistic yet respected for their watchfulness and humility. Many Nelcrath still believe Nelcradia walks among mortals in humble guises.

### **Mipswaddle Svirfneblin (\<10% of population)**

This small but distinct community of deep gnomes descends from survivors rescued by Boreth Umberfist (Umberfist elder after year 378\) from a mind flayer invasion that destroyed their Underdark settlement. The Mipswaddle brought stealth, Underdark lore, and a cautious gratitude toward the Umberfist line. They contribute specialized knowledge of deep tunnels and have integrated as a quiet minority, though some older dwarves still regard them as “surface-touched” or tied to the old threats beneath the mountain.

### **Other Races (\~10% of population)**

A modest mix of humans, elves, half-elves, and other surface folk live in Dunduar as traders, artisans, scholars, and occasional adventurers. Many arrived via the New Climb road after the Awakening shifted access. They add diversity to the forges, markets, and halls but hold limited political weight compared to the established clans and Nelcrath.

## Holidays and Traditions

Dunduar’s holidays and traditions are slow, deliberate, and deeply tied to the mountain’s survival. They emphasize remembrance of the Collapse, gratitude for the deep foundations, the gifts of ore and craft, and the watchful protection of good intent. Most celebrations involve the entire community — dwarves, Nelcrath, and Mipswaddle alike — and often include both solemn reflection and practical acts that strengthen the kingdom.

### **Morn of Dunduar (Hemist 18th)**

The most somber day of the year. Citizens gather at the site of the original collapse or around the oldest foundational pillars to remember the day the village fell. Lithoguard lead formal inspections of key supports while the community renews vows to maintain the Stone’s Integrity. Offerings of stone and ore are placed in the deep chasm. Nelcrath often speak on the cost of pride and the strength found in endurance. No loud celebration occurs — the day is marked by quiet labor and remembrance.

### **Refounding Vigil (Baegrum 7th–8th)**

A two-day observance marking the decision to move inside the mountain and begin building the deep foundations. The first day is reflective, with storytelling of the survivors and the engineering of the interlocking pillars. The second day shifts to celebration, with communal meals, toasts to the Ancestors, and ceremonial reinforcement of symbolic pillars. Many families add a small stone or metal token to the oldest supports as a personal act of contribution.

### **Vein’s Gift / Ore Discovery Day (Talil 30th)**

A festival celebrating the discovery of Dunduar Ore and the mountain’s continued bounty. Smiths and jewelers display masterworks, and the Great Foundry holds public demonstrations. Offerings are made to both Muerg and Goldraen. Amberstone (when found) is sometimes featured in special displays. The day includes crafting contests and the sharing of new techniques, especially those developed with Mipswaddle ingenuity.

### **Foundry’s Blessing (Date varies, often late in the year)**

An annual thanks for the Great Foundry and the alliance with the Mipswaddle svirfneblin that made it possible. Boreth Umberfist’s rescue of the deep gnomes is retold, followed by joint feasts between dwarves and Mipswaddle. Tinkering exhibitions and demonstrations of the Foundry’s efficiency are highlights. Many view this as a day when the community’s different peoples stand together as one mountain.

## Government

Dunduar is ruled by King Ky’meag Umberfist (r. 1211–present), who ascended the Umberfist throne at age 147 following his father’s death. He governs within a deeply traditional framework shaped by the Collapse, the refounding, and the enduring lesson that “stone remembers.” Ky’meag strives for level-headed decisions but carries the weight of centuries of bureaucratic precedent and unresolved ancestral obligations; he frequently turns to his mother, Eringar, for counsel.

The following components weigh into the governing of Dunduar.

### **I. The Source of Sovereign Power**

Sovereign authority flows from the bloodline of the Umberfist line, sanctified by the mandate of the Ancestors. The king’s legitimacy rests on direct descent from Dundrim Umberfist and on the approval of the Silent Council. Wealth from Dunduar Ore strengthens the realm but does not confer rule; only lineage confirmed by ancestral law grants the right to sit the throne.

### **II. The High Council (The Living)**

The king chairs the High Council. Permanent seats belong to the chieftain of the Cragfury clan (largest single bloc in the kingdom), senior representatives of the Umberfist royal line, the Grand Guildmaster of the Forge (overseeing Dunduar Ore working), and a captain of the Deepguard (responsible for Underdark-facing defenses). A Nelcrath voice may be invited on matters of mercy and justice. The Mipswaddle svirfneblin hold no formal seat but supply critical Underdark intelligence when summoned. All major decisions require the king’s seal and alignment with ancestral precedent.

### **III. The Silent Council (The Dead)**

Ancestral law and the recorded memory of the Collapse (Morn of Dunduar, year 59\) and the refounding carry greater weight than any living decree. Rulings that contradict the “stone’s remembrance”—founding oaths, the deep foundational pillars, or lessons of endurance—can be declared void by the Silent Council through ritual consultation, rune-reading, or tomb observance. This layer of oversight is the primary source of the slow, deliberate bureaucracy that burdens Ky’meag; many modern proposals die in the Silent Council before reaching the High Council floor.

### **IV. Territorial Administration**

Distant mining colonies and surface trade outposts (especially those reached via the New Climb after the Awakening) report through trusted guild caravans and Deepguard couriers. Taxes and ore shipments travel in heavily escorted convoys. Reports follow strict ancestral formats and are cross-checked against historical ledgers; deviations are investigated as potential threats to the mountain’s stability. The system prioritizes control and memory over speed.

### **V. Tensions and Factions**

Two primary factions shape daily governance. Progressive Guilds (primarily forge and younger mining interests) push for faster lawmaking and more aggressive exploitation of Dunduar Ore and deeper ley flows. Traditionalist Clans (led by Cragfury hardliners and elder Umberfist voices) insist that all decisions must pass the Silent Council and move at the pace of stone—decades if necessary. King Ky’meag sits between these poles, advised by Eringar and constrained by ancestral law. The Mipswaddle and Nelcrath minorities exert quiet influence: the former through Underdark knowledge, the latter through moral weight on mercy and justice. Lingering clan fractures, especially around sealed chambers and past Cragfury disappearances, add further friction beneath the surface.

## Laws

Dwarven justice in Dunduar moves at the Pace of Stone: deliberately slow, prioritizing the stability of the community and the mountain over individual speed or sentiment. The system emphasizes restitution—repaying harm through labor, ore, or service that strengthens the whole—rather than rehabilitation or swift punishment. All proceedings are bound by ancestral law; the Silent Council can nullify any ruling that contradicts the lessons of the Collapse or the deep foundations.

### **Magistrates (Civil Disputes)**

Civil matters—property lines, trade contracts, water rights, and clan-ledger disagreements—are settled by Magistrates. They rule strictly on historical precedent, clan records, physical measurements, and the stability of the mountain’s foundational pillars. Emotional appeals are disallowed. A verdict typically requires the losing party to make direct restitution—coin, labor on the deep foundations, or ore deliveries that benefit the community. 

### **Tribunal of the Pillars (Major Criminal Offenses)**

### Treason, murder, sabotage of the foundries or foundational pillars, and severe Underdark threats are tried before the Tribunal of the Pillars.  Three judges sit in judgment inside vast, echoing vaults built into the oldest supporting stone:

1. ### One voice for the Sovereign (representing King Ky’meag Umberfist).

2. ### One voice for the High Guilds (forge and mining interests).

3. ### One voice for the Ancestors (a high-ranking cleric or Silent Council delegate who ensures the ruling aligns with ancestral law).

Public galleries allow the community to witness and remember. Verdicts focus on restitution that repairs the harm done to the mountain and the clans—often years of labor sealing breaches, reinforcing pillars, or mining reparations. 

### **The Right of Appeal – The Long Wait**

A convicted dwarf may appeal directly to King Ky’meag Umberfist. Because of the Pace of Stone and the requirement that every appeal pass the Silent Council’s scrutiny, a case may not reach the royal docket for 5 to 10 years. The prisoner remains confined in the Deep Cells beneath the oldest foundations for the duration.

Ky’meag personally reviews only those appeals that survive ancestral review; the backlog adds to his already heavy burden of inherited obligations. Successful appeals are rare and usually result in adjusted restitution rather than full exoneration. Mipswaddle and Nelcrath appellants face the same timeline, though Nelcrath advocates sometimes petition the Ancestors’ seat for swifter mercy reviews on humanitarian grounds.

### **Guard Enforcement**

Dunduar does not maintain traditional surface armies. Its defense and order forces are built for the mountain: tight-formation tunnel fighters, structural engineers, and specialists who operate in absolute darkness. Every guard unit exists to prevent another Morn of Dunduar and to protect the deep foundations that keep the kingdom standing.

* **The Internal Watch**:  The Internal Watch serve as both building inspectors and enforcers of public order. Their core mandate is “the King’s Peace and the Stone’s Integrity.” In a city carved inside living rock, a single compromised pillar or unstable wall can kill thousands; the Internal Watch therefore hold absolute authority to commandeer property, close streets, or evacuate districts the moment they detect structural danger.  
  They patrol the cramped cavern streets and main halls, manage crowd control during festivals or emergencies, and conduct regular inspections of the oldest foundational pillars. While they answer to the High Council, their structural authority can override even clan or guild objections when the mountain itself is at risk. Many Internal Watch are drawn from Cragfury mining families and Umberfist traditionalists; they view their work as sacred guardianship of the lessons learned in year 59\.  
* **Deep Wardens – The Perimeter Guard**:  Stationed at the lowest vertical boundaries of the kingdom, the Deep Wardens are grim, seasoned veterans who face outward toward the Underdark. They enforce martial law in the border zones, control all movement through abandoned shafts and old breach tunnels, and maintain quarantine protocols for any strange deep-earth blights, curses, or unknown entities.  
  The Deep Wardens remember the original Underdark incursions that led to the Collapse. They work closely with Mipswaddle svirfneblin when Underdark knowledge is required, though they remain wary of anything that originates below the foundational pillars. Their authority is near-absolute in the deep perimeter; even the king’s writ can be delayed if a Warden declares a quarantine or structural lockdown.

## Economy and Trade

Dunduar’s economy is built on the mountain’s gifts and one fateful alliance. The kingdom’s primary exports remain iron and silver, which it supplies in greater volume than any other mining power in Myrdae. This dominance stems from the most productive Foundry on the continent, developed after King Boreth Umberfist rescued the Mipswaddle svirfneblin from a mind flayer invasion. The deep gnomes’ tinkering expertise transformed Dunduar’s forges into marvels of efficiency.

### **Primary Exports and Resources**

* **Iron and Silver**: The backbone of Dunduar’s wealth and its greatest contribution to Myrdae. The Great Foundry processes these metals at unmatched speed and scale.  
* **Dunduar Ore:** Discovered in year 81, this magically potent metal remains the kingdom’s most prestigious export. Its high value continues to draw master smiths, jewelers, and mages from across the continent.  
* **Amberstone**: A rare and valuable resinous gem that fueled early prosperity after the founding. Once abundant in the initial mining years, Amberstone deposits have grown scarce, though occasional finds still command high prices.  
* **Gold and Platinum**: Secondary but steady yields that supplement the main exports.

### **The Great Foundry**

The single most important development in Dunduar’s economic history occurred after Boreth Umberfist aided the Mipswaddle svirfneblin. In gratitude and partnership, the deep gnomes brought their exceptional tinkering skills to the mountain. Working alongside Cragfury miners and Umberfist engineers, they designed and built the Great Foundry — the most efficient and productive facility of its kind in Myrdae.

The Foundry’s innovations allow Dunduar to smelt and refine iron and silver faster and in greater quantities than any rival operation. This advantage has made Dunduar the primary supplier of these critical metals across the continent, generating steady wealth that funds both the deep foundations and the royal treasury.

### **Trade Routes and Partners**

Since the Awakening sealed the old northeast gate, all major trade moves through the New Climb road into Orbansia. Guild caravans and heavily escorted ore trains carry iron, silver, and finished goods outward while returning with foreign coin, tools, and the occasional foreign artisan seeking Dunduar Ore.

The magical properties of Dunduar Ore ensure a constant stream of high-value visitors and commissions. Foreign smiths and mages often establish temporary workshops inside the mountain, paying premium rates for access to the ore and the Foundry’s capabilities.

### **Economic Dynamics**

Cragfury dwarves dominate the mining sector and hold significant influence over resource allocation. The Umberfist royal line, through King Ky’meag, oversees the Foundry’s output and the distribution of wealth. Mipswaddle svirfneblin remain essential to the Foundry’s continued edge, though their small numbers limit their formal political power. 

## Religion

Dunduar’s spiritual life centers on three deities whose domains directly support the kingdom’s survival inside the mountain. While other gods are acknowledged, the people of Dunduar look first to Muerg, Goldraen, and Ol’farium for guidance in mining, crafting, and protection.

### **Muerg – The Earth Father**

Muerg is revered as the provider of ores and riches. He is seen as the ancient force that shaped the bones of the world and continues to bless the deep veins beneath Dunduar. Miners and Cragfury clansfolk offer prayers and small offerings before descending into new shafts. Muerg’s favor is believed to have guided the original discovery of both iron and the magical Dunduar Ore. Shrines to him are simple stone niches carved near active mine faces and the deepest foundational pillars.

### **Goldraen – Lord of Craft**

Goldraen embodies the mastery of crafting that turns raw stone and metal into tools, weapons, and wonders. He is especially venerated by the Great Foundry and all who work Dunduar Ore. The Mipswaddle svirfneblin who helped design the Foundry are often viewed as having been guided by Goldraen’s hand. Rituals honoring him focus on precision, endurance, and the transformation of the mountain’s gifts into strength for the community. Many master smiths and engineers maintain small altars within their workshops.

### **Ol’farium – The Platinum Wyrm**

Ol’farium is honored as the great platinum wyrm whose spirit sees all beneath the mountain and protects those of good intent. His worship is closely tied to the Nelcrath dragonborn and to the Deep Wardens who guard the lowest boundaries. Ol’farium represents watchful guardianship, moral clarity, and the defense of the innocent against the darkness below. His influence appears most strongly in the Tribunal of the Pillars, where the Ancestors’ seat often invokes his name when weighing mercy against justice. Many Lithoguard and Deep Wardens carry small platinum tokens bearing his mark.

### **Worship and Integration**

The three deities are frequently honored together in ceremonies that link the earth’s gifts (Muerg), the skill to shape them (Goldraen), and the will to protect what is made (Ol’farium). Major rituals often take place near the oldest foundational pillars or within the Great Foundry itself. Clerics and devout layfolk emphasize that the mountain provides, craft preserves, and righteous vigilance endures. This triad reinforces Dunduar’s core values of community stability, ancestral memory, and measured strength.
