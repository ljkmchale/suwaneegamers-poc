# **Overview**

Nunglthil is an Underdark trading enclave on the black shores of Rothenloch that has endured for more than a century by maintaining a strictly enforced neutrality. It is the single most reliable crossing point between surface merchants and Underdark cultures, a place where trade, information, and influence flow as freely as coin — but only because its five enclaves uphold an unspoken pact: violence threatens everyone’s livelihood. Beneath this uneasy peace lies a constant hum of politics, secrets, and mutual suspicion. Each enclave manipulates trade, territory, and reputation to secure subtle advantages while never quite risking open conflict.

Nunglthil is a balanced ecosystem, not a lawful sanctuary.  Every faction here has goals, grudges, and vulnerabilities, and the trading post thrives because no one power can afford to dominate the others. The surface enclave Kar’Solar is quietly influenced by thieves’ guilds; Gral’thune stewards the physical stability of the settlement; Selraeth’s drow maintain intricate social webs; Durrak’s duergar operate under strict hierarchies of labor, security, and profit; and Glimmerdel’s svirfneblin remain the quietest but most observant power.  This balance introduces intrigue, shifting alliances, and morally gray negotiations without breaking the essential premise: *Nunglthil survives because everyone needs it.*

## 

## Nunglthil is a rare sanctuary deep in the Underdark — a crescent of wooden docks and lantern-lit caverns along the still waters of Rothenloch, where surface travelers and Underdark peoples can trade without fear of being attacked. Though each part of the settlement belongs to one of five distinct enclaves, all who enter agree to the same rule: keep the peace, or be cast out. The result is a bustling, strange, and vibrant crossroads of cultures, where merchants, scouts, cooks, scholars, hunters, and artisans mingle under the glow of bioluminescent fungus and flickering lanterns.

## While the settlement can be safe enough for travelers, Nunglthil is far from simple. Every enclave keeps to its traditions — the Selraeth drow maintain elegant but watchful halls, the duergar run industrious forges, the svirfneblin trade delicate wonders, and the ulbin and dwarves of Gral’thune tend the sturdy structures that keep the whole place from collapsing. Visitors are welcomed so long as they show respect, mind local customs, and honor the no-violence pact. For those willing to navigate its blend of shadows and diplomacy, Nunglthil offers rare goods, unexpected allies, and secrets whispered from both the deepest tunnels and the world above.

## **History**

Nunglthil began not as a market, but as a refuge. During the height of the Demon Wars, when incursions from the deep places of the Underdark shattered settlements and turned long-held tunnels into killing grounds, disparate peoples fled toward the shores of Rothenloch. Ulbin, duergar, svirfneblin, and drow found themselves pressed into the same cavern with no safe route forward or back. From necessity rather than trust, they forged what became known as the Refuge Pact—an agreement to suspend rivalries, share defenses, and hold the cavern together against the demonic threat.

As the wars deepened, the cavern at Rothenloch proved uniquely defensible. Its sheer cliffs, controllable dock access, and the river that carried supplies deeper into the Underdark allowed Nunglthil to evolve into a joint stronghold. Fortifications were carved into the stone, watch posts established along the lake and tunnels, and mixed patrols coordinated resistance against demon incursions. When the demonic advance was finally broken, the fort was no longer needed—but the bonds forged in survival endured. In the years that followed, Nunglthil shed its martial purpose and transformed into a neutral trading hub, with surface traders joining.  They preserved the spirit of the Refuge Pact as a vow of peace.

## **Citizenry**

Nunglthil is home to a rare and uneasy mix of peoples drawn from both the Underdark and the surface world. Its population is divided among five pact-bound enclaves—Gral’thune, Selraeth, Kar’Solar, Durrak, and Glimmerdel—each maintaining its own customs, internal authority, and traditions. Ulbin, dwarves, drow, duergar, svirfneblin, and surface folk live in close proximity, bound not by shared culture but by mutual reliance. Though tensions and old prejudices persist, open violence is rare, as the survival of the settlement depends on restraint and cooperation.

The people of Nunglthil tend to be pragmatic, guarded, and keenly aware of reputation. **Favor**—one’s standing within an enclave or with respected traders—matters more than wealth alone and shapes social interactions, trade opportunities, and access to information. Long-time residents are accustomed to seeing strangers and outsiders pass through, and while initial suspicion is common, those who prove reliable often find acceptance. Citizens value stability above all else; having survived war, demonic incursions, and the collapse of other Underdark settlements, the people of Nunglthil understand that peace here is not idealistic—it is necessary.

### **Gral’thune – “The Strongback”**

Made up almost exclusively of ulbin and dwarves, Gral’thune is the backbone of Nunglthil. They hold the pact of peace most sacred, and violations are handled with firm, unyielding justice. They are slow to anger, but once angered, equally slow to forgive.  The enclaves usually say, “*If Gral’thune ever fell, Nunglthil would collapse within a week.*”

### **Selraeth – “The Silken Dominion”**

### Selraeth is a drow enclave that balances poise with tension. Unlike the more ruthless cities of their people, Selraeth was founded by drow houses who believed that controlled diplomacy and trade would strengthen their influence far more than mindless violence.  Though less openly treacherous than their kin, Selraeth is still a place of shifting alliances and poised daggers, where their presence ensures that Nungylthil is always in an undercurrent of political tension.

### **Kar’Solar – “The Sunbearers”**

Widely viewed as a den of thieves, Kar’Solar is the enclave formed by surface dwellers who descended into Nunglthil generations ago to establish permanent trade routes into the Underdark.  Despite the reputation, Kar’Solar has strict internal codes: no murder within the enclave, no theft from Nunglthil's neutral zones, and no endangering trade. Breaking these rules results in swift, quiet retribution.  Over the decades, it has become a tangled collection of:  thieves’ guild offshoots, smugglers, itinerant merchants, fortune-seekers and guildless wanderers.

### **Durrak – “The Ironbound”**

Duergar established Durrak to ensure they would have controlled access to surface goods while avoiding exploitation by other races.  Duergar merchants are famously humorless but unfailingly direct. They value the trading pact because it extracts wealth from their neighbors, not because they seek harmony. However, despite their pragmatism, they honor bargains — and betrayal is punished without hesitation.

### **Glimmerdel – “The Glow Borough”**

Glimmerdel is probably the most approachable enclave, made up of svirfneblin that bring an uncommon sense of cheer and optimism to Nungylthil.  Though often underestimated, Glimmerdel has a tight communal network and fiercely guards Nungylthil’s neutrality. They are the first to sense danger and the fastest to coordinate warnings.

## **Traditions**

### **Tokens of Safe Return**

Before undertaking dangerous journeys, residents and travelers of Nunglthil often leave behind a personal token—such as a trinket, charm, or written note—with a trusted innkeeper, enclave representative, or at the Dais of Devotion. If the bearer does return, the token is reclaimed and often worn or displayed as a sign of survival. If they do not, the token is offered at the Dais or passed to kin, ensuring the traveler is remembered even if they are lost to the depths.

### **The Open Feast**

On the first day of every harmon, the docks and harbor platforms of Nunglthil are given over to the Open Feast, a communal gathering where food and drink are shared openly. Long tables are set along the water’s edge, braziers are lit, and the people exchange meals, stories, and tales. Old rivalries are expected to rest for the evening. The people believe that survival is easier when stories, warnings, and successes are shared.

## **Government**

Nunglthil is governed by the Pact Council, a cooperative body formed from the five enclaves that comprise the settlement. Each enclave appoints a single representative—known informally as a **Pact Voice**—to speak and act on its behalf. The Council exists solely to oversee matters that affect the settlement as a whole, including the enforcement of neutrality, regulation of shared trade spaces, control of harbor access, and the defense of Nunglthil against external threats. Internal laws, customs, and discipline remain the exclusive domain of each enclave, preserving their autonomy while binding them to the common pact.

Decisions of the Pact Council are reached through consensus whenever possible. Minor matters require agreement from a majority of the enclaves, while major actions—such as altering the terms of neutrality, expelling an enclave, or mobilizing settlement-wide defenses—require unanimous consent. Enforcement is delegated rather than centralized:

* Durrak enforces contracts and penalties,   
* Glimmerdel coordinates warnings and route surveillance,   
* Gral’thune oversees physical defenses and peacekeeping,   
* Kar’Solar maintains surface relations and logistics, and  
* Selraeth manages intelligence and mediation.

Alongside formal governance, Favor functions as a powerful informal authority; reputation and recognized service often determine access, influence, and trust more effectively than written decree. This balance of shared rule and mutual restraint has allowed Nunglthil to endure where more rigid powers have failed.

## **Laws**

Law in Nunglthil is founded on the Pact of Neutrality, a simple but absolute principle: violence and actions that threaten trade or stability are forbidden in shared spaces. Each enclave enforces its own internal laws, but crimes committed in neutral zones—docks, markets, the Dais of Devotion, and major thoroughfares—fall under the authority of the Pact Council. There is no unified legal code; instead, judgments are guided by precedent, enclave customs, and the practical need to preserve order and commerce.

Punishment in Nunglthil favors restitution and consequence over execution. Common penalties include fines paid in coin or labor, seizure of goods, temporary restriction from trade, or the loss of Favor. Severe or repeated offenses may result in public stripping of an enclave’s favor badge, enforced exile from Nunglthil, or, in rare cases, permanent banishment under escort. Violence within sacred or neutral spaces—especially the Dais of Devotion—is considered one of the gravest crimes and may provoke a unified enclave response. Justice in Nunglthil is swift, pragmatic, and rarely merciful, driven by the understanding that survival in the Underdark depends on order far more than forgiveness. 

### **Punishment**

Punishment for crimes in Nunglthil may include one or more of the following, determined by severity, location, intent, prior offenses, and enclave involvement:

* **Loss of Favor** (temporary or permanent, enclave-specific or settlement-wide)  
* **Restitution** (coin, goods, services, or labor paid to an injured party or enclave)  
* **Trade Sanctions** (denial of access to markets, docks, or services)  
* **Edict of Restriction** (forbidding certain actions, locations, or dealings)  
* **Forced Labor** (usually assigned by Gral’thune or Durrak)  
* **Imprisonment** (short-term holding only; long-term incarceration is rare)  
* **Exile from Nunglthil** (temporary or permanent, escorted)  
* **Public Stripping of Favor Badg**e   
* **Execution** (exceedingly rare; reserved for existential threats)

Imprisonment is generally viewed as wasteful in the Underdark and is avoided unless no other solution preserves order.

### **Crimes Against an Enclave**

Crimes committed within an enclave’s space or against its people fall primarily under that enclave’s authority. Punishments vary widely by culture.

### **Gral’thune – The Strongback**

* Emphasizes restitution, labor, and public accountability  
* Violence against the enclave often results in forced labor, loss of Favor, or exile  
* Repeat offenders are barred permanently from Gral’thune trade

### **Selraeth – The Silken Ward**

* Punishments are severe, quiet, and precise  
* Offenses may result in disappearance from trade, poison-oaths, or long exile  
* Public punishment is rare; consequences are felt through isolation and fear  
* Punishment by death is more frequent with Selraeth

### **Kar’Solar – The Surface Quarter**

* Enforces strict internal codes  
* Punishments include financial ruin, expulsion from guild protection, or quiet elimination  
* Crimes that threaten trade or neutrality are punished harshly

### **Durrak – The Ironbound**

* Law is contractual and rigid  
* Violations result in seizure of assets, debt-bond labor, or permanent trade bans  
* Betrayal of contract is considered one of the gravest offenses

### **Glimmerdel – The Glow Borough**

* Punishments focus on social exclusion, loss of communal trust, and Favor removal  
* Repeat or severe offenders are quietly expelled  
* Violence within Glimmerdel is extremely rare and met with unified response

### **Crimes Against Nunglthil – Pact Violations**

Crimes that threaten neutrality, shared spaces, or survival are considered crimes against Nunglthil itself and are judged by the Pact Council.

### **Grave Offenses**

* Murder in neutral zones  
* Sabotage of docks or defenses  
* Inciting enclave war  
* Demon worship or summoning  
* Poisoning shared water or food supplies

Typical Punishments:  Permanent exile, total loss of Favor, seizure of assets, or execution if Nunglthil is endangered.

### **Major Offenses**

* Theft in shared markets  
* Espionage against an enclave  
* Repeated violence or threats  
* Smuggling that destabilizes trade

Typical Punishments:  Restitution, trade bans, forced labor, stripping of Favor, temporary exile.

### **Minor Offenses**

* Disorderly conduct  
* Weapon brandishing  
* Fraud or deception  
* Violation of edicts

Typical Punishments:  Fines, restitution, edicts, temporary loss of access or Favor.

## **Economy and Trade**

Nunglthil exists for one purpose above all others: trade without bloodshed. Its position along the brackish waters of Rothenloch and the river that flows deeper into the Underdark makes it a rare and indispensable waypoint between subterranean realms and the surface world. Goods, information, and influence pass through its docks as readily as coin, and its continued survival depends on the strict maintenance of neutrality. Trade here is governed as much by reputation—known locally as Favor—as by currency, with long-standing relationships often outweighing raw wealth.

Rather than operating as a unified market, Nunglthil’s economy is shaped by its five pact-bound enclaves, each contributing distinct goods and services while guarding their autonomy. 

* Gral’thune forms the settlement’s industrial backbone, providing metalworking, stonecraft, raw materials, escort services, and oversight of Nunglthil’s defenses.   
* Selraeth trades in rare fungi, fine silks, poisons, magical components, and high-value secrets, weaving political influence as deftly as fabric.   
* Kar’Solar handles the gray-market flow of relics, salvaged artifacts, illicit enchantments, and smuggled surface goods, operating under strict internal codes that protect trade stability.   
* Durrak specializes in masterwork weapons, armor, and precision trade contracts, valuing profit and enforceable agreements above all else.   
* Glimmerdel excels in gemcraft, fungal alchemy, delicate charms, trinkets, and navigation routes, and is often the first to detect threats to trade or neutrality.

Together, these enclaves form a tightly interdependent economic system where no single group can dominate without endangering the whole. Occasional feuds and rivalries flare, but open disruption of trade is swiftly punished, for all who dwell in Nunglthil understand the same truth: *if commerce fails here, survival soon follows*.

## **Religion**

Religion in Nunglthil is practiced quietly, personally, and without dominance. As a settlement founded on survival, neutrality, and cooperation among disparate peoples, Nunglthil does not elevate any single deity, pantheon, or creed above another. Instead, it recognizes the importance of faith as a stabilizing force and permits worship so long as it does not threaten the Pact of Neutrality or the safety of the settlement. Open proselytizing, coercive rites, or divine mandates that call for violence are not tolerated.

At the heart of religious life stands the Dais of Devotion, a large communal chamber open to all faiths. Its circular altar—broken into four marble quarters—bears carvings of symbols from both Old Gods and New Order deities, as well as lesser-known or regional powers. Candles, flammable fungi, and small trinket offerings are constantly renewed by worshippers. No clergy permanently preside over the Dais; instead, individuals come to pray, meditate, or perform simple rites in their own manner. On rare occasions, itinerant priests or mystics may lead brief observances, but none are permitted to claim authority over the space.

Faith in Nunglthil tends toward practical devotion rather than elaborate ritual. Many residents honor deities of protection, endurance, trade, craftsmanship, guidance, or fate—gods whose domains align with the realities of Underdark life. Some offer prayers before journeys or negotiations; others seek solace after loss or failure. It is also common for travelers to light a candle at the Dais upon arrival, acknowledging both the dangers they have survived and those yet to come. In Nunglthil, belief is respected, but it is action—keeping the Pact, honoring Favor, and protecting the fragile peace—that is considered the truest expression of devotion.

# **Layout**

Nunglthil is carved directly into the cliffside bordering Rothenloch and arranged in four distinct layers. Stone stairwells, and switchback ramps connect the layers, allowing movement without exposing the settlement to uncontrolled access.

The **Main Layer** forms the heart of Nunglthil’s activity. Here, wide docks line both the lake’s edge and the interior cavern where Rothenloch’s waters flow inward and feed the river beyond. Boats and cargo platforms crowd the harbor, while shops, trade halls, and small enclave offices are carved into the surrounding rock. This layer is loud, busy, and perpetually lit by fungus braziers and lanterns. Several shops facing the lake or cavern include internal staircases that rise into private upper floors, blending commerce and residence within the same carved spaces.

The **Second Layer** serves primarily as a residential tier and overlooks the harbor cavern below. Balconies, railings, and carved viewing platforms allow residents to observe dock activity from above. Most long-term inhabitants of Nunglthil live here, along with the second-floor guest rooms of the Darkbottom Draught inn and tavern. The layer is quieter and more orderly than the docks below, with narrow walkways branching into communal housing, private quarters, and upper shop levels connected to the main trade floor.

The **Third Layer** is markedly smaller and more controlled. It contains higher-status residences, secure chambers, and the Pact Council Chamber, where representatives of the five enclaves meet to govern matters affecting Nunglthil as a whole. Windows and narrow overlooks peer out across Rothenloch, offering both prestige and discretion. Access to this layer is restricted and often monitored, reinforcing its political and symbolic importance.

Above all rests the **Fourth Layer**, consisting of a single fortified lookout chamber overlooking Rothenloch. This post is used to monitor traffic on the lake, watch for threats approaching from the water, and signal warnings to the guards.

## **Armstack**

Armstack is a well-lit arms and armor shop in Nunglthil, notable for its brightness and for the sheer density of weaponry on display. The shop is operated by **Reddig Oddbrow**, a talkative duergar from the city of Dhaltarune who thrives on conversation as much as commerce. Reddig maintains a close working relationship with the Doomhorn smiths of Firmforge, and the majority of his stock consists of their weapons and armor. Supplementing this are pieces acquired through trade—items sold by travelers, guards, and adventurers passing through Nunglthil.

To showcase his wares, Reddig has lined the shop’s walls with polished ivory marble, making Armstack one of the brightest interiors in the settlement when illuminated by the numerous sconces placed throughout. Armor stands and weapon racks are arranged densely but intentionally, mixing functional gear with more decorative pieces to catch the eye. Reddig encourages browsing, often launching into stories about where a piece came from or how it performed in the field. While his demeanor is friendly and open, his prices are firm, and he expects customers to respect the craftsmanship behind every item.

## **Coppereyes Gemcraft**

Coppereyes Gemcraft is the premier gemcutting and jewel-trading establishment in Nunglthil, operated by **Scalimank Coppereyes** under the watchful authority of his aunt, **Antadda Coppereyes** — the leader of Glimmerdel. The shop occupies a prominent position just a few doors to the right of the trading post area in Nunglthil’s main cavern, marked by soft blue fungus-lanterns that illuminate intricately carved stone signage. Inside, the store is a blend of meticulous svirfneblin design and cautious Underdark security: reinforced stone counters, illusion-warded display cases, and glow-crystal sconces that highlight each gemstone with magical clarity.

Scalimank runs the day-to-day operations with precision bordering on obsession. He offers masterfully cut gems, enchanted stones, and appraisal services, and deals in rare materials sourced from both surface and deep Underdark mines. His status — and the backing of Antadda — grants the shop considerable political weight. Customers seeking unique stones or high-value exchanges often need to present adequate “favor” or reputable references. The shop is also a quiet node of influence within Glimmerdel; Antadda uses it to monitor trade shifts across all enclaves and to subtly assert her authority without leaving her own ward.

## **Dais of Devotion (alter house)**

The Dais of Devotion is Nunglthil’s central place of worship—an enclave-neutral chamber where all faiths, surface or Underdark, may honor their gods without judgment or interference. The room is large, and carved entirely from natural stone, softened by cushions, woven mats, and clusters of braziers placed against the walls. Its centerpiece is a wide, elevated dais supporting a massive circular altar of white marble streaked with gold veining. The altar itself is divided into four equal quarters, leaving cross-shaped passages so worshipers can walk freely into the open middle. Both the inner and outer walls of the altar are intricately carved with symbols representing dozens of deities from Myrdae—some ancient and nearly forgotten, others commonly worshiped today.

The 4-foot-wide altar ring is covered with burning candles, flammable glow-fungi, small offerings, handwritten prayers, and trinkets left in reverence. The chamber’s illumination relies entirely on candlelight, creating a warm, flickering, golden glow that reflects off the marble and the polished stone floor. No enclave claims authority over the Dais, and violent acts committed here carry severe social consequences. Many come seeking comfort, guidance, or anonymity. The room is kept clean and orderly by rotating volunteers from all the enclaves, reinforcing its status as neutral and sacred ground.

## **Seafarer’s Supplies**

Seafarer’s Supplies is a practical, well-stocked outfitter located at the far end of the docks on the left side of Nunglthil’s main harbor, overlooking the dark waters of Rothenloch. The shop caters to anyone who makes their living on the lake or the river beyond it, providing gear for navigation, fishing, maintenance, and survival on brackish Underdark waters. 

The shop is owned and operated by **Ronsen “Rigger” Drace**, a surface-born human from the settlement of Mulshear and a long-time member of Kar’Solar. Ronsen is experienced, pragmatic, and deeply familiar with the dangers of Rothenloch. His wife, Phoebe, a half-elf, assists with sales, inventory, and customer relations, often acting as the more personable face of the business. Together they maintain strong ties with dockworkers, fishers, and caravan crews.

## **Seat of Salraeth**

The Seat of Salraeth serves as the administrative and diplomatic office of the Selraeth enclave within Nunglthil. Unlike the enclave’s more public-facing establishments, this location is deliberately understated—designed to blend into the surrounding stone and avoid drawing attention. From here, Selraeth manages trade agreements, negotiates discreet arrangements, and monitors political currents both within Nunglthil and beyond.

The interior is orderly and precise. Records of trade, contracts, and favors are maintained in coded ledgers and enchanted documents, many of which are kept in locked cabinets or sealed alcoves. Meetings held here are rarely spontaneous; most visitors arrive by appointment or invitation. While the office does not appear overtly guarded, Selraeth agents are always nearby, and the space is warded against eavesdropping. The Silken Ledger is where Selraeth’s influence is quietly measured, adjusted, and extended—without blades ever needing to be drawn.

## **Silken Secrets**

Silken Secrets is one of the most alluring and quietly dangerous establishments in Nunglthil, operated by the Selraeth enclave. The shop is known for its exquisite drow craftsmanship—fabrics that shimmer like moonlit silk, exotic clothing from the deep Underdark, and tailored pieces designed for nobles, performers, diplomats, and those who simply crave luxury. The storefront is tastefully understated, but inside the atmosphere transforms into one of dim violet light, warm shadows, and sensuous elegance. Violet and silver drapery hangs from carved niches, and the entire floor is covered in a plush, tightly woven cloth that muffles footsteps and exudes warmth beneath the feet.

The front half of the shop focuses on high-quality attire, rare textiles, and eccentric fashion pieces unique to drow artisanship. The back half, accessible through a shimmering curtain of silver-threaded lace, is dedicated to the more dangerous pleasures: poisons disguised as perfumes, oils that seduce or sting, pheromonal essences, paralytics hidden in scented vials, and toxins stored in ornate, decorative flasks. The shop is meticulously curated and its wares expensive; **Szoradryn of House Yerenduhl** runs Silken Secrets with suave charm and cutting discernment. He has no patience for those who treat his work as trivial and will dismiss unserious visitors with cold politeness. Behind the charm is a calculating mind, fully aware of who buys what—and why.

## **Soothing Spores**

Soothing Spores is a small but well-regarded specialty shop situated beside the market stalls in Nunglthil’s central harbor area. The space is intimate and densely arranged, with wall-mounted shelves and low display tables filled with jars, bundles, sachets, and carefully labeled containers of fungi-based remedies. 

The shop is run solely by **Nelka Niddlebom**, an elderly female svirfneblin whose passion for fungi borders on reverence. Nelka speaks eagerly and at length about fungal species, their properties, and their proper use, and she is quick to correct misconceptions. While her public offerings focus on mild, practical remedies, she is deeply knowledgeable about more potent hallucinogenic and mind-altering fungi. Such items are never openly displayed and are only discussed with those she deems respectful, stable, and trustworthy. Nelka believes fungi are tools of balance—capable of healing, insight, or harm depending entirely on intent and preparation.

## **Storeroom**

The Storeroom is a large, secure storage chamber attached directly to the Trading Stalls of Nunglthil, used by vendors who remain in the market for multiple days. It exists to reduce congestion in the harbor area and to provide a trusted place where goods can be kept between market sessions without the risk of theft or sabotage. Access to the Storeroom is strictly controlled by the **Fungadyn** family.

The chamber is sealed by a massive iron door that can only be opened by **Droann Fungadyn** or one of his sons, **Padon** or **Kren**. Inside, the space is open and uncluttered, with heavy stone flooring and reinforced shelving bolted into the walls to accommodate crates, barrels, sacks, and sealed containers. A Fungadyn is always present while the Storeroom is in use, overseeing the placement and removal of goods, recording access, and ensuring that items are neither tampered with nor exchanged without authorization.

## **Sweetspring (bathhouse)**

Sweetspring Bathhouse is just to the right of Deep Desires along the main layer.  Sweetspring preserves a natural Underdark aesthetic with a gently sloping, unworked tunnel leading inward to a single, broad, naturally rounded chamber dominated by a large spring-fed pool. Clear, warm freshwater flows steadily through the basin, entering from one side and exiting the other, ensuring constant renewal. Though the water is pristine, small, slow-moving cyclones circle the surface, keeping it perpetually rippled and obscuring the depths below.

The bathhouse is overseen by **Cidus**, a Storm Giant Goliath whose imposing size contrasts with his calm, attentive demeanor. He maintains order through presence alone, ensuring the space remains peaceful and inviolate. Bioluminescent cleaning fungi line the chamber walls; harvested and handed to bathers, the sponge-like growth gently cleans skin and hair without soap or scent.

## **Trader’s Tankard (pub)**

The Trader’s Tankard is a dockside pub overlooking Rothenloch, known for being quieter, dimmer, and more discreet than the Darkbottom Draught. While not overtly criminal, it attracts those who prefer private conversation, subtle deals, and minimal attention. The establishment has earned a reputation as a place where information changes hands as often as coin, making it a favored haunt of smugglers, middlemen, and traders who value discretion over revelry.

The interior is arranged around a rectangular bar positioned at the center of the room, with small, partially covered booths lining the walls. These booths allow for private discussions without complete isolation, striking a balance between secrecy and safety. **Ormagg Sternbelcher**, a duergar, tends the bar and enforces a strict no-performers policy—music, speeches, and spectacle are unwelcome. Lighting is kept low, voices are expected to remain measured, and Gral’thune guards rotate through the premises to ensure neutrality and prevent violence. When busy, low conversation and sharp banter fill the space; during quieter stretches, the pub can feel almost unnervingly silent.

## **Trading Stalls**

The Trading Stalls is an open market area situated along the inner harbor docks of Nunglthil.  The area consists of an expansive, cavernous platform with a smooth stone floor worn flat by constant foot traffic. Vendors arrive by boat or tunnel, erecting temporary stalls to buy, sell, and barter goods ranging from raw materials and foodstuffs to curiosities and salvaged finds. The market shifts daily in both layout and offerings.

Oversight of the Trading Stalls falls to **Droann Fungadyn**, a respected male ulbin from the settlement of Varrivir. Droann ensures that the Pact’s rules are followed—no violence, no fraud that threatens stability, and no obstruction of trade. His two sons, **Padon** and **Kren**, assist him by managing stall placements, collecting fees, monitoring disputes, and coordinating access to the attached Storeroom, a secured storage area where vendors staying multiple days may keep their goods between market days. The Trading Stalls are closely watched by Gral’thune, but Droann’s steady presence and reputation for fairness keep the market running smoothly.
