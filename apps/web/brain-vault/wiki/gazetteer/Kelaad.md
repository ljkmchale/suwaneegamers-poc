# Overview

Kelaad is a small coastal town nestled along the northern edge of Holder’s Cove, an inlet of the Tideless Span. Once a humble thorp known as Featherwell—named for the flocks of terns that nested along the shores—the village has grown into a small but bustling fishing and trade settlement. Though it is now formally recognized as Kelaad, many locals—particularly the older generations—still call it Featherwell out of tradition. Kelaad is a tight-knit maritime community, shaped by its connection to the sea, the land, and the trade that passes through its modest docks.

## 

## The scent of salt and fresh fish lingers in the air as you approach the bustling village of Kelaad, nestled along the northern curve of Holder’s Cove. Wooden docks stretch into the calm waters of the inlet, where fishing boats and merchant vessels sway with the tide. Beyond the docks, the village rises gently on sloping terrain, with timber and stone buildings clustered along well-trodden dirt roads. Further inland, the landscape transitions into dense coastal woodlands, their deep green canopies swaying gently in the ocean breeze. The people here move with purpose, tending to nets, unloading cargo, or haggling in the small market square.

## History

Originally founded in 1140 as Featherwell, the small coastal thorp was named for the flocks of terns that nested along Holder’s Cove. The settlement began as a modest fishing hamlet, with families relying on small boats, tidal harvesting, and simple trade with passing ships to survive. Over time, as the population grew, Featherwell expanded to support its needs, with hunters, farmers, and craftsmen establishing a more sustainable livelihood.

By 1195, Featherwell had grown, prompting the construction of proper docks to facilitate trade and travel. Merchants and fishermen could now receive supplies more easily, and a small market square developed near the shore. Around this time, the village elders sought to rename Featherwell, giving it an identity that reflected its stronger, more established community. In 1200, the settlement officially adopted the name Kelaad, marking the start of a new era of prosperity. Trade with traveling merchants strengthened its economy, and the boat-building industry became a local specialty.

For decades, Kelaad thrived as a quiet, stable village, but trouble found its way to its shores in late Natali (Spring), 1246, when a group of coastal raiders struck under the cover of night. Avoiding the main docks, the raiders pillaged outlying farmsteads, burning crops and homes before vanishing back into the sea. The attack left families displaced, supplies ruined, and tensions high as the villagers scrambled to rebuild before winter.

### **Holidays and Traditions**

Kelaad’s people are independent and hardworking, shaped by their reliance on the sea. They respect the power of nature and often look to the moons, the tides, and the winds for guidance.

The village hosts seasonal festivals, particularly during fishing harvests or in honor of Sylunara, Coralei, and Cembus, deities associated with the sea, travel, and transformation.

Many sailors and fishermen in Kelaad hold strong beliefs in omens, particularly regarding the moons and sea creatures. It is considered bad luck to set sail on a night when neither moon is visible.

## Government

Kelaad is governed primarily by its Harbormaster, Fanson "Feisty Fan" Edgelow, who oversees trade, security, and the day-to-day operations of the village. Unlike the formal lordships of larger settlements, Kelaad’s leadership is pragmatic and community-driven, with Fanson acting as the de facto leader due to his experience, charisma, and deep connection to the village. His role extends beyond managing the docks—he mediates disputes, enforces local laws through the Harbor Watch, and ensures that trade remains steady to sustain Kelaad’s economy.

Fanson is not alone in guiding the village. When tough decisions arise, Fanson often seeks Alara’s wisdom, especially regarding the well-being of the villagers, natural omens, and concerns that affect the farmland. 

While Fanson and Alara hold the most influence, the village itself operates as a tight-knit community where major decisions, such as large-scale trade agreements or defense efforts, are discussed openly with key villagers, including boat builders, fishers, and farmers. The Harbor Watch enforces Fanson’s rulings, ensuring order in the market and along the docks, but they rarely act with unchecked authority, as the village relies on cooperation rather than strict governance.

### **Harbormaster “Feisty Fan” Fanson Edgelow**

Fanson "Feisty Fan" Edgelow is the energetic and well-loved harbormaster of Kelaad. A middle-aged man, Fanson is a fixture of the village, known for his quick wit, sharp tongue, and unwavering devotion to Kelaad. Whether he's barking orders at dockhands or personally welcoming new ships into port, his enthusiasm never wavers. Though he is lighthearted and enjoys a good laugh and a stiff drink, Fanson takes his duty seriously, ensuring the market and docks run efficiently and that trade remains steady. He also oversees the Harbor Watch, trusting them to keep order in the village.

### **Alara, the village Wisdom**

Alara, the village wisdom, serves as an advisor and spiritual guide. As an elder figure with extensive knowledge of herbalism, healing, and the traditions of the village, she offers counsel on matters that extend beyond commerce and security. 

### **Harbor Watch**

The Harbor Watch serves as Kelaad’s modest but capable guard force. Reporting directly to Fanson, these guards are disciplined, fair, and respected by the townsfolk. Their greyish-blue studded leather armor, white tunics, and practical weapons give them a distinct, nautical appearance, fitting for their primary duty of protecting the market, docks, and trade routes. Though their numbers are small, the Watch responds swiftly to disturbances and assists in the farmlands when needed. Their spears, round shields, and nets make them effective in deterring troublemakers without excessive force.

Though not as imposing as a city garrison, they act with confidence and discipline. Villagers nod in respect as they pass, a sign that these guards are trusted keepers of Kelaad’s peace.

## Laws

Kelaad does not have a formal military presence, but it does have a village watch, led by a harbormaster who oversees both dock security and local disputes. While the village is relatively peaceful, pirates, smugglers, and raiders occasionally pose a threat, particularly those operating out of the Tideless Span.  And though Kelaad has its share of rough sailors and traders, most crime is minor—bar brawls, pickpocketing, or the occasional dispute over trade agreements.

Characters engaging in criminal activity are arrested by the guard and charged per the [City Ordinances](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1BGx_-fz7LsgElP6Lk2RFbh2-RRFTnOhx7RJ15ymoShs/edit#heading=h.9ltjapyzb7pv). 

## Economy and Trade

Kelaad’s economy is heavily reliant on the sea. The primary industries include:

* Fishing – The village provides fresh and salted fish to other settlements along the coast.

* Boatbuilding & Repair – A small but skilled group of boatwrights craft fishing boats, rowboats, and simple trade skiffs. While they lack the capacity to build full-sized ships, Kelaad’s boatbuilders are known for their sturdy, reliable craft.

* Herbalism & Healing – The coastal woodlands provide access to medicinal plants, and the village has a small group of herbalists and healers who supply tonics, poultices, and remedies.

With its modest docks, Kelaad can only accommodate two larger ships at a time, though smaller boats frequently come and go. Because of its limited port capacity, most trade is conducted with nearby villages and towns rather than major cities.

## Religion

Kelaad is not dominated by any single faith, but several deities are commonly worshipped:

* Sylunara (Goddess of the Moons & Transformation) – Sailors and travelers look to Sylunara for guidance, particularly when navigating at night.

* Coralei (The Tide Keeper, Goddess of the Seas & Oceans) – Revered by the fishing and maritime community, with small shrines built along the docks.

* Cembus (The Seeker, God of Exploration & Travel) – Many merchants and traders invoke Cembus’ blessing before setting sail or departing on a journey.

* Vallari (The Dawn Princess, Goddess of Life & Nature) – Farmers and herbalists offer prayers and offerings to Vallari, especially during planting seasons.

## Balding Tern, The

The Balding Tern serves as Kelaad’s main gathering place, where fishermen, boat-builders, traders, and travelers share news, rumors, and hearty meals. Harri Gaeldon, the gruff but good-natured owner, has seen his share of adventure and is a wealth of nautical knowledge—willing to spin a tale over a drink or offer advice to those with business on the water. He’s well-connected in Kelaad and could be a useful ally, though he expects fair dealings in return. His second-in-command, Trudi Petalbee, keeps the tavern running smoothly, handling rowdy patrons with a sharp tongue and a knowing smirk. She’s more perceptive than she lets on and picks up on gossip and secrets that might interest the players.

The Balding Tern is an ideal hub for gathering information, finding work, or making local contacts. Sailors passing through may have heard news from across the Tideless Span, while locals discuss unusual happenings—be it a strange wreck off the coast, whispers of smugglers, or rumors of sea creatures stirring in the depths. The inn’s rooms are simple but secure, offering a safe rest before the next leg of an adventure. 

## 

## Stepping into The Balding Tern, the warmth of lantern light and the scent of salted fish and spiced stew welcome you from the briny air of the cove. The tavern is sturdy and practical, its weathered wooden beams and creaking floorboards bearing the marks of years spent serving sailors and travelers alike. Above the well-worn bar, a large ship’s wheel hangs as a centerpiece.  There is mismatched furniture that appears to be salvaged from old ships, and faded maps and fishing nets decorate the walls. A staircase in the back leads up to rooms that can be purchased for a stay.
