## About Kelaad

Kelaad sits on the southern shore of Holder’s Cove, a wide bite of the Tideless Span that used to be a tight inlet. Wooden docks take the fishing boats and, at most, two larger keels at a time. Timber and stone climb the slope behind them; woods close in beyond the last farm clearing. Older folk still call the place Featherwell. The rest answer to Kelaad, and to three white feathers on a wooden shield. Salt and fish hang in the air. A visitor finds work, a berth, a rumor from across the Span, or a room at the Balding Tern—then learns the town is smaller than the cove makes it look.

### **The People**

About nine hundred souls live here, most of them human or half-elf, with dwarves, elves, halflings and gnomes mixed through the streets and a few other folk off the ships. Strangers are given a stall before they are given a speech; the cove eats if the keels keep coming. Houses stand between water and wood. The farms are small. Work is nets, hulls, and herbs. Featherwell habit dies hard: fish are cleaned in the river, never on the dock, and a dead tern means the stretch is cursed. Plenty wait out a dark-moon night before they put to sea, then pretend they were only checking the weather.

### **Beliefs**

No single god owns Kelaad. Sailors look to Sylunara when the moons are the only road, and they will not launch when both are gone. Coralei keeps the water; her Tidecaller’s Shrine stands by the piers. Merchants touch Cembus before a crossing. Vallari is the wood and the garden, and the herbalists still thank her for the years the trees ran rich. Harvest festivals and moon nights do more work than any temple. Old omen and New Order sit in the same kitchen, and nobody thinks that is strange.

### **Economy and Trade**

The town lives on the water and the trees behind it. Fresh and salted fish leave for other shores. Boatwrights turn out sturdy skiffs and fishing hulls—not ships of any size, but craft that come home. Herbalists walk the coastal wood for leaf and root, and Alara’s people sell what they make. Most trade is with nearby places: across the Span to Ingriss, Eastmare, and Oldport, inland toward Raselle and Leterboun. Two large ships can lie at the docks. After that, you wait your turn or you lighter the cargo in.

### **Holy Days & Traditions**

Kelaad keeps few holy days and a great many habits. When the nets come in heavy, the town eats on the square and pours a cup for Coralei at the Tidecaller’s Shrine. Planters and herbalists mark Vallari when the wood greens. Sailors give Sylunara the night both moons stand together, and they will not launch when both are dark. Cembus gets a word and a coin before a keel leaves the cove. The old Featherwell rules still sit beside all of that: fish cleaned in the river, never on the dock; a dead tern means the stretch is unlucky; no new name on a hull until that stretch turns. Festivals here look like work that stopped for an evening, not a city pageant.

# Once Featherwell

Kelaad sits on the southern shore of Holder’s Cove, a wide bite of the Tideless Span. It was not always so. Before the Awakening the place was a tight inlet, and the thorp on its bank was called Featherwell—named for the terns that nested the shallows in such numbers that the strand looked feathered after every tide.

The old name has not died. Elders still say Featherwell when they mean home. The rest of the town answered the opened cove with a new one: Kelaad, and three white feathers on a wooden shield.

## 

## The scent of salt and fresh fish lingers in the air as you approach the bustling town of Kelaad, nestled along the northern curve of Holder’s Cove. Wooden docks stretch into the calm waters of the inlet, where fishing boats and merchant vessels sway with the tide. Beyond the docks, the town rises gently on sloping terrain, with timber and stone buildings clustered along well-trodden dirt roads. Further inland, the landscape transitions into dense coastal woodlands, their deep green canopies swaying gently in the ocean breeze. 

## History

Here are the key dates and events that have shaped Kelaad:

* 1140. Founded as Featherwell on a narrow inlet of what would later be called Holder’s Cove. Tern flocks nested the shore. Families lived by small boats, tidal harvest, and whatever trade a passing ship would spare.

* 1195, Year of Bearing. Proper docks and a shore market went in. The inlet still pinched access to the Tideless Span, so most trade stayed local. Elders talked of a stronger name. They did not take one.

* 1227, Year of Abandonment (the Awakening). Muerg and his earthen mights remade the coast. The inlet was torn open into the present Holder’s Cove and given a true mouth onto the Span. Some shoreline was lost; the town held. Docks that had served a pocket now faced open water. Old-god prayers went unanswered. Traffic from Inverland and the mainland road became possible for the first time.

* 1228, Year of Enlightenment. The town took the name Kelaad and the three-feather arms—tern sign of Featherwell, set as three for the trades that kept the place: net, hull, and herb. New Order faith took root at the water (Coralei, Sylunara, Cembus) and in the woods (Vallari). Vallari’s ten-year blessing (1228–1238) doubled the woodland yield and made the herbalists more than a sideline. Boatwrights shifted from inlet skiffs to sturdier craft fit for the Span.

* 1228–1246. Kelaad grew into a small fishing-and-trade town. Docks still take only two larger ships at a time. Older folk keep the name Featherwell. No later disaster has broken that pattern. Present year is 1246, Year of Discovery.

## Harbormaster & Elder

Kelaad has no lord. The town answers to Harbormaster Fanson “Feisty Fan” Edgelow, who sits in the keep above the cove. The title never changed when Featherwell became a town: the opened cove made the docks the town’s heart, so the dock-master became burgomaster in all but name. Folk still say Harbormaster. He runs trade, the Watch, disputes, and defense. Elder Alara is his main advisor—the Wisdom, healer, and the voice for woods, sick, and old custom. Day-to-day steel belongs to Sergeant Raug Morley of the Harbor Watch. Large calls (trade pacts, defense of the town) are talked through with fishers, boatwrights, and farmers before Fanson binds them. Kelaad answers to no seat up the Raselle road.

### **Harbormaster “Feisty Fan” Fanson Edgelow**

*Male human, about 50\. De facto burgomaster. Seat: the keep. Second office: the docks.*  
He is wiry, salt-and-pepper cropped hair, green eyes, sailor’s garb and a captain’s whistle even in the keep hall. Quick tongue, stiff drink, no patience for a title that would make him sound like a mainland governor. He earned the post by keeping the cove working after 1228 and has held it by acclaim since. If he falls, the town will have to name another Harbormaster; there is no heir.

## 

## A wiry man of fifty comes on like weather off the Span—salt-and-pepper hair cropped short, green eyes already measuring you. Sturdy sailor’s cloth, not court dress, and a captain’s whistle at his belt. 

### **Elder Alara**

*Female half-elf, elder. Town Wisdom and keep healer.*  
She keeps the infirmary in the keep and the old Featherwell memory in her head: herbs from the coastal wood, births, burials, and which moon to wait on. Fanson does not move on land, sick, or sacred matters without her. She does not command the Watch. 

## 

## An older woman in a plain shawl and stained healer’s sleeves, hair gone mostly white and bound off her face. Her hands are lined with work, not jewelry, and smell faintly of crushed leaf and salt. 

### **Sergeant Raug Morley**

*Male human. Sergeant of the Harbor Watch. Reports to Fanson.*  
He holds the yard, the gates, and the street when the Harbormaster is on the water. Spears, round shields, nets; beige studded leather and brown tunics. Small force, used to dock brawls and smugglers, not a standing army.

### **Harbor Watch**

Kelaad’s Watch is small and local. Sergeant Raug Morley runs the yard, gates, streets, docks, and market. He reports to Harbormaster Fanson. They are not a garrison and do not rule the town. Duties: keep order at the water and the square, walk the farm tracks when called, hold the keep gate. Kit is built for the cove:  spear, round shield, and net.

## Culture and Society

Kelaad is mostly human and half-elf town, with enough dwarves, elves, halflings and gnomes that the docks never look like one people. Stranger faces turn up with the keels and are given a stall before they are given a sermon. Trade keeps the cove alive; a shut door would do what a storm could not.

The houses stand between water and wood. A few small farms break the trees, but the forest has the last word on three sides. Folk here work nets, hulls, and herbs, and they take a Span captain’s coin without pretending the town is bigger than it is.

### **Superstitions**

Featherwell never left; the people here are superstitious. A dead tern means the stretch is cursed—no launch, no new name on a hull, no handshake on a deal until the days turn. Fish are cleaned in the river, never on the boards of the dock. Plenty will not put out when both moons are dark. The young roll their eyes and still wait for the stretch to pass.

### **Laws and Enforcement**

Kelaad keeps the peace with a short Watch and a Harbormaster who still hears cases himself.  A charge that cannot wait for morning goes to the keep.

| Body | Role | Reach |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Harbor Watch | Patrol, arrest and holding | Docks, market streets, keep gate |
| Sergeant Morley | Commands the Watch; first officer on a seizure | Town |
| Harbormaster Edgelow | Tariffs, smuggling, restricted cargo | Town; keep is his court |
| Elder Alara | Counsel on custom | Asked in; does not pass sentence |

The Watch makes the arrest. Fanson hears it. Alara speaks if the matter touches old Featherwell custom. Raug does not judge his own catch.

### **Arrest and Judgement**

The following steps highlight the standard procedures for arresting those that violate the laws.

1. ***Stop or seizure***  
   A Watch patrol may hold anyone they have cause to hold. On the docks that includes cargo and papers. They take the accused to the keep. They do not hold a body on the boards overnight.  
2. ***Charge***  
   Raug or the Watch on duty names the charge in the keep. No charge means questions and the door. Fanson can amend a charge; he cannot ignore one already spoken in front of witnesses.  
3. ***Holding***  
   The keep has a small lockup. Overnight is common. The town has no second jail. A ship in the cove is not a cell—if they mean to run, the Watch takes them off it.  
4. ***Judgment***  
   Fanson hears the case in the keep hall, usually the same day or the next.  
* Minor — brawl, dock fine, small theft, a knife shown and not used.  
* Serious — blood drawn, burglary, wrecking a boat, striking the Watch, smuggling that starves the town.  
* He takes testimony and whoever saw it. It is not a long trial. Alara may sit the hall; she does not close it.  
5. ***Sentence***  
   Fine, damages, a season of dock or mill labor, a stretch in the lockup, or exile from the cove. Unpaid fines become labor. Death is not a tool of this court; a crime that would ask for it is held and sent up the Raselle road.

A typical adventurer case is one scene: Watch stop, keep, Fanson. Only exile or a sending up the road takes longer.

## Holy Days, Festivals and Traditions

Kelaad’s people are independent and hardworking, shaped by their reliance on the sea. They respect the power of nature and often look to the moons, the tides, and the winds for guidance.

### **Religion**

Kelaad is not dominated by any single faith, but several deities are commonly worshipped:

* Sylunara (Goddess of the Moons & Transformation) – Sailors and travelers look to Sylunara for guidance, particularly when navigating at night.

* Coralei (The Tide Keeper, Goddess of the Seas & Oceans) – Revered by the fishing and maritime community, with small shrines built along the docks.

* Cembus (The Seeker, God of Exploration & Travel) – Many merchants and traders invoke Cembus’ blessing before setting sail or departing on a journey.

* Vallari (The Dawn Princess, Goddess of Life & Nature) – Farmers and herbalists offer prayers and offerings to Vallari, especially during planting seasons.

The town hosts seasonal festivals, particularly during fishing harvests or in honor of Sylunara, Coralei, and Cembus, deities associated with the sea, travel, and transformation.

Many sailors and fishermen in Kelaad hold strong beliefs in omens, particularly regarding the moons and sea creatures. It is considered bad luck to set sail on a night when neither moon is visible.

## Economy and Trade

Kelaad’s economy is heavily reliant on the sea. The primary industries include:

* Fishing – The town provides fresh and salted fish to other settlements along the coast.

* Boatbuilding & Repair – A small but skilled group of boatwrights craft fishing boats, rowboats, and simple trade skiffs. While they lack the capacity to build full-sized ships, Kelaad’s boatbuilders are known for their sturdy, reliable craft.

* Herbalism & Healing – The coastal woodlands provide access to medicinal plants, and the town has a small group of herbalists and healers who supply tonics, poultices, and remedies.

With its modest docks, Kelaad can only accommodate two larger ships at a time, though smaller boats frequently come and go. Because of its limited port capacity, most trade is conducted with nearby towns and towns rather than major cities.

## Tavern:  Balding Tern, The

The Balding Tern serves as Kelaad’s main gathering place, where fishermen, boat-builders, traders, and travelers share news, rumors, and hearty meals. Harri Gaeldon, the gruff but good-natured owner, has seen his share of adventure and is a wealth of nautical knowledge—willing to spin a tale over a drink or offer advice to those with business on the water. He’s well-connected in Kelaad and could be a useful ally, though he expects fair dealings in return. His second-in-command, Trudi Petalbee, keeps the tavern running smoothly, handling rowdy patrons with a sharp tongue and a knowing smirk. She’s more perceptive than she lets on and picks up on gossip and secrets that might interest the players.

The Balding Tern is an ideal hub for gathering information, finding work, or making local contacts. Sailors passing through may have heard news from across the Tideless Span, while locals discuss unusual happenings—be it a strange wreck off the coast, whispers of smugglers, or rumors of sea creatures stirring in the depths. The inn’s rooms are simple but secure, offering a safe rest before the next leg of an adventure. 

## 

## Stepping into The Balding Tern, the warmth of lantern light and the scent of salted fish and spiced stew welcome you from the briny air of the cove. The tavern is sturdy and practical, its weathered wooden beams and creaking floorboards bearing the marks of years spent serving sailors and travelers alike. Above the well-worn bar, a large ship’s wheel hangs as a centerpiece.  There is mismatched furniture that appears to be salvaged from old ships, and faded maps and fishing nets decorate the walls. A staircase in the back leads up to rooms that can be purchased for a stay.
