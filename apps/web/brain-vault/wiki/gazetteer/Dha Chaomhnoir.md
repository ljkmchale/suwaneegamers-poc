## Overview

Dhá Chaomhnóir, commonly referred to as Dhá, is located on the southern coast of Inverland.  Housing the largest of Vaults in the Reserve, the city boasts the largest number of wealthy residents in all of Myrdae.  With high end-trade and access to both money and a busy port, the city attracts tourists and merchants alike, but also attracts a criminal element, too.  Additionally, there is a large working class as well, as one would expect with so many job opportunities. 

Dhá is one of the busiest ports in Inverland as well, with an advantageous location of idyllic weather, calm water, and easy trade routes to mainland Myrdae.  As such, the city is a hub for both imports and exports for Inverland.

## 

## As one the richest cities in all of Myrdae, Dhá Chaomhnóir, called simply Dhá by most from Inverland, is known for its favorable climate, fair rules and regulations, and for having the largest Vault, not only in Inverland, but throughout Myrdae.  

## Unlike other cities where the affluent live seasonally, the wealthy live here year-round.  And while most cities have a class system, the high class in Dhá are uber-elite, largely because of the proximity to their wealth, the high-end trade available in the city, and the quick access to the thriving port, making sea trade and travel both simple and convenient. 

## Dhá is known for its well marked streets and neighborhoods, defined by largely by class or purpose. The entire city is covered in the iconography of the Guardians, who are large, twin statues that dwell in the Vault are said to come to life to battle any threat to the city.

### **Organizations**

Lionheart \- Lion insignia on the heart.  Criminal syndicate (mob-like organization), that appear as a group to guard Dhá

This is a group of environmental enthusiasts. They worship Madre Verde \- who everyone assumes is their somewhat questionable personification of mother nature. They are reviled by most of the people in the towns that have heard of them. They are a force of good and bad depending on the day and viewpoint. They can help or hinder travelers but gravitate to nature lovers.

The Great Houses keep them out of the City.

They were led by Ainran, a very powerful and charismatic leader. He was the subject of a bounty reward from the Cur family. He has not been seen since leaving for the most recent All Bright ceremony. It is assumed he has been killed for the reward, but the Greens insist he has been taken by the Mother to become a god. After a few incursions into the city that were rebuffed by the Cur family, the greens have returned to patrolling the roads and hassling merchants who do not show proper respect to Madre Verde.

Populi Verde \- Group that worships Madre Verde (not the same as “The Mother”).  Was known internally for the sacrifices they made on All-Bright.  Had been “Wanted” in the city at one point because they were wrongfully accused of much of the crime in the city.  Were involved in the takedown of the cultist group called “The Woeborn” (group who worshiped Suthrin).

# Government

### **Family Ties**

Dhá is controlled by a few powerful families. The ***Cur*** family (Crest is a lion with a heart) is one of the most powerful. They control many of the brothels and gambling houses in Dunmoore. Their gold-laden wagons transport payments from their “businesses” in the towns and cities throughout Dunmoore to the Vaults. The leader of the Cur family is referred to as Don Cur.

The House of ***Krem*** was a powerful family. The head of the house was a warlock of significant power who staffed the home with undead. It is rumored that the House of Krem made a move to usurp the power of the Cur family by raiding its brothel payments along the road to Blackfield. Some say they even dared to rob the Vault of the Cur family under the noses of the very Guardians themselves. The Vault Keepers and the Cur family deny that particular rumor.

However, the Warlock Krem and his concubine were found murdered in their bed and the home was nearly burned to the ground. The Cur family insisted on leading the cleanup and subsequent investigation.

Not much is known of the other families, but they all live in an uneasy truce controlling various aspects of Dhá.

## Vaults, The

## 

## The Vault is part of the Realm Depository & Exchange (or RDE), an organization that has set up an network of repositories that act as an exchange system for coins and legal tender across Myrdae.  The location in Dhá is the largest of the Vaults in Myrdae.

## This pyramid-shaped building is surrounded by large circular grounds secured with 30’ tall walls that encompass the center of the city.  There are three entrances into the grounds, each manned by round-the-clock security.  Once inside the outer wall, grounds are open, covered in gravel, and provide a line of sight across the vast area, save for a 4’ tall low wall that forms an inside 8’ perimeter around the base of the main building. There are four smaller structures inside the grounds \- two at one entrance, one at another, and a larger one at the third entrance.  These house the associates, guards, and monks who live and work in the Vault.

## As you enter the building from one of three entrances, circular stairs lead down and up in the center of the pyramid, and you can see several tables and chairs set up around the perimeter of the room.  Upon entering, a Guard will usually inquire why you have come and check your credentials, and you will then be led by a Monk to your destination.  If you are setting up an account, your business will be conducted by an Associate at one of the tables on the main floor.  Then to make a deposit and/or if you are accessing a vault from a previously set up account (and this is where your funds are held), you will be led down the stairs, where at the bottom you will find 50’ tall walls in a circular chamber with two large stone statues seated on thrones in the center.  You can see 12 large wooden doors evenly spaced around the circle with large markings above each door. An Associate and Monk will then lead you to the appropriate door where you may make deposits or withdrawals. If you need to access funds or make a deposit and this is not where your funds are held, you will be taken upstairs, where you will meet with an Associate and Vault Keeper to authenticate your funds before conducting your business.  The top of the 30’ stairs opens into a small room with two doors on the right and left.

### **The Bottom Floor:** 

The markings above the doors in the Vault chamber are elvish and are numbered 1-12 set up like a clock.  Position 12 is for the wealthiest clients; followed by 10-11 for wealthy; 3-9 for well off merchants; 4-8 for the middle class; and 1-3 for the unwashed and/or poorest customers.  Additional guards are posted at doors 9-12.  Once inside of a door, it is closed and locked and you are lead to numbered vaults of various sizes.  There are 5’ niches every 10’, on alternating sides of the 300’ halls, where clients may utilize the counters to handle their contents.  Additionally, there are several large vaults in hallways 9-12, where the vaults themselves are large enough for the clients to be inside their vaults privately.

The large stone statues seated on chairs are actually Guardians \- who come to life if needed \- in service of security to the Vault.

### **The Upstairs Chamber**  

Rising up 30’, the upper chamber is a small room with two doors on the right and left.  These doors lead to 2 outer rooms that are 15’x15’ each.  They consist of a central table and two chairs, as well as countertops along two of the walls, and are used by personnel to facilitate business with customers. There is also a door that leads out of both rooms to an inner room. Customers using the RDE for transfers do not access the inner room \- their transactions are handled by Associates and the VK.  The inner room holds a teleportation circle (with a sigil).  The circle is used to transfer goods as needed to other vaults.  It is also used by the elite and well-connected to travel, upon request.  

### **Personnel** 

All those employed in the RDE throughout Myrdae as guards, associates, and Vault Keepers are male elves.  Those who are Monks remain hooded when in public and are made up of various races and genders. There are two guards who are stationed at each entrance at all times (entrances to the city, as well as the entrances into the building itself).  Guards rotate shifts every 4 hours, and usually work 2 or 3 shifts each day.  Monks perform various tasks inside the Vault (usually as escorts or runners), but you can also occasionally see them outside within the vault walls, tending to maintenance and upkeep of the grounds and housing structures.  Monks usually work in 6-hour shifts.  Monks speak only when spoken to, say as little as possible, and never reveal their names. Associates all work inside and interface with customers, usually working 6-hour shifts.  Most are multilingual, have excellent memories, and are gifted with math and numbers.

In addition to the security described above, the Vault in Dhá also houses 2 guardians \- who are large stone golems who live inside the Vault in the base of the pyramid, and come to life when/if needed.

This vault is part of the “Reserve” network of repositories across Myrdae and adheres to the [vault rules](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1BGx_-fz7LsgElP6Lk2RFbh2-RRFTnOhx7RJ15ymoShs/edit#heading=h.nq73cmib4srm) found in the Myrdae Reference of DMs.

## Tavern Games

### **Bones**

(from *Descent into Avernus*)

* Each player requires several six-sided dice.   
* Each player puts the agreed ante in the pot.  
* Each player rolls three (3) dice.   
* Play then proceeds clockwise around the table, with the host of the game going last.  
* On their turn, a player can choose to “stand” or “roll.”   
  * Stand: If the player stands, the next player can take a turn.   
  * Roll:  A player who rolls takes an additional die and rolls it.   
  * Bust:  If the total of their dice exceeds 21, they “bust” and are out of the game. Otherwise they can keep rolling additional dice until they either stand or break.  
* After everyone has had a turn, the highest point total (excluding players who busted) wins the game and takes the pot.

### **Eye’s Out**

* Each player starts with four (4) six-sided dice.   
* Each player puts the agreed ante in the pot.  
* Each player rolls all the dice.   
* All ones (1) that are rolled are discarded.  
* Each player that wants to stay in must add another ante.  
* The last person with dice remaining wins the pot.

### **Three Pikes**

* Each player starts with five (5) six-sided dice.   
* Each player puts the agreed ante in the pot.  
* Each player rolls three (3) dice and keeps the rolls secret.   
* Play then proceeds clockwise around the table to bet, check or fold.  
  * Bet: The player raises the pot by the ante.   
  * Call:  The player must match the bets.  
  * Fold:  The player loses their ante and is out of the game.  
* All players still in the game roll an additional die, keeping the roll a secret.  
* Play then proceeds clockwise around the table to bet, check or fold.  
* All players still in the game roll their final die; again, keeping the roll secret.  
* Play then proceeds clockwise around the table to bet, check or fold.  
* Player with the best hand wins the pot determined by the following:  
  * Five of a kind  
  * Four of a kind  
  * Full house (three of one, two of another)  
  * Straight (five in a row)  
  * Three of a kind  
  * Two pair (two of one, two of another)  
  * One pair  
  * High number

### **Arm Wrestle**

* This is a Strength challenge.   
* You need three (3) steps to wrestle their arm to the table.  
* You make a contested strength check.  
  * Success: you move one (1) step closer to victory.  
  * Success (natural 20\):  you move two (2) steps closer to victory.  
  * Fail:  you lose one (1) step.

### **Dagger Juggling**

* This is a Dexterity challenge.   
* Bet is made on success, which can be done by number of rounds or by betting on each check.  
* Roll a DC check based on the number of daggers you are juggling:  
  * DC10: for 2 daggers  
  * DC15: for 4 daggers  
  * DC20: for 4 daggers  
* A failed throw caused 1d4 non-lethal damage.
