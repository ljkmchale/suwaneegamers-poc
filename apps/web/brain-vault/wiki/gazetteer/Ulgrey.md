## About Ulgrey

Ulgrey is a modest but busy crossroads village located at the fork of the Greyrun River. It sits at the meeting point of four roads: the New Climb north to the dwarven halls of Dunduar, the Glimmercoast Way west to Emberstran, the South Kahlbit Trace east toward Trailpoint, and a smaller road south to Harbok. The village serves as a practical stopping point for travelers and merchants moving between the mountains, the coast, and the inland settlements.

### **The People**

Ulgrey is home to a practical and hardworking mix of humans, weefolk, half-elves, and a few others drawn by the opportunities of the crossroads. Most residents are involved in river fishing, farming, trade, or crafting. The village has a welcoming but no-nonsense attitude toward outsiders — travelers are common, and the locals have learned to be efficient hosts while still maintaining a close-knit community feel. Leadership has recently passed to the Basser family, who moved from the Riverdog Inn into the Mansion to serve as Burgomaster.

### **Beliefs**

The village was originally founded by worshippers of the elemental god Torec, and his stone circle on the northern rise remains an important site for seasonal ceremonies. For a time the people turned toward Neera, but after the Awakening her church fell silent. Today, most spiritual life centers around the worship of Cembus. The Ulgrey Oratory has been reshaped to serve both as a place of worship and a sanctuary for those on the road. Locals tend to be pragmatic in their beliefs, respecting the elements while placing strong value on safe passage and hospitality to travelers.

### **Economy and Trade**

Ulgrey’s economy is built on two main pillars: river fishing and its position as a waypoint for dwarven goods. Fishermen work the Greyrun and its feeder streams, supplying fresh catch to the village and passing caravans. The road north to Dunduar brings a steady flow of iron, copper, and worked metal, much of which is processed or sold onward through Grabner’s Smithy. Ferebert’s Mill produces highly regarded “Ferebert’s Gold” flour, while the Crossroads Market (held twice every stretch) and Solly’s Supplies serve as the main hubs for local and traveling trade. The village also profits from boat rentals and river passage arranged at the Dockhouse.

### **Holidays and Traditions**

Ulgrey observes the seasonal ceremonies at Torec’s Circle, particularly those marking changes in weather and the river’s flow. The most widely celebrated event is the Riverwake Festival, held twice a year when the market square fills with music, food, and offerings to both Torec and Cembus for safe travels and good fishing. Many locals also maintain small personal traditions of leaving offerings in the stone bowls at the graveyard or lighting a candle in the Oratory’s lantern before beginning a long journey. Market days themselves have become a kind of minor recurring celebration, as farmers, fishers, and travelers gather to trade, share news, and enjoy Shanna’s Jolly Cakes and Harrek’s signature drinks at the Riverdog Inn.

## History

Ulgrey’s earliest roots trace back to a small band of roughly twenty worshippers of the Hooded Lady who settled at the river fork after the death of their leader, Amberblade. They sealed his body in their former underground encampment and abandoned their old faith, turning instead to the elemental god Torec. With the guidance of the moon elf wizard Paeris Iya’ren, they raised the circle of standing stones on the northern rise that still bears Torec’s name.

As the settlement grew, the people gradually shifted their devotion toward Neera, goddess of protection. In 1220 the Church of Neera was completed, becoming the finest structure in the village at the time. The Awakening in 1227 changed everything. When Neera fell silent and stopped answering prayers, the town’s cleric, Zyre Wiscard, abandoned Ulgrey in search of answers. The church stood empty for five years.

In 1232, Mother Velna Isenbell, a Seeker of the Advents of Harmony devoted to Cembus, god of Exploration and Travel, arrived in the village. She convinced the townsfolk to allow her to take over the abandoned church and rededicated it as the Ulgrey Oratory, reshaping its interior to better serve travelers while preserving its strong stone exterior.

Roughly ten to twelve years ago, leadership of the village passed to the Basser family. Elsie and Milton Basser, who had successfully run the Riverdog Inn for many years, were asked to take over as Burgomaster and moved into the Mansion. They sold the Riverdog Inn to Harrek and Shanna, who continue to operate it today.

The village has continued to develop around its position as a crossroads and river waypoint. The Riverdog Inn remains the oldest building in Ulgrey, having been expanded many times over the decades. Paeris’ Rise, the tower, is the second-oldest structure. More recently, Gruno Grabner returned from several years of training with the dwarves of Dunduar and now works alongside his mother Agnes at the smithy, bringing new techniques to the village.

## Citizenry

Ulgrey is home to a practical, hardworking mix of humans, weefolk (gnomes and halflings), half-elves, and a few others. Most residents make their living through fishing, farming, trade, or skilled crafts. The village has a no-nonsense but welcoming attitude toward outsiders, shaped by its position as a busy crossroads. Travelers are common, and the locals have learned to be efficient hosts while still maintaining a tight-knit community.

Most residents fall into practical, working classes: fishers, farmers, traders, crafters, and laborers. There are few idle rich or dedicated scholars. The village has a strong “everyone works” culture, and even the leadership (the Basser family) maintains a hands-on approach. Because of the constant flow of travelers, many locals are comfortable dealing with outsiders, though they remain protective of their community and resources.

## Holidays and Traditions

Ulgrey’s holidays and traditions reflect its layered history and practical, river-crossroads lifestyle.

### **Seasonal Rites at Torec’s Circle**

Old school villagers honor seasonal rites at Torec’s Circle.  Four times a year, at the solstices and equinoxes, they gather at Torec’s Circle for simple ceremonies honoring the elemental god. These rites focus on the changing seasons, and protection from harsh weather. Offerings of water, ash, earth, and sand are commonly left at the stones. 

### **Riverwake**

The largest and most widely celebrated event in Ulgrey is Riverwake, held twice a year — once in early spring when the river swells, and again in autumn before the waters slow. The market square fills with music, food stalls, and dancing. Boats are blessed, and offerings are made to both Torec and Cembus for good fishing and safe passage. Harrek and Shanna usually host a large gathering at the Riverdog Inn during this time.

### **Market Days**

While not a formal holiday, the Crossroads Market (held twice every stretch) functions as a recurring social celebration. Farmers, fishers, and travelers gather not only to trade but to share news, enjoy Shanna’s Jolly Cakes, and drink Harrek’s signature ales and liquors. These days are often the liveliest regular events in the village.

### **Graveyard Offerings**

Many families regularly visit the graveyard to leave small offerings in the stone bowls beside graves (water, ash, sand, or earth). This practice is tied to Torec and is seen as a way of asking the elements to watch over the dead. Odo Mukalbon quietly maintains these bowls as part of his duties.

## Government

Ulgrey follows a simple but effective system of governance centered on a single Burgomaster supported by appointed civic officials.

### **Elsie Basser**

The current Burgomeister is Elsie Basser, a pragmatic and respected human woman. Roughly ten years ago, she and her husband Milton were asked to take over leadership of the village. They moved from the Riverdog Inn into the Mansion, where Elsie now serves as Burgomaster. Her husband Milton Basser assists with trade, relations with Dunduar, and general village matters. Their daughter Sarni Basser helps with record-keeping and administrative tasks. 

### **Appointed Officials**

Two important positions are appointed (or reappointed) by the Burgomaster to help maintain order and protect village resources:

Elsie Basser prefers a hands-on, practical approach to leadership. She handles most disputes personally and relies on Liah Khess and Guth Turhammer to manage day-to-day enforcement and protection of village resources. For larger issues, she consults a small circle of trusted advisors that includes Mother Velna Isenbell, Harrek of the Riverdog Inn, and occasionally Paeris Iya’ren.

## Laws

### Ulgrey has no standing guard, militia, or official watch. Instead, the village relies on a system of rotating civic duty. Able-bodied men take turns serving on the night watch, unpaid, to help protect the village. When someone breaks the law, they are typically restrained and held until the Constable can decide what should be done.

### **Rotating Watch**

Every able-bodied man in the village is legally required to take his turn on the night watch. This duty rotates through the village so no single person bears the burden for long. Watchmen patrol at night, watching for fires, stray livestock, and suspicious travelers. The watch is completely unpaid and is considered a civic obligation rather than a formal position. 

### **Handling Offenders**

Ulgrey has no jail. When someone is caught breaking the law, they are typically tied up or placed in manacles on the spot and held until they can be brought before the Constable. The Constable then determines the appropriate response. Minor offenses are often handled with fines, public labor, or restitution. More serious crimes may result in harsher punishment, temporary exile, or — in rare cases — being turned over to outside authorities. The village prefers to handle matters internally whenever possible.

## Economy and Trade

Ulgrey is not a wealthy village, but it is stable. Its economy is practical and interconnected, built on two main strengths: its position as a crossroads and its access to the Greyrun River. Fresh fish, dwarven metal goods, quality flour, and basic supplies flow through the village daily. Travelers and merchants regularly stop here to rest, resupply, and trade, making the village a practical waypoint between the mountains, the coast, and the inland settlements.

### **River and Fishing**

The Greyrun River and its two feeder streams provide a steady supply of fresh fish. Fishermen sell their daily catch directly at the Dockhouse, which also handles boat rentals and river passage for those traveling downstream. The river trade is a consistent, if modest, source of income for many families.

### **Dwarven Trade**

The road north to Dunduar brings a regular flow of iron, copper, silver, and worked metal goods. Much of this material passes through Ulgrey on its way to Emberstran and Trailpoint. Grabner’s Smithy benefits significantly from this trade, as Gruno Grabner’s dwarven training allows the village to produce higher-quality metalwork than most settlements its size.

### **Agriculture and Milling**

Local farms supply grain and produce. Ferebert’s Mill grinds grain into “Ferebert’s Gold,” a fine, highly regarded flour that commands premium prices from bakers in neighboring settlements. When grain is scarce, the mill switches to a secondary lumber operation, cutting timber for the village and passing merchants.

### **Market and Retail**

The Crossroads Market operates twice every stretch in the central square, serving as the main hub for local farmers, fishers, and traveling merchants. Goods that do not sell at market often end up at Solly’s Supplies, giving the village a steady secondary marketplace for tools, clothing, dried goods, and oddities.

### **Hospitality and Services**

The Riverdog Inn provides lodging, meals, and drinks to travelers, while the Dockhouse offers boat rentals and river passage. These services bring in steady coin from merchants, adventurers, and migrants moving along the four roads.

## Religion

The people of Ulgrey hold a practical and layered view of faith. While many still respect the old elemental god Torec and his stone circle on the northern rise, most now look to the worship of Cembus, god of Exploration and Travel. The Ulgrey Oratory serves as the spiritual heart of the village, functioning as both a place of worship and a sanctuary for those on the road.

In 1232, Mother Velna Isenbell, a Seeker of the Advents of Harmony devoted to Cembus (god of Exploration and Travel), arrived in Ulgrey. She took over the abandoned church and rededicated it as the Ulgrey Oratory. While she preserved the strong stone exterior, she reshaped the interior to better serve travelers, adding the Wayfarer’s Gallery and the open Roadwardens’ Cloister.

Despite the shift toward Cembus, Torec is still respected. The stone circle remains an active site for seasonal ceremonies, particularly those tied to the river, weather, and the changing seasons. Gruno Grabner helps maintain the circle, and many locals still leave small offerings of water, ash, earth, or sand at the stones or in the bowls beside graves.

Faith in Ulgrey is pragmatic rather than fervent. Most residents honor both the old elemental ways and the newer worship of Cembus, seeing them as complementary rather than conflicting. The village’s spiritual life is closely tied to its identity as a crossroads and river settlement — focused on safe passage, the changing seasons, and practical protection for those who live and travel here.

## Dockhouse

Orwin Jasce, once a vigorous man, is now quite old but still runs the Dockhouse with the help of his young granddaughter, Loravie. The building no longer functions as the village’s general store. It has been converted into a working fishery and river waystation.

Fishermen sell their catch directly from the dock, and Orwin manages the rental and sale of boats for those wishing to travel downriver. The Dockhouse serves as the main hub for all river-based commerce and transportation in Ulgrey.

## Ferebert Mill

The mill’s greatest secret and source of its reputation is its main grindstone, which was carved decades ago from one of the original standing stones of Torec’s Circle with the blessing of Paeris Iya’ren. Many locals believe this gives the stone a minor elemental blessing.

This enchanted millstone produces exceptionally fine, golden-hued flour known as “Ferebert’s Gold,” which commands a premium price from bakers and merchants in Emberstran and Trailpoint. When grain is scarce, Ferebert engages a secondary saw blade powered by the same waterwheel to process lumber for the village and passing traders.

## 

## Up the river on the northeastern edge of Ulgrey stands Ferebert’s Mill, a large wooden building with a constantly turning waterwheel. The mill is known throughout the region for producing “Ferebert’s Gold,” a fine, slightly golden flour that many claim makes the best bread and pastries in the area. The rhythmic sound of the millstone and rushing water can be heard from a good distance away.

## Grabner’s Smithy

Agnes Grabner has run the smithy for over forty years and remains a skilled and respected craftswoman. Roughly twelve years ago she sent her son Gruno north to train with the dwarves of Dunduar. He returned two years ago with advanced dwarven techniques in both forging and metalworking. Together they now produce some of the finest metalwork in the region, ranging from everyday tools and hardware to quality weapons and armor.

Gruno is expected to fully take over the smithy within the next few years as his mother begins to slow down.

## 

## A short walk north of the central crossroads stands Grabner’s Smithy, easily recognized by the rhythmic ring of hammer on anvil and the glow of the forge visible through the wide doors. The smithy is a sturdy, practical building with a large open workspace and a smaller attached home. A variety of finished tools, horseshoes, and simple weapons are displayed neatly on racks outside. The air smells of hot iron, charcoal, and scorched earth.

## Graveyard

The Ulgrey Graveyard sits on the northeastern side of the village along the riverbank. Many of the graves feature Torec’s Vigil Stones — small bowls or recesses where locals leave offerings of water, ash, earth, or sand to ask the elemental god to watch over their ancestors.

The site is also known for The Mistwalk. Thick fog regularly rolls in from the river, especially during dawn and dusk. During these misty periods, faint glowing elemental wisps can occasionally be seen drifting between the graves.

## 

## On the northeastern edge of Ulgrey, lies the village graveyard. Weathered headstones and monuments dot the gentle slope, many of them carved with elemental symbols sacred to Torec. Small stone bowls can be seen beside many graves, often holding offerings of water, ash, or sand. The grounds are frequently shrouded in river mist, giving the cemetery a haunting but peaceful atmosphere.

## Paeris’ Rise (Tower)

This tower is the second oldest building in Ulgrey, constructed shortly after the Riverdog Inn. Paeris Iya’ren, a moon elf wizard with a deep affinity for elemental magic — especially lightning — has lived here for well over a century. She was present during the founding years of the village and personally assisted in the raising and consecration of Torec’s Circle, drawn by the god’s dominion over the elemental forces.

Paeris tends to keep to herself, but she is a respected figure in Ulgrey. Her tower contains a modest library and laboratory where she continues her arcane studies.

## 

## Standing near the Riverdog Inn at the southern end of the village is a narrow three-story stone tower known locally as Paeris’ Rise. A permanent magical light glows from the iron wind vane at its peak, serving as a beacon for travelers approaching Ulgrey after dark. The tower is one of the oldest structures in the village, its weathered stones covered in ivy.

The Riverdog Inn is the true heart and oldest structure of Ulgrey. Originally a small dock house and tavern, it has been expanded many times as the village grew. The patchwork of different wood types and architectural styles tells the story of the village’s development.  
For as long as anyone can remember, the owners of the inn have always kept a dog that freely roams the premises and greets guests arriving from the river or the road. The current owners, Harrek and Shanna Vellor, continue this tradition.

## Torec’s Circle

Torec’s Circle is one of the oldest and most spiritually significant locations in Ulgrey. The monumental stones were raised by the village’s original settlers when they turned from the Hooded Lady (Phoe) to the worship of the elemental god Torec. Paeris Iya’ren, the moon elf wizard, was directly involved in the raising and consecration of the circle.

The site remains an active place of worship, especially during seasonal ceremonies honoring the elements. One of the original stones was later carved into the main grindstone for Ferebert’s Mill with Paeris’s blessing. The circle continues to be carefully maintained, most notably by Gruno Grabner, who has taken a personal interest in its upkeep.

## 

## On a small rise to the north of the village stands Torec’s Circle, a ring of ancient standing stones etched with elemental symbols. The site has a quiet, reverent feeling and is often visited by locals and travelers alike. The stones are large, weathered, and clearly very old, with faint carvings of flames, waves, mountains, and swirling winds still visible on their surfaces.

## Ulgrey Oratory

Originally constructed in 1220 as the Church of Neera, this solid stone building was designed in the protective, fortress-like style favored by followers of the goddess of protection. After the Awakening in 1227, when Neera ceased answering prayers, the town’s cleric Zyre Wiscard abandoned Ulgrey in search of answers. The church stood empty for five years.

In 1232, Mother Velna Isenbell, a Seeker of the Advents of Harmony devoted to Cembus (god of Exploration and Travel), arrived in the village. She convinced the townsfolk to allow her to take over the abandoned building and rededicate it to Cembus. While she preserved the strong, protective stone exterior, she significantly altered the interior to better serve travelers and the worship of a god of the road.

Mother Isenbell added the Wayfarer’s Gallery, covering large portions of the interior walls with detailed painted maps of the surrounding region. She also opened one entire side of the building, creating the Roadwardens’ Cloister — a bright, open-air space with long tables and benches where weary travelers can rest, eat, and exchange news from the four roads. The building now functions less as a traditional temple and more as a sanctuary and gathering place for those who travel through Ulgrey.

## 

## Built of heavy stone in a strong, protective style, the Ulgrey Oratory still carries the solid presence of its original purpose as a church dedicated to Neera. Inside, however, the space feels more open and welcoming than a traditional temple. Large sections of the walls are covered with painted maps of the surrounding lands, and one entire side of the building can be opened to a bright, airy cloister where travelers often rest and share stories.
