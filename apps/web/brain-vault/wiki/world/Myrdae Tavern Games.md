## Tavern Games

### **Bones**

  - Each player requires several six-sided dice.
  - Each player puts the agreed ante in the pot.
  - Each player rolls three (3) dice. 
  - Play then proceeds clockwise around the table, with the host of the game going last.
  - On their turn, a player can choose to “stand” or “roll.” 
      
      - Stand: If the player stands, the next player can take a turn. 
      - Roll:  A player who rolls takes an additional die and rolls it. 
      - Bust:  If the total of their dice exceeds 21, they “bust” and are out of the game. Otherwise they can keep rolling additional dice until they either stand or break.
  - After everyone has had a turn, the highest point total (excluding players who busted) wins the game and takes the pot.

### **Eye’s Out**

  - Each player starts with four (4) six-sided dice. 
  - Each player puts the agreed ante in the pot.
  - Each player rolls all the dice. 
  - All ones (1) that are rolled are discarded.
  - Each player that wants to stay in must add another ante.
  - The last person with dice remaining wins the pot.

### **Three Pikes**

  - Each player starts with five (5) six-sided dice. 
  - Each player puts the agreed ante in the pot.
  - Each player rolls three (3) dice and keeps the rolls secret. 
  - Play then proceeds clockwise around the table to bet, check or fold.
      
      - Bet: The player raises the pot by the ante. 
      - Call:  The player must match the bets.
      - Fold:  The player loses their ante and is out of the game.
  - All players still in the game roll an additional die, keeping the roll a secret.
  - Play then proceeds clockwise around the table to bet, check or fold.
  - All players still in the game roll their final die; again, keeping the roll secret.
  - Play then proceeds clockwise around the table to bet, check or fold.
  - Player with the best hand wins the pot determined by the following:
      
      - Five of a kind
      - Four of a kind
      - Full house (three of one, two of another)
      - Straight (five in a row)
      - Three of a kind
      - Two pair (two of one, two of another)
      - One pair
      - High number

### **Life and Death**

[Rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/kftgv/the-stygian-gambit#LifeandDeath) from *Keys from the Golden Vault*

Life and death is a dice game played between the house (represented by a dealer) and a player. While up to five players can sit at a table, their only opponent is the house.

To play:

  - Each player places a bet
  - Once bets have been placed, the house and the players each roll a d20. 
      
      - If a player rolls lower than the dealer, the house wins. 
      - A player who rolls higher than the dealer reclaims the money they bet and wins that same amount from the house.
  - War. A player who ties with the house has two options: 
      
      - the player can surrender and lose half their bet, or 
      - the player can “go to war,” in which case the player must double their bet, and then the player and dealer both roll again.
  - Push Your Luck. When a player wins a roll of life and death, they can opt to push their luck on their next bet. In this case, they bet everything they won on the last roll (their ante plus the winnings from the house). If they win, the house pays double their bet.

### **Three-Dragon Ante**

[Rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/kftgv/the-stygian-gambit#ThreeDragonAnte) from *Keys from the Golden Vault*

Three-dragon ante is a card game. If you own an actual three-dragon ante deck, you can play the card game for real. Otherwise, adjudicate the outcome of a three-dragon ante game using d12s and the following rules:

  - Step 1. Randomly determine which participant is the dealer.
  - Step 2. Each participant (including the dealer) places their opening bet, then rolls five d12s to determine their hand, keeping these die rolls hidden from the other participants.
  - Step 3. Starting to the left of the dealer and continuing clockwise, each participant reveals one of their die rolls. This step is repeated twice more; on the second and third round, each participant can raise their bet before revealing their next die roll. The other participants each have three options: match the bet, raise their bet (in which case all participants must match that bet in turn), or fold, forfeiting any bet they’ve placed and dropping out of the game.
  - Step 4. Each participant who hasn’t folded totals their die rolls. The one with the highest total wins. The winner becomes the dealer for the next game (assuming the participant wants to keep playing).

### **Arm Wrestle**

  - This is a Strength challenge. 
  - You need three (3) steps to wrestle their arm to the table.
  - You make a contested strength check.
      
      - Success: you move one (1) step closer to victory.
      - Success (natural 20):  you move two (2) steps closer to victory.
      - Fail:  you lose one (1) step.

### **Dagger Juggling**

  - This is a Dexterity challenge. 
  - Bet is made on success, which can be done by number of rounds or by betting on each check.
  - Roll a DC check based on the number of daggers you are juggling:
      
      - DC10: for 2 daggers
      - DC15: for 4 daggers
      - DC20: for 4 daggers
  - A failed throw caused 1d4 non-lethal damage.
