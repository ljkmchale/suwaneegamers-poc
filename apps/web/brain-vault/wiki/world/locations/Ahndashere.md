# Ahndashere

## Scope

World lore - canonical geography of [[Myrdae]]. Campaign party experiences belong in campaign-specific location pages.

## Overview

Ahndashere is a modest coastal town in [[Territories of Myrdae|Baltwood]], where the [[Northern Stoneflow]] empties into the northern coast of the [[Glimmering Sea]]. It began as a marsh village before the Awakening and grew into a practical river-and-sea settlement after the Glimmering Sea expanded, deepened the marshes, and forced people to rebuild on stilts, boardwalks, and the dry green hills at the town's heart.

The town is defined by two linked landscapes: brackish lowlands of stilted houses, rope bridges, reed-choked channels, and working docks, and central hill neighborhoods of halfling burrows, gardens, chimneys, and the main road. Reed cutters, crabbers, salt workers, pilots, sailors, smugglers, fortune-seekers, and hillstead halflings all share the settlement. Ahndashere is smaller and rougher than the great Glimmering Sea cities, but its lantern-lit causeways and marsh traditions make it a durable local hub.

## History

Before the Awakening, Ahndashere clustered around a stone shrine to Torec, the old elemental god. The villagers believed the meeting of river, swamp, and sea was one of Torec's chosen convergences and relied on that blessing against storms, floods, and swamp spirits.

When the Glimmering Sea surged inland during the Awakening, the shoreline swallowed much of the old village. The central hills remained above the floodwaters, so the survivors rebuilt the low settlement on stilts and boardwalks while many families moved into hill burrows and gardens. Refugees from land lost to the Glimmering Sea also settled here, turning the old marsh village into a small but enduring town.

## Citizenry and Traditions

Ahndashere's people are a blend of marsh folk, riverfolk, seafarers, and hillstead halflings. The boardwalk and stilt communities tend toward caution, pragmatism, and suspicion of outsiders, but honest visitors who work hard can earn real welcome. The halflings of the central hills give the settlement stability through gardens, burrows, communal meals, and hospitality.

The town's traditions are survival-minded rather than grand. Halfling households observe **Harvest Rest** each autumn with shared meals of roasted root vegetables, smoked eel, and spiced marsh-carrot bread. Boatmen toss a pebble into the Stoneflow before setting out, a custom traced to Torec's old shrine. Traders hang ribbon charms on carts for Osanna's favor, and when storms rise all lanterns in town are lit together as a sign that the hills and water stand as one community.

## Government

Ahndashere is led by a Lord or Lady appointed every six years by the settlement elder, not by bloodline or popular vote. Since the Awakening, the elder has been **Myranda Fenberry**, a halfling shaman treated as the town's spiritual anchor and oracle.

Four years before the source's current frame, Myranda appointed **Lord Hommof Tsuldor**, a young fisherman with little political experience. Hommof governs through older practical laws first codified by the elder council, leans heavily on Myranda's counsel, and maintains order through a small guard force.

The guard is led by **Captain Hester Rinby**, a sturdy middle-aged officer known for practical judgment, warnings before punishment in minor cases, and steady loyalty to the community.

## Laws

Ahndashere preserves law as remembered sayings rather than thick written codes:

- Keep the water clean and the hills green.
- The elder's appointment of a Lord or Lady is not disputed.
- The road must stay clear and the docks must stay open.
- When danger comes, no one stands alone.
- Deal honestly in coin, catch, craft, and measure.
- Bloodshed must have cause, never spite.
- Honor the dead and do not plunder graves, shrines, or drowned relics.

Punishments are practical: fines, labor, exile, or, in the gravest cases, death. When ordinary judgment fails, townsfolk still turn to Myranda Fenberry.

## Economy and Trade

Ahndashere's economy rests on swamp, sea, hill, and road. Marsh trades include eel traps, crab pots, reed cutting, peat digging, and salt-boiling. The hill gardens provide herbs, root vegetables, small grain plots, peppery watercress, and sweet marsh carrots that travel well.

The Northern Stoneflow and the coastal road make Ahndashere a minor but useful trade stop. River pilots ferry goods upstream, coastal traders resupply at the docks, and smugglers use the twisting channels to move contraband under cover of honest catch.

## Religion

The town's oldest sacred site is the weathered Shrine of Torec on the hill rise, a ring of stone markers once tended by priests of the old elemental god. Nobody actively worships Torec in the present source frame, but the shrine remains a place of quiet reflection and local memory.

New Order worship is scattered through small shrines and altars. **Coralei** is honored by sailors, pilots, and fisherfolk; **Osanna** by merchants and travelers; **Villari** by hill gardeners and those seeking growth; and **Muerg** by marsh-dwellers seeking strength against flood, storm, and mire.
