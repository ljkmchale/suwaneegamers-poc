## Cross-Campaign Pattern: Defiled Hearts And Corruption

All three campaigns deal with corrupted, removed, or spiritually compromised hearts — but each campaign approaches the theme differently:

- **SoD**: The Cult of the Immured literally removes and petrifies human hearts using defiled black salt. The ritual uses souls as fiendish fuel. The corruption is mechanical, deliberate, and tied to a network.
- **HoE**: The Diverra faith's Shattered Hearts doctrine concerns hearts displaced from their sacred context. The Blackrend blight and the Marrowstone both carry necrotic / dark-power overtones. Aurelius's Bloom Aeterna may instruct how to restore shattered hearts.
- **Silent Vanguard**: No heart corruption thread yet documented, but the zombie fish outbreak in Brinecross and the trafficking operation have an underlying theme of people being used as resources.

These three corruptions do not share a source — they are separate storylines — but the thematic overlap is notable when answering questions about what the DM is doing at a world-building level.

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: Fiendish Or Supernatural Entities Using People

- **SoD**: Aeolenne exploits Zephyra's fiendish heritage to possess her. The cult freed Aeolenne as their end goal.
- **HoE**: Lady Rook uses mortals (Hap's inheritance, Petal as a spy, Ky'tha's trust) as tools for her vault agenda. Her goals may not be fiendish in origin, but her method is manipulation.
- **Silent Vanguard**: The trafficking operation literally moves people as cargo into the colosseum network.

Across all three campaigns, the party is placed between powerful entities that exploit or trade in people, and the question of who deserves trust is always live.

---

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: Legitimacy And False Authority

Each campaign has a civic or religious authority structure that turns out to be compromised or partial:

- **SoD**: The Adsuren Center is the town's civic hub — and contains the cult's base beneath it. The Intendant himself is Summoner Karranok.
- **HoE**: The EGD's legitimacy can be forged. The Cloak uses fake inspectors and assassins. The Vintner's Guild is embroiled in murder. The Diverra temple Aurelius knows may be teaching an incomplete version of the faith.
- **Silent Vanguard**: The Gilded Ledger and Salt and Cinder Press suggest the Tratta press and financial institutions are corrupt. The colosseum operates as a legitimate venue while trafficking people.

## Player Habit Profiles

Patterns drawn only from documented play of actual characters. Character suggestions in source notes ("X should play Y") are brainstorming — not evidence of playing habits. Players with only one documented campaign are marked; their patterns are tentative until more data exists.

### Lesley Poole — Ky'tha (HoE), Esylla (SoD)

**Social and stealth tools over direct force.** Ky'tha scouts, searches, and reads physical clues before the group commits. Esylla probes scenes socially with charm and pointed questions before acting. Neither character leads with weapons.

**Maintains outside contact networks.** Ky'tha has Tessa and an underworld network. Esylla has Mollywop and her aunt via Sending. Both position themselves as the party's bridge to outside sources of information.

**Tracks hidden or occult threads independently.** Ky'tha follows the Dunduar/Muerg vision and the Cragfury sealed-ring mystery as a private thread. Esylla tracks the Caldwynn watermark and shipping network behind the black salt on her own.

**Charlatan or thief-adjacent framing.** Thief subclass rogue. Charlatan background warlock. The "working an angle" posture is consistent across both characters even though they approach it differently.

**Non-standard species with distinct cultural weight.** Dwarf rogue who operates in elven and underworld spaces (Ky'tha). High elf warlock with refined noble sensibility and an outside magical contact (Esylla). Both choices carry cultural specificity that shapes how the character reads other people.

### Sean Poole — Ainslie (HoE)

*One campaign documented. Patterns are tentative.*

**Uses identity as a tool and commits hard to it.** Ainslie presents as official observer, EGD authority, noble bloodline, or intimidating arcane figure depending on what the scene needs. Once he commits to a story, he holds it even when it becomes morally messy.

**Drawn to inherited secrets and ancestral power.** The Journal of Tsulanan, Irragosa, Tsulanan's bloodline, and the vault are all personal to Ainslie in ways that go beyond the party mission. He gravitates toward mysteries that implicate his origins.

**Uses magic for conversation control rather than raw damage.** Sending, Detect Thoughts, telepathy, illusions, Haste on allies, and telekinetic shoves are his primary toolkit. He coordinates through manipulation and enhancement more than direct firepower.

**Social operator who can turn morally dark under pressure.** Ainslie uses charm and authority comfortably, but he also intimidates, reads minds without consent, and helps dispose of Petal's body. He will do what the situation demands and justify it through framing.

### Ty Cooper — Hap (HoE)

*One campaign documented. Patterns are tentative.*

**Unexpected warmth and rural plainspokenness for a frightening-looking character.** Hap is a tiefling who reads as genuinely good-hearted, self-described as pure, and motivated by grief and faith rather than ambition. The gap between appearance and personality is part of the character concept.

**Driven by a personal grief mission alongside the party goal.** Hap's father's murder, the stolen ring, the Cloak of Defiance, and Diverra's vision are personal stakes running parallel to HoE's main thread. He never loses sight of them.

**Uses a familiar or animal companion as a key partner.** Fleur is central — for scouting, spell delivery, and emotional weight. Fleur is even sacrificed temporarily in a dangerous fight. The companion relationship is not incidental; it shapes Hap's tactics and identity.

**Protects allies directly and physically.** Battle Master tactics, deflecting damage meant for others, and moving toward the biggest threat rather than away from it. Protection is the default posture.

### Emma Cooper — Zymve (HoE)

*One campaign documented. Joins late in HoE. Patterns are tentative.*

**Uses music and performance as primary social and interpretive tools.** Zymve connects, inspires, comforts, intimidates, and dominates rooms through bardic performance. She also interprets magical mystery — the Journal of Tsulanan — through melody and emotional meaning rather than logical analysis.

**Says the brutal thing others will not.** Zymve cuts through social comfort when she decides something needs to be said or done. She is nurturing and ruthless in the same arc: she repairs Brander's confidence and later stomps a defeated enemy's head.

**Removes persistent threats permanently rather than negotiating further.** Petal tracking the party is a concrete example. Once Zymve decides someone will not stop, she ends it.

**Carries pre-campaign trauma that shapes how she reads danger.** Fleeing an Underdark cult is not backstory flavor — it drives her distrust of control, her reaction to the Journal's pull, and her friendship terms with the party.

### Brian Winniford — Escanor (SoD), Cletus (Silent Vanguard)

*Cletus has no scene-level documentation yet. Patterns are primarily from Escanor.*

**Plays human clerics in both documented campaigns.** This is a consistent build preference — same class, same species across SoD and Silent Vanguard. The stats are also nearly identical (AC 13/15, Passive Perception 15, Passive Insight 15, languages Common/Draconic/Giant). Brian builds a recognizable archetype and inhabits it deeply rather than varying wildly.

**Moral anchor and civic stabilizer.** Escanor moves toward responsibility immediately when people are hurt, frightened, or in moral danger. He asks what is right and spiritually clean before asking what is profitable or practical.

**Uses religious and civic authority as stabilizing tools.** Connects the party to local priests, guard captains, and civic structures. Brings cult evidence to official hands. Cleans up after the party's chaos rather than adding to it.

**Conscience of the group without being preachy.** Escanor notices when the party is about to do something wrong, says so, and then keeps working the mission anyway. He does not refuse to function; he corrects and moves on.

### Tiffany — Lila (SoD)

*One campaign documented. Patterns are tentative.*

**Glamorous aesthetic paired with support-first combat role.** Crystals, jewels, a sparkling presence — and then her mechanical role is keeping allies alive rather than seeking personal glory. The beauty is part of the character identity; the support is how she plays.

**Stays at safe distance and prioritizes party survival.** Temporary hit points, healing, and burst damage from range. She moves away from danger rather than into it, but she picks the right moment to spike damage when it matters.

**Has prophetic or visionary abilities that give early warning.** Crystal visions of a grave, a red ritual room, Kenton with a starry sword, and a forest shadow/roar appear before the party has hard proof. Lila's value is partly in pattern-matching between symbolic vision and current clue.

**Emotionally direct and willing to name the problem.** She is one of the characters most willing to challenge Kenton openly about his theft and lies. Support-first does not mean passive.

### Jenny McHale — Zephyra (SoD)

*One campaign documented. Patterns are tentative.*

**Moves toward trouble to protect the vulnerable.** Zephyra intervenes physically and protectively as a default, even when she is not perfectly suited for formal authority. She acts like a protector before she is officially recognized as one.

**Carries a deep emotional thread around identity and being treated as monstrous.** Her tiefling heritage, the "devil spawn" memories, and Aeolenne's claim all push on who Zephyra is allowed to be. The emotional stakes are personal, not tactical.

**Faces her worst fear directly rather than avoiding it.** When Aeolenne takes control, Zephyra breaks it through memory and identity — she reaches back to childhood shame to pull herself out. Her inner strength is the mechanic, not a die roll.

**Front-line and rage-forward but with a community anchor.** She is tied to Adsuren's guard structure, the village watch, and local civic life. She is not just a barbarian; she is a recognizable community member who hits things hard.

### Joshua John — Og (HoE)

*One campaign documented. Patterns are tentative.*

**Party-first tank who does not need a personal spotlight.** Og moves toward the biggest threat, holds the line, and creates space for others to act. He does not compete for narrative attention — he makes the action possible for others.

**Immediate and direct.** Moves first into physical danger, tests evidence physically, and responds to problems with action rather than analysis.

**Develops unexpected spiritual depth over time.** Og starts as a reliable goliath tank with no documented personal quest. By Session 24 he is the Life Tree's E'erspan, thorn-bearer, with a deepening bond to Thornleaf and a personal thread that the early sessions did not hint at. The archetype opens up rather than staying flat.

**Trusted and loyal as a party constant.** Ainslie uses Og as the example of friendship when explaining trust to Zymve. Ky'tha and Og have a longstanding field bond. He is the quiet center other characters orbit around.

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: Chip Poole's Playing Habits

Chip plays Therric (SoD — Ranger/Monk, Half-orc), Lucerion (Bloody Endeavor — Paladin of Natafae, Elf), and Draelith (Dungeons III — Wizard, Human). These are the only characters he has played. Patterns are drawn only from documented play of these three.

**Strong, personal divine devotion.** Each character has a spiritual or cosmic identity that goes beyond class mechanics. Therric holds reverence for multiple nature deities and is genuinely frightened by hellish signs. Lucerion is an ardent follower of Natafae (the Mistress of Pain) who believes suffering is transformative and cannot die. Draelith is Celestine-devoted — he explains his faith through a harp-shaped birthmark and uses illusion magic as an extension of starcraft and stagecraft. The divine connection is never casual; it shapes how the character reads the world.

**Unusual class builds or character concepts.** Therric is a Ranger/Monk hybrid playing as a rugged wilderness skirmisher. Lucerion is a Glory Paladin who worships pain and actively courts being hurt. Draelith is a Wizard who leans into illusion and theatrical performance rather than raw power. Chip consistently picks something that has a distinctive mechanical or tonal angle rather than a straightforward archetype.

**Sensory and environmental engagement.** Therric reads tracks, smell, taste, water, soil, and dreams before trusting social explanations. Lucerion processes danger through physical sensation — he courts harm as spiritual experience. Draelith uses Minor Illusion and Prestidigitation constantly for misdirection and stagecraft. Each character engages with the world through a distinctive sensory or perceptual lens.

**An independent investigative streak.** Therric scouts ahead and works environmental clues before the group commits. Lucerion pursues his own leads (Nurador, the red arm's meaning, Natafae's plan) alongside party goals. Draelith chases his own research threads — the Osmanae instrument, Umlin's mapping job, the sending stone. Chip's characters always have a personal angle running in parallel with the group mission.

**A personal mystical thread that is not yet resolved.** Therric has recurring dreams about a clawed creature and a female elf-like quarry. Lucerion has a supernatural red arm given in a dream after losing his original arm to a hag. Draelith has a harp birthmark and a connection to an ancient observatory. Each character carries an unresolved personal mystery with a spiritual or supernatural dimension.

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: The Party Contains The DM's Character

Larry/Lawrence McHale plays Aurelius (HoE), Kenton (SoD), and Jett Blackwood (Silent Vanguard). Across all three:

- **Aurelius**: The moral and spiritual anchor. He heals, raises the dead, and challenges the party when they go too far.
- **Kenton**: The chaos variable. He steals, lies, kills prisoners, and keeps the party honest about their own compromises by constantly making new ones.
- **Jett**: The physical stabilizer. He holds doors, solves equipment problems, and grounds the party in direct action.

The three characters cover completely different roles and ethical postures, suggesting intentional range.

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: The Party Leaves Unresolved Survivors

Each campaign has abandoned people the party could return to:

- **SoD**: Brinecross survivors (Nora, Linda) and ongoing Brinecross outbreak. Mollywop, Lobella, and the Sapcutt aftermath.
- **HoE**: Arnol's strange behavior is unresolved. The Blackrend may still be spreading. The zombie ship / vanished crew from Session 01 was never fully explained.
- **Silent Vanguard**: Harold is sick and left behind in Brinecross. The Brinecross outbreak itself is unresolved.

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: The Vault / Secret With A Power That Wants It

Each campaign features a powerful entity whose real goal is something hidden or locked:

- **SoD**: Aeolenne was imprisoned inside the cult's ritual mechanism. The entire Cult of the Immured, Eyovar's 20-year bargain, and the escalating sacrifices were all, unknowingly to Eyovar, building toward her freedom. The party freed her by dismantling the ritual.
- **HoE**: Lady Rook (Bivra Rook; source variants include Bidva/Bodiva) has organized her entire Emberstran operation — the Marrowstone, Petal, the underdark supply chain, the hostage leverage — around gaining access to the vault beneath Mount Emberstran. She is not the only one: Ainslie's bloodline, Irragosa, and the Cloak of Defiance all have competing vault interests.
- **Silent Vanguard**: The trafficking network in Tratta operates behind legitimate institutions (broadsheets, the colosseum) toward an endpoint the party has not yet fully reached.

In each case the "secret" was not created by the party but was there before them. The question the DM keeps asking: will the party open the lock on purpose, or be tricked into opening it?

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: The Hidden Parent / Heritage Reveal

Each campaign uses a player character's heritage as a live plot mechanism rather than backstory:

- **SoD**: Zephyra's tiefling heritage has a source — Aeolenne, a succubus-type fiend who claims to be her mother (or parent; she uses both gendered terms). Zephyra believed her parents were both human. The heritage is now weaponized as a possession vector.
- **HoE**: Ainslie's sorcerous bloodline traces to Tsulanan, a dragonrider-era figure, through Irragosa's recognition and the Journal of Tsulanan's behavior. Hap's family tragedy (Benoit's murder, falsified records, the "G" medallion) is the Garemon heritage being systematically erased by the Cloak.
- **Silent Vanguard**: No heritage reveal documented yet, but three sessions is early.

The pattern: the character thought their origins were settled. The campaign is built to show they were not.

---

## Cross-Campaign Pattern: The Trusted Insider Who Was Complicit

Each campaign has a figure who appeared to be on the party's side but was already compromised:

- **SoD**: Eyovar Dunajor — the town's civic Intendant, trusted public figure, and the party's administrative superior — was running the cult beneath his own office for 20 years.
- **HoE**: Arnol is not malicious, but he was recruited into the Eradic of Ghone by Ainslie and remained entangled long after the party knew. Jae Vynaar falsified records under pressure. The EGD was signed by the Black Quill, not the Lord — the party's official legitimacy was never what it appeared.
- **Silent Vanguard**: The broadsheet / financial institutions in Tratta (Gilded Ledger, Salt and Cinder Press) suggest civic legitimacy is a front.

The party consistently builds trust with institutions or individuals who turn out to have been compromised before they arrived.
