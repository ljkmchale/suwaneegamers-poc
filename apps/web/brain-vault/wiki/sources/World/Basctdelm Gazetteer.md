	About Basctdelm
Basctdelm rises like a stone crown from the eastern edge of Lake Tribathe, the fortified capital of the Bathaen Empire and seat of Queen Breya Talward. Built on a massive island and ringed by water on nearly all sides, the city is deliberately isolated—connected to the mainland only by two heavily guarded bridges.. From the bustling Trade District at the mouth of the Dibaryn River, where ships from across Myrdae crowd the docks, the city climbs through layered districts toward the High District perched on the northwestern heights. Stone walls, towers, and imperial banners dominate the skyline, while the ever-present patrols of the Basctdelm Watch (and occasional griffon rider overhead) remind visitors that order here is not just tradition—it is law. To the approaching traveler, Basctdelm feels less like a port and more like a watchful stronghold that has seen empires rise and fracture, yet still stands.
The People
The people of Basctdelm are as layered as the city itself. Nearly two million souls live in and around the capital, creating one of the most diverse populations in Myrdae—almost any ancestry can be found walking its streets. In the Trade District and along the waterfront, merchants, sailors, dockworkers, and travelers from distant lands haggle in a dozen tongues. Higher districts host imperial officials, noble houses of the old Compact Lineages (and their lingering rivalries), scholars attached to the Queen’s Academy or Braethyn’s Archives, and the disciplined ranks of the Basctdelm Watch.  Life here blends ambition with a deep awareness of the crown’s gaze; everyone knows their role in keeping the empire’s heart beating steadily.
Beliefs
Basctdelm’s culture prizes order, vigilance, and loyalty to the crown above almost all else. Strict laws are enforced openly by the blue-and-gold Basctdelm Watch—whose griffon-mounted patrols sweep the skies—and more quietly by the plainclothes Weavebinders who police the misuse of magic. Justice is swift and public.  The city welcomes shrines and temples to all the gods. Daily life carries a sense of solemn duty mixed with quiet pride in living at the empire’s center. Old noble rivalries and the memory of House Talward’s rise still simmer beneath the orderly surface, but most citizens focus on their place in the Bathaen capital.
Economy and Trade
Basctdelm functions as the empire’s tightly regulated commercial heart rather than a chaotic free port. Nearly all major imports and exports move through the Trade District at the confluence of the Dibaryn River and Lake Tribathe, flowing onward to the Broken Deep. Imperial customs officers and professional guilds keep a firm hand on the flow of goods, ensuring that commerce serves the crown’s stability as much as profit. Merchants from across Myrdae still gather here in force, and the city offers access to nearly every good or service a large capital could want—from armories and arcane shops to bustling inns and specialty crafters. Trade feels deliberate and observed, reinforcing Basctdelm’s role as the steady hand guiding the Bathaen Empire’s prosperity.
Holidays and Traditions
Basctdelm has many celebrations throughout the year, but the primary one observed by nearly all citizens is Capital Day, marking the date in 373 when Basctdelm became the capital of the Bathaen Empire. On the 15th of Paramor, the city honors its elevation as the empire’s seat of order and authority with grand parades of the Basctdelm Watch and griffon riders, public addresses from the High District, and extended markets and feasts throughout the Trade District. Citizens and visitors alike reaffirm their loyalty to Queen Breya and the enduring stability of the realm, turning the day into a powerful expression of unity and imperial pride.

The High Seat of Mistridge
Basctdelm is the capital of Halesworth and the seat of imperial authority for the Bathaen Empire, rising from a massive island anchored along the eastern edge of Lake Tribathe. Securely separated from the mainland by water on all sides save two controlled crossings, the city was shaped first for defense and governance, and only secondarily for trade. Its isolation is intentional—Basctdelm is not a crossroads, but a stronghold.
The city ascends gradually from east to northwest, its districts stacked both physically and symbolically. At the water’s edge lies the Trade District, built directly at the mouth of the Dibaryn River, where the river meets Lake Tribathe before flowing eastward to the Broken Deep. Nearly all imports, exports, and visitors arrive here by ship, making the district the city’s most diverse and active quarter. Warehouses, docks, customs halls, and merchant enclaves dominate the shoreline, while imperial oversight ensures that commerce entering Basctdelm is carefully regulated.
From the Trade District, the city rises inward through the Lower District and onward to the High District, which crowns the island’s northwestern heights. This upper reach—what travelers see when approaching from the west across Lake Tribathe—is a formidable silhouette of stone walls, towers, and elevated keeps. The High District houses the empire’s ruling institutions, noble estates, and centers of law, its elevation reinforcing the authority and permanence of the Bathaen crown.
Basctdelm is connected to the mainland by only two major roads, each tightly controlled. To the northeast, the Star Reach Bridge spans the water toward Wheldrake, serving as the city’s primary overland artery. To the southeast, the Historic Crossway Bridge carries traffic through the Lower District and into Halesworth. Together, these crossings ensure that movement into and out of the capital is deliberate, monitored, and defensible.
Approached from Lake Tribathe, Basctdelm appears less like a port city and more like a fortified citadel rising from the water—its High District looming above the lake, stonework catching the light while mist and waterfalls cling to the island’s cliffs. It is a city that projects stability, authority, and distance: a place where the empire watches the world, rather than the world passing freely through it.


Basctdelm appears as a vast, stone-built city of bridges, towers, and tiered districts, alive with banners bearing imperial colors and the steady rhythm of patrols and commerce. Markets bustle with merchants from across Myrdae, couriers hurry between ministries and halls, and the air carries the sense that every action is observed—or recorded. 
History of Basctdelm
Origin
Basctdelm was founded not as a center of commerce, but as a deliberate instrument of order. The city was established upon Mistridge Island, a massive, defensible landmass anchored along the eastern edge of Lake Tribathe. The island takes its name from the western ridge, whose heights slow the nightly mist rolling in from the lake and shield the eastern docks and lower settlements. Its earliest purpose was to control movement between the Dibaryn River and the lake, adjudicate disputes among neighboring territories, and serve as a neutral seat of authority removed from local ambitions. The city’s isolation was intentional, its founders believing that distance from the mainland would insulate governance from factional influence and reduce the likelihood of internal revolt.
Long before the city rose, early human settlers made their homes on Mistridge Island. Among them was the Aladana family, who claimed the upper reaches of the overgrown western cliffs. It was there that they first encountered a family of wild griffons nesting among the crags. The Aladanas are credited with being the first to successfully tame these griffons. Their success laid the foundation for what would later become the Basctdelm Watch, whose aerial forces trace their lineage directly back to those original griffons. This bond between the island’s early inhabitants and the griffons became one of the first pillars of order and defense on Mistridge Island.
In its earliest centuries, Basctdelm was administered by appointed regents and custodial governors, often drawn from legal, military, or scholarly traditions. While effective, this structure bred resentment among surrounding lands, who viewed the city less as a protector and more as an imposed arbiter. This tension—between order imposed and order accepted—would define Basctdelm’s identity for generations to come.
Rising Lineages
As Basctdelm grew in population and influence, authority gradually shifted from appointed stewards to a coalition of powerful noble houses, commonly referred to as the Compact Lineages. Each lineage governed a vital civic function—defense, taxation, river traffic, or law—and together they maintained a fragile balance of power. Though this system prevented outright civil war, it fostered corruption, secret alliances, and quiet rivalries that often paralyzed decisive action.
The rise of House Talward marked a decisive turning point. Initially elevated as stabilizing stewards during a prolonged period of political deadlock, the Talwards consolidated authority by dismantling the Compact Lineages through legal reform, strategic marriages, and the quiet removal of dissenting voices. Though their ascension brought unity and consistency to Basctdelm’s rule, it also left behind unresolved grievances among the old houses—grievances that still linger beneath the city’s orderly surface.
Prudent Leadership
The foundation of the Bathaen Empire was laid under King Irion Talward, grandfather of Moldan Talward, whose reign transformed Basctdelm’s authority into lasting imperial structure. Rather than ruling through conquest alone, Irion secured the Empire’s longevity through diplomacy, most notably by forging a binding pact with the leaders of Palason and Qal’dynn. This agreement established shared responsibilities, mutual defense, and recognition of Basctdelm as the Empire’s keystone, allowing the Bathaen Empire—then composed of three principal territories—to endure through balance rather than domination.
That stability was shattered in the years surrounding the Awakening. Territorial borders fractured, some regions splintered away, and others were drawn into the Empire out of necessity or protection, rendering the old pact obsolete. Following the assassination of King Moldan Talward, Queen Breya inherited not only the throne but the impossible task of preserving unity amid upheaval. Operating at first under the fading framework of Irion’s pact, she ultimately oversaw the forging of a new imperial accord—one reshaped to reflect altered borders, new territories, and lingering mistrust. Her reign has since been defined by careful negotiation and restraint, rebuilding consensus where certainty once stood, and proving that the Bathaen Empire endures not by force of legacy alone, but by the resolve of its sovereign.

Crown of the High Seat
Queen Breya Talward ascended to the throne in 1218 after the sudden and tragic death of her husband, King Moldan Talward, who passed at the young age of 34. Since then, Queen Breya has guided the city through nearly three decades of leadership, becoming a revered figure in the kingdom. She is a mother to two children: her firstborn son, Alskar, who entered the world in 1208, and her daughter, Feyna, born in 1211.
Queen Breya Talward
Breya came to the High Seat from a dead king, with two young children, and an empire whose old pact was already coming apart. She has spent nearly thirty years putting it back together one careful conversation at a time. That work is still the person she is.
She listens before she answers. The room often thinks she is stern until they notice she is weighing them, not dismissing them. She remembers names, old grievances, and what a request will cost people who will never stand in Talward Keep. Flattery does not interest her. A clean purpose does. She has held too many audiences to be surprised, and too many fragile accords to spend the crown’s strength lightly.
The Compact houses and the newer territories are never far from her mind. She knows the High Seat was built by breaking other claims, and that Palason and Qal’dynn still have to choose this arrangement every year. When she gives help, it is because the thing asked of her keeps the empire and her people from cracking. When she refuses, it is rarely cruelty. 
Meeting her should feel like being received by someone who has already survived the worst day of the empire and chosen, every morning since, to keep it standing.


Queen Breya Talward is a tall and slender woman with sharp elegant features. Her brunette hair cascades down her back, though a striking silver streak woven through the left side catches the light. She wears a royal blue gown trimmed with gold and a thin ornate crown decorated with sapphires, embodying the primary colors of Basctdelm. Her piercing gaze suggests a stern demeanor, yet the warmth in her eyes reveals an approachable nature, one who listens intently and weighs her words carefully. 
Crownguard
The Crownguard are the Queen’s household troops, not a second city watch. Use them at Talward Keep, in the High District when the crown is moving, and anywhere a Talward is present. They control the keep doors, screen visitors, and take custody of anyone who threatens the Queen or the royal house. Ordinary street crime is handed to the Watch. They are disciplined, few, and hard to impress. A writ from a High Seat clerk, an introduction from someone the court already knows, or a genuine crisis will get a PC past them. Swagger will not. If they draw steel, the scene has become a High Seat matter.


The Crownguard stand in polished steel and deep blue cloaks, the Talward crown stitched in gold at the shoulder, visors up and eyes unblinking. They do not patrol like the Watch; they hold the door as if the keep itself were watching through them.
Basctdelm Watch
The Watch is the city’s everyday law. Use them in every surface district: street stops, tavern fights, brandishing, theft, and the first response to almost any public trouble. They staff the watch-houses, take charges, and move prisoners to a justiciar. Griffon riders are uncommon and should feel like a serious answer, not background traffic. The Watch is proud, visible, and used to armed travelers. Cause and courtesy keep a stop short. A writ, guild work, or clear purpose will usually suffice. Repeated noise, drawn steel without cause, or contempt turns a patrol into a charge. They do not police the Bellows and they do not override the Crownguard.


Watch officers move through the streets in royal-blue tabards trimmed with gold, the griffon of Basctdelm bright on the chest, some on foot and some on horseback. Now and then a griffon rider cuts the air above the rooftops, and the crowd parts without being told.
Basctdelm Weavebinders
Weavebinders police the misuse of magic. Use them when a spell is cast in public, a charm or compulsion is suspected, or a caster refuses registration. They do not walk beats in uniform. A Watch patrol usually fetches one, or one is already in the crowd. They investigate first and bind if they must. They file nothing themselves; the Watch still writes the charge and a justiciar still judges. A cooperative caster gets a warning, an edict, or a fine. A hostile one gets bound and held. They are not the Watch and not the Crownguard. If the crime is steel, leave it with the Watch. If the crime is the Weave, the Weavebinder owns the scene.


There is no badge that marks a Weavebinder at a glance: a scholar in plain robes, a merchant with ink-stained fingers, a laborer who does not quite belong to the work. Only when the Weave is mishandled do they step out of the crowd, calm and certain, as if they had been watching the whole time.
Offices of the High Seat
Player characters almost never begin with the Queen. The High Seat is close; the throne is not. Most business of the empire is done in halls, watch-houses, and dock offices by people who have heard every story before.
Petitions, writs, and complaints start with a High Seat clerk. Street trouble starts at a watch-house. Trouble at the river mouth starts with customs. Unlawful magic draws a Weavebinder. Large sums go to the Vault. The Crownguard keeps the keep doors. Justiciars are reached after a charge is filed, or when a clerk sets a hearing.

The Watch makes the arrest. A justiciar passes sentence. The Crownguard does not police taverns. Weavebinders do not replace the Watch — they take the case when the crime is the Weave.
Arrest and Judgement 
The following steps highlight the standard procedures for arresting those that violate the laws.
1. Stop or seizure
A Watch patrol may detain anyone they have cause to hold. Customs may detain over cargo and papers at the docks or bridges. A Weavebinder may detain for active or recent unlawful magic and usually calls the Watch to complete the arrest.
2. Charge
At the nearest watch-house, an officer records the charge against the city ordinances. No charge means no holding beyond short questioning. A guild Speaker may attend; that does not stop the charge.
3. Holding
Each district keeps a watch-house lockup. Overnight holding is common. Serious cases move to central holding under the High District. The Bellows has no official lockup. Anyone taken there is brought to the surface.
4. Judgment
* Minor offenses — peace, small theft, brandishing, dock fines: a district justiciar, often the same day or the next.
* Serious offenses — violence, burglary, forgery, major theft, assault on officials: a senior justiciar.
* Crown or imperial crimes — harm to the Queen or Talwards, impersonating Watch or Crownguard, treason, high magic against the state: the High Seat court. The Tend may hold the accused until then.
Judgment is public enough to be seen. It is not a long trial. Watch testimony and witnesses are usually enough.
5. Sentence
Punishments follow the ordinances outlined below: damages, fine, edict, flogging, imprisonment, hard labor, exile, or death. Unpaid fines become imprisonment or labor. Guild standing may argue for a fine instead of the lash. It does not erase a High Seat crime.
A typical adventurer case is one scene: stop, watch-house, charge, justiciar. Only High Seat crimes become a longer problem.
Special Cases
* Bellows. The Watch does not patrol the Bellows. It is a wild, dangerous reach beneath the High District where official law barely runs and almost anything goes. Raids happen only when the High Seat decides they must. Anyone dragged out of the Bellows is charged on the surface like any other prisoner.
* Magic. Weavebinders decide whether the Weave was misused and may bind the caster pending judgment. The Watch still files the charge.
* Docks and bridges. Customs seizes goods on the spot and turns people over to the Watch.
* High District and keep. The Crownguard seize people in the keep or in the Queen’s presence. Ordinary offenders go to the Watch. Anyone who threatened the crown stays with the Crownguard.
* Armed travelers. Tolerated with cause — Watch work, guild contract, or official writ. Brandishing without cause is still a charge.

Economy and Trade
At a Glance
Basctdelm is the tightly regulated commercial heart of the Bathaen Empire. Nearly all significant trade flows through the Trade District under heavy imperial customs and professional guild oversight, prioritizing crown stability over free-market chaos. The city offers the full range of goods and services expected of a major capital, but every major transaction is deliberate, observed, and ultimately serves imperial interests.
Key Drivers
The city’s economy is built from four key areas:
1. Harbor and Trade District traffic — Almost all major imports, exports, and bulk goods move through the docks and warehouses at the mouth of the Dibaryn River. This is the primary engine of visible commerce.
2. Imperial contracts and requisitions — Crown purchases, military supply, and official building projects inject steady, high-volume demand that favors reliable guild members and established merchants.
3. Professional guild services — Skilled labor, quality control, and specialized production (armoring, ship work, arcane goods, construction, etc.) generate significant ongoing income and set many of the city’s service rates.
4. Internal consumption — Feeding, clothing, housing, and entertaining nearly two million residents creates constant local demand that supports markets, inns, and everyday crafters even when long-distance trade slows.
Imports & Exports
Primary Imports:
* Grain, livestock, and preserved foods (to feed the capital)
* Timber, stone, and raw metals
* Luxury textiles, spices, and exotic goods
* Magical components and rare reagents
Primary Exports:
* Finished manufactured goods (armor, tools, ships, etc.)
* Processed goods and packaged commodities
* High-value specialized items produced with guild oversight
* Imperial administrative and bureaucratic services (indirect economic influence)
Currency & Pricing Notes
Standard coinage of the Bathaen Empire and common trade currencies of Myrdae are accepted without issue.  Imperial tariffs, docking fees, and customs duties apply to most bulk shipments entering the Trade District.  Guild-controlled services (especially skilled crafting, construction, and specialized labor) tend to run at the higher end of normal ranges for a city of this size. Everyday market goods remain reasonably priced due to constant internal demand.  The Vault of Basctdelm (North Ward) handles large-scale banking, gem exchange, and official financial services under Reserve network rules.
Guilds & Regulation
Professional guilds maintain quality standards and set rates for most skilled trades. They operate under clear imperial oversight rather than as independent powers. Customs officers and the Basctdelm Watch enforce trade regulations; the Weavebinders monitor magical commerce. Full details on individual guilds appear in the Professional Guilds section.
People of Basctdelm
Basctdelm has a population of almost 2,000,000 people.  Around three-fourths of these people live in the city proper that is surrounded by water, with the rest in surrounding neighborhoods and farm lands.  
City Areas
The city is recognizes the following areas as “districts”:
* High District: wealthy nobles, spacious estates, formal gardens, keep/castle, very orderly
* North District: upper-class homes, guild mansions, tidy streets, some shops and inns for the wealthy
* Central District: dense middle-class blocks, busiest commercial area, shops, inns, several plazas and market streets
* Trade District: major docks, warehouses, merchant housing, inns
* South District: dense working-class housing, tight streets, workshops, inns
* Lower District: poor crowded housing, rough waterfront, few inns, a scattering of docks
* Outer District: sparse, meadows, scattered structures
There are two other locales that are notable in Basctdelm:
* The Bellows – There is a large underground area residing beneath the High District that is where the black market networks of Basctdelm operate.
* The Outskirts – The area outside of the city proper is referred to as the Outskirts, which is made up of farms, orchards, suburban homes, and groves.
Species (Races)
Bastdelm is a very diverse city, where almost any ancestry can be seen.


Professional Guilds
Professional guilds in Basctdelm are recognized partners of the crown rather than independent powers. They maintain quality standards, regulate skilled trades, and set many service rates under clear imperial oversight. Customs officers, the Basctdelm Watch, and the Weavebinders ensure that guild activities support the empire’s stability and controlled commerce. Serious practitioners are expected to hold membership; guilds can make independent operation difficult through restricted supplies, withheld contracts, or formal complaints. 
Guild Interaction
Professional guilds in Basctdelm expect serious practitioners to hold membership. A character with a relevant background, tool proficiency, or demonstrated skill is normally accepted; those with the Guild Artisan background (or equivalent) may begin with free membership in one appropriate guild. Independents can still practice their trade, but guilds routinely make this harder by restricting material supplies, withholding contracts, or filing formal complaints.
Typical dues are 1 gp per harmon, or 7 gp per year if paid in advance. Apprentices usually pay half. Failure to remain current can lead to suspension or expulsion.
Most player characters deal first with a guild’s Public Contact or Speaker. Direct approaches to a Guildmaster are uncommon without an introduction or significant business. Arcane spellcasters who live or work regularly in the city are expected to register with the appropriate authorities (often involving the Weavebinders); refusal can limit access to components, libraries, and official work.
Guilds respond best to courtesy, clear purpose, and respect for procedure. Open hostility or attempts to undercut established rates are remembered.

Notable Locations
Beacon of Iyo
The Beacon of Iyo [EE-YO] is a ten-sided lighthouse standing on a small island just off the eastern coast of Mistridge Island, between the main ports and the mouth of the Dibaryn River. Originally an Osmanae celestial watchtower, it now serves as both a maritime navigation aid for ships and an aerial waypoint platform for the Basctdelm Watch’s griffon riders. The structure is maintained through a combination of traditional care and minor magical reinforcement of its unique starlight crystal.


A tall, ten-sided stone tower rises from a small rocky island between the eastern docks and the mouth of the Dibaryn River. Even in daylight the structure draws the eye, its sides narrowing as they climb toward a steady, pure white light that pulses gently from a large crystal set high in the tower. Just above the light, a wide stone platform juts out, large enough for griffons to land. As you draw closer, you can see the crystal slowly rotating, casting its unwavering glow across the water and sky.
The Beacon of Iyo is kept operational by a small dedicated staff. The Beacon Keeper manages the nightly activation and basic maintenance of the crystal light.  A small team of Tower Masons performs regular structural repairs to the ten-sided tower and platform. At night, a Night Signal Operator works with the Beacon Keeper to send or interpret any coded light signals requested by the Watch or river traffic.

Braethyn’s Archives
Braethyn’s Archives is the oldest and most prestigious library in Basctdelm, serving as the city’s primary repository of historical records, scholarly works, and rare or restricted knowledge. It is commonly used by scholars, nobles, researchers, and those seeking specific information that cannot be easily found elsewhere in the city.  The Archives works well as a location for information-gathering scenes, research-based quests, or meetings with knowledgeable non-player characters. It is especially useful when players need access to historical events, rare texts, or sensitive records that may be hidden in the restricted Preserved Collection. Because of its strict rules and layered access, it also creates natural opportunities for tension, negotiation, or intrigue when players attempt to obtain restricted materials.The Braethyn’s Archives layout is made up of:
* an Admittance Area where visitors are cleared before access to the library, 
* the Main Library where all of the books and scrolls are organized, 
* the Preserved Collection which is the restricted fourth floor where the most valuable archives are stored, and 
* the Undercroft where miscellaneous parchments are used to create the tomes and scrolls of the library. 
Admittance Area


As you step into Braethyn’s Archives, you find yourself in a grand circular admittance area, the floor a gleaming expanse of polished marble that reflects the light streaming from high windows. The room is open and inviting, yet a subtle air of formality hangs in the space. This is the southern entrance to the archives, where visitors await clearance to enter the library proper. Along the curved walls are wooden benches where patrons can sit while clerks, stationed at a modest desk, process their requests. Dominating the northern side of the room are two ornate double doors, their intricate carvings depicting the pursuit of knowledge through artful images of quills, scrolls, and tomes.
Visitors to Braethyn’s Archives are required to be cleared before gaining access to the library.  They will be required to sign in to the visitor’s log while the clerk validates they have violated any library rules in the past that restrict their access.  Once the visitors have been cleared, the clerk will allow them to pass into the Main Library.
Main Library


Beyond the double doors, you step into the heart of Braethyn’s Archives, a massive three-story chamber with dark wooden shelves lining the circular walls, each one filled with meticulously arranged books and scrolls. The upper levels are lofted, with wrought iron railings and wooden staircases on opposite sides leading to the second and third floors. On the ground level, clusters of small tables and chairs provide spaces for quiet study and reflection, their surfaces dotted with the occasional flickering candle. Overseeing it all is the large, raised curator’s desk on the northern wall, a position that offers a commanding view of the entire library. 
Assistant roam the main library to assist visitors with locating the tomes or scrolls the visitors are looking for.  Each visitor is assigned a table where they can review the materials they are reviewing.  The table can be used to return to, and visitors are allowed to bring their own items to copy contents from the library, but they are not allowed to remove items from the library.
Preserved Collection


Accessible only to those with proper authorization, the Preserved Collection is hidden beyond the public view. Reached via a staircase from the third floor, its single chamber sealed behind a heavy locked door. Inside, the room is dimly lit and impeccably maintained, with shelves of rare books and scrolls displayed under protective glass or bound in reinforced cases. Every visitor here is accompanied by an escort.
There are some special archived items that are stored in the Preserved Collections.  Visitors are not allowed access to them without special authorization or justified need.  If they are allowed, the Curator will give them access and assign the visitor an escort that will accompany the visitor through their review of any items in the Preserved Collection.
The Undercroft


Hidden beneath the main library is the undercroft, accessed through a secured panel door behind the curator’s desk. A circular staircase descends into a dimly lit chamber that feels like stepping into a scholar’s workshop. The air here is musty, the faint smell of old parchment mixing with the sharper tang of ink. Crates filled with loose parchments, scrolls, and ancient texts are stacked in organized chaos, creating narrow pathways between them. At the center of this subterranean archive is a large desk where the scribe works tirelessly, transcribing loose materials into bound tomes to be cataloged in the greater library above. 
There is rarely any need for anyone to enter the Undercroft, but there is typically a scribe or two on site working to transcribe or preserve the items in the library.

Double Mice Inn
The Double Mice Inn is the largest and most popular inn in Basctdelm’s Lower District. It caters primarily to travelers, merchants, laborers, and adventurers who want affordable lodging and a lively atmosphere. The inn makes most of its coin from its busy tavern and gambling, with room rentals as a secondary source of income. Because of its size and constant flow of people, it is an excellent location for players to gather information, meet contacts, or get into (or break up) trouble. Rowdiness is common, but the inn maintains just enough order through its bouncers to avoid constant Watch intervention.


The Double Mice Inn is easy to spot even from a distance, its large three-story frame standing out among the smaller buildings of the Lower District. Warm light spills from its windows, and the muffled sounds of laughter, music, and raised voices can be heard even before you reach the door. A simple wooden sign showing two mice sharing a mug of ale hangs above the entrance. Despite its worn appearance, the inn gives off a lively, welcoming energy that draws in a steady stream of patrons.
It's not uncommon for things to get rowdy in the Double Mice, which is why there is always at least one bouncer helping clear out the culprits of any disruption.  Anyone caught committing a crime will be held by the bouncer and handed over to the Basctdelm Watch.
The Tavern
The tavern is the true heart of the Double Mice Inn. It is loud, crowded, and energetic most hours of the day and night. Gambling is extremely common, and almost any common tavern game can be found here. The staff is used to handling rowdy behavior, and at least one bouncer is always on duty to remove troublemakers. The tavern is a good place for players to gather rumors, hire minor help, or get into bar fights. Information flows freely here, though much of it is exaggerated or only partially true. 


The tavern is a wide, open space filled with the constant noise of conversation, clinking mugs, and occasional bursts of laughter. Long wooden tables and mismatched chairs crowd the floor, many of them occupied by laborers, travelers, and off-duty city workers. A long bar runs along the back wall, where a pair of bartenders move quickly to keep up with orders. The smell of roasted meat, spilled ale, and woodsmoke hangs thick in the air. In one corner, a small group is loudly playing dice, while a bard in another corner struggles to be heard over the din. Several large men with serious expressions stand near the walls, watching the crowd — the inn’s bouncers keeping an eye out for trouble.

Dripping Dagger Tavern


Descending the worn steps into the Dripping Dagger tavern feels like entering a crypt; the walls are carved from rough stone, slick with moisture and streaked with patches of moss. The main room is dimly lit lanterns hanging from iron chains.  At the center of the room is the bar, a long slab of polished black stone that glimmers faintly in the lantern light. Above it hangs a massive iron dagger, its rusted blade streaked with lines that resemble blood trickling down the hilt.   Hidden alcoves and shadowed corners line the walls, perfect for those seeking anonymity. Every surface, from the tables to the stools, is slightly damp to the touch, adding to the oppressive atmosphere. 
Whispers of a secret passage leading to the Bellows swirl among the patrons, though no one openly acknowledges it.

Talward’s Talon (Griffon Grounds)
Talward's Talon is a floating sky castle positioned directly above Basctdelm’s High District. It serves as the primary facility for griffon husbandry, stables, breeding, and rider training for the Basctdelm Watch. 
The castle was originally known as the Bloodstorm Citadel and functioned as the lair of an evil cloud giant. The Knights of Myr defeated the giant and gifted the sky castle to Queen Breya Talward. It has since been repurposed from its previous Meridian role into its current function as a dedicated griffon facility.
Layout – The original configuration of the cloud giant castle was adapted to accommodate its new purpose.  
* Top:  The top level is a rectangular command post that is open to the sky and has many griffon nests
* Middle:  The central level functions as a large area where the griffons east and live
* Bottom:  The lower level holds rider barracks, gear and storage.
Dozens of griffons cycle through the facility at any given time. Daily operations include the breeding program, training of young griffons, rider drills, and preparation for patrol rotations. The site is staffed by a senior griffon master who is a veteran Watch rider, along with trainers, stablehands, and a small detachment of Weavebinders who maintain the castle’s magical floatation systems.
Access to Talward's Talon is primarily by griffon flight from Talward Garrison or from designated landing platforms in the High District. The castle is protected by heavy magical wards and a constant Watch presence. Unauthorized approaches or landings trigger an immediate armed response, and there is no public access to the facility.
This location works well for several types of scenes. It can serve as the starting point or briefing location for aerial missions involving the Watch. Player characters who are griffon riders can undergo evaluation or advanced training here. The site is also useful for stories involving sabotage of the breeding program, theft of valuable griffons, or interaction with Meridian agents who occasionally visit from House of Myr. The lower levels in V3 offer opportunities to discover lingering cloud giant artifacts or unstable residual magic from the castle’s original occupant.

  The Bellows
The Bellows is the large underground district that lies directly beneath the High District of Basctdelm. It is not a slum, but a deliberately shady and secretive area filled with tunneled roads, carved shops, and hidden establishments. Many of the city’s criminal networks and black-market connections run through the Bellows, and it serves as the current headquarters of the Cloak of Defiance.
Access and Layout
The Bellows is reached primarily through the city’s Loop road. Both the North Loop and South Loop roads lead west and descend into the district, where they join to form Bellow’s Lead — the main 20-foot-wide by 20-foot-tall tunnel that runs beneath the High District. This central artery was originally carved during the mining of stone used to build Basctdelm’s walls and structures. Numerous smaller side tunnels branch off Bellow’s Lead, leading to individual shops, taverns, and carved buildings.
The Bellows is gray stone and shadow. Most lanterns are kept low or intentionally snuffed, creating a consistently dark, close environment even during daylight hours above. The air is cool and damp near a large lake known as “The Void”. While not overtly violent in its public spaces, the district carries a constant undercurrent of secrecy and danger. Deals, information, and illicit goods move freely here.

Crimson Seat, the
The Crimson Seat is the hidden headquarters of the Cloak of Defiance, one of the most powerful and well-organized thieves’ guilds operating in Basctdelm. Located on the far side of The Void (the large underground lake beneath the High District), the Crimson Seat is deliberately isolated and difficult to reach without permission. It serves as both the guild’s primary base of operations and the personal residence of its leader, the Shadow Sovereign.
Leadership and Control
The Crimson Seat is under the direct control of the Shadow Sovereign, the current leader of the Cloak of Defiance. She maintains strict authority over who is allowed to enter the headquarters and what activities are permitted there. While the tavern level functions as a gathering place for guild agents, the upper levels are reserved for trusted members and important business. The Shadow Sovereign rarely leaves the Crimson Seat and conducts most of her business from her private sitting room on the top floor, which overlooks The Void.
Access and Security
There are two main ways to reach the Crimson Seat:
1. By ferry across The Void from Bellow’s Lead.
2. Through secret passages known only to high-ranking members of the guild.
The location is well defended. Armed guards are stationed at the dock, and the building contains multiple layers of security, including traps, hidden positions, and restricted access to the upper floors. The isolation provided by the lake makes it difficult for enemies to launch a direct assault without being noticed.
Daily Operations
The ground floor operates as a tavern that serves as the public face of the headquarters. This is where most Cloak of Defiance agents meet, receive assignments, exchange information, and relax between jobs. The upper levels are used for planning operations, storing sensitive records and equipment, housing important guests or agents in hiding, and conducting the guild’s more secretive business.
The Crimson Seat also functions as a safehouse for approved individuals. The Shadow Sovereign can grant temporary sanctuary to guild members or valuable contacts, but such privileges are rarely given and can be revoked at any time.
Dock & Tavern Level (Ground Floor)
This is the public face of the Crimson Seat. The open tavern serves as the main gathering point for Cloak of Defiance agents. The large warehouse doors can be closed quickly to seal off the area if needed. Two guard shacks on either side of the cobblestone path provide security for anyone arriving or leaving by ferry. The open design overlooking The Void makes it easy to watch for threats approaching by water. Most day-to-day Cloak business and meetings happen here.

Leadership Level (Top Floor)

It contains the Shadow Sovereign’s personal sitting room (featuring the large crimson throne-like seat), her private quarters, a small war room for high-level planning, and the records vault’s most sensitive materials.
