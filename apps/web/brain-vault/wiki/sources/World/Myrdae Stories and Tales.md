Betrayal of Amriel
Long ago, Di'Belgi, the powerful demoness known as the Craving Queen, deceived Amriel, an angel of the celestial host. Using her fiendish magic to hide her true nature, Di'Belgi beguiled Amriel into falling deeply in love with her. At her behest, the angel was convinced to steal the Crown of Lyess, a sacred artifact belonging to the goddess of magic, Lyess.
The Crown was adorned with eight orbs, each representing one of the schools of magic, and held within them the vast and ancient knowledge of their respective schools. Once attuned to the crown, one could access the deepest spells and secrets of magic, making it one of the most coveted relics in all of Myrdae.
When Amriel presented the crown to Di'Belgi, her true nature was revealed. Betrayed and devastated by the demon’s treachery, Amriel fought her in a mighty battle, during which the eight orbs were scattered across the lands of Myrdae. The conflict unleashed a demonic invasion, as Di'Belgi summoned her armies from the Underdark, seeking the scattered orbs for herself.
Blood Wars Arrive
Di’Belgi’s demonic incursion led to the Blood Wars on Myrdae, as devils from the Nine Hells, in a rare alignment of interest with mortals, rose to aid the people of Myrdae against Di'Belgi’s forces. This infernal war raged both on the surface and in the Underdark, wreaking devastation across the lands.
Amriel, seeking redemption for his betrayal, descended into the depths of Myrdae to fight against Di'Belgi’s demonic legions. There, he met a powerful mage, Yibrix, skilled in the art of runes. Together, they developed Runeweaving, an art that allowed magical power to be infused into runes and tattooed onto individuals, granting them the ability to wield magic through these enchanted runes. The secrets of this art were captured in the Affirmation of Strife, a powerful tome that also foretold future calamities.
The Forging of Dwarves
In the deep elder days, long centuries before the Fracturing shattered the land, Muerg the Earth Father felt the young world’s bones groaning and shifting. The primordial earth trembled with instability, and Muerg knew that without anchors the surface itself might one day tear apart. He sought a mortal champion worthy of the task and found Goldraen—a lesser hero among the early peoples, a master smith whose skill with hammer and forge was unmatched.
Muerg drew forth a portion of his own primordial earth essence and bestowed it upon Goldraen. For decades the smith labored in the hidden hearts of the mountains, shaping the first dwarves from living stone. Each figure was awakened with the soul of the earth and the fire of creation burning within. When the final clan stirred to life, the act of that divine forging elevated Goldraen himself. He ascended as the immortal Evercast, god of craft and creation, forever bound to the children he had shaped.
Muerg called the dwarves into being for three distinct purposes. The Mountain Dwarves, the traditional and stubborn guardians, were called to fortify the high peaks and hold the world’s surface bones against collapse. Stout, resolute, and unmovable as the stone from which they were carved, they became the builders of great strongholds and the keepers of the deepest mines.
The Barren, also known as Crag and Hill Dwarves, were marked by warmer, more natural skin tones that echo sunlit stone. Muerg summoned them to wander the crags, hills, and open lands, granting them excitable spirits and quick hands so they might bind the fractured surface with lively energy, trade, brew, and adapt wherever the world required mending.
The Duergar, or Gray Dwarves, were called specifically to tend the Underdark’s depths. Muerg charged them with sealing hidden rifts, guarding the world’s underbelly from primordial threats, and maintaining the stability of the dark places far below the light.
The dwarves emerged already organized into proud clans—Orebane among the oldest—with bloodlines, traditions, and forges ready. They spread across Myrdae as living anchors, raising strongholds, delving mines, and continuing the First Forge’s labor. Their halls still echo with the strike of hammers that once rang in the mountain hearts when the world was young.
To this day, dwarves of every kind feel the weight of the world’s bones in their blood and the spark of Goldraen’s First Forge in their hands. When they swear an oath or strike iron, many still whisper the old words: “By Muerg’s stone and Goldraen’s fire, we hold.”
Prophecy of the Year of Rising
The Affirmation of Strife contained a chilling prophecy, warning of the fall of the Assembly of Essence and the rise of four great powers during the Year of Rising. These four entities would bring chaos and destruction to Myrdae:
* Di’Belgi, the Craving Queen – A demoness of unquenchable hunger for power and chaos, seeking to reignite the Blood Wars and reclaim the orbs of Lyess.
* Itoro Gradel, the Time Bender – A mysterious and powerful sorcerer who could manipulate the very fabric of time, distorting reality itself.
* Nethyndria, the Immortal Wyrm – An ancient dragon with the secret to eternal life, fated to rise from slumber and unleash destruction upon the world.
* Suthrin, the Son of Vengeance – A figure consumed by hatred and revenge, whose coming would bring war and strife to the realms of Myrdae.
While it took many years of brutal conflict, there are recorded visions from priests of Lyess that speak of Amriel’s atonement and his ascension to celestial planes through the escort of a great golden ki-rin named Behki.  The tome, lost for ages, was said to have been discovered by an Avantee in the Advents of Harmony named Yedera.
Layeth & Brault - Torn Apart
One night, some say after consuming too much of the local spirits, Smott, the god of destruction, changed his avatar to resemble a beautiful, caring, and passionate elven male who crept into the sleeping quarters of Tiash, the goddess of balance. Entranced with this creature and somewhat intoxicated herself – what was in the wine that night – she allowed herself to be seduced and found herself with child not long after. The union produced twins – a girl and boy (in keeping with balance, of course). Only Smott knew the truth of what had transpired that night – and he initially kept it to himself.
The children – who Tiash named Layeth and Brault – were beautiful but bore no resemblance to their elven father. Tiash spent many harmons searching the lands for her special elf, but never found him. As the twins grew, it became clear that in addition to looking like Tiash, they had also inherited much of their mother’s abilities that mortals didn’t have. In fact, they appeared to rival the talents of one of their peers – young mistress Phoe, the daughter of Lyess, goddess of magic, and Athuel, god of knowledge. The three toddlers often played together, and everyone took pleasure in commenting about their obvious gifts.
Smott enjoyed his private lark, but over time vanity started to get the best of him. Every few harmons, when the gods and goddesses convened as a group on Oberra, he’d see his offspring and began to resent how cherished they were among the group, as well as all of the praise bestowed upon Tiash for raising them. Of course, bent on destruction, he decided to end the charade. One night, when the twins were about 3-1/2 years old, Smott reappeared to Tiash as her elven mate just as she was entering the great hall to meet with the other gods and goddesses. Happy to see him, she sent for the children so that their father could meet them. After greetings were exchanged and before the entire council, Smott changed back into his true self – revealing the truth of the children’s parentage to not only Tiash, but to all present. Highly angered and betrayed, Tiash cast Smott aside – imploring on the others to punish him for his actions. Neera, goddess of order, in her infinite wisdom reminded Tiash that the union was between both of them – that, while fooled by Smott’s disguise, she had played a role that night as well and that no matter how she felt about Smott, the children are a part of him, too. While Tiash knew that Neera was correct, she couldn’t look at her children the same way. Her heart was broken. How could they be the offspring of the cruel Smott? She begged Neera to take the children while she took some time to figure out how to make everything right and balanced again.
After some time, and upon consultation and reflection among the deities, Tiash decided that the most balanced course of action would be to give the boy to Smott to raise, while she would raise the girl. There were other conditions as well: for his deception, Smott would not be allowed to have contact with Layeth and she would never know that he was her father; as such, the two children would not be referred to as twins or even siblings; the council would not speak the truth of their parentage outside of the chamber walls, and, while Tiash would be allowed to see Brault, she would not acknowledge that she was his mother – he would simply be referred to as Smott’s son.
As time went on, despite the maternal anguish Tiash felt separating her children, this arrangement seemed to work. As they matured into adults, Layeth became the demigoddess of sacrifice (in recognition of what Tiash gave up), while Brault became the demigod of war (a befitting title for the son of his two parents). Neera, glad for her intervention to help establish order and keep the peace among the deities, always felt that the solution Tiash crafted was somewhat flawed. But she didn’t know how or why until sometime much later…the day that Brault and Layeth met as adults.
It should come as no surprise that someday the two were destined to meet. While Smott was strictly forbidden to see Layeth, there was nothing in place to prevent his son from roaming the lands when they were in residence. Such it was on that fateful day that Layeth, in the guise of a young maiden with fair skin and long red hair, was enjoying splashing around in the large lake area north of the Distancion Mountains (what is now known as Lake Tribathe). Apparently alone except for the company of some birds and small animals, Brault thought she was lovely and watched her for some time. Because she wasn’t doing anything extraordinary, he had no reason to think she was anything other than she appeared – a maiden of the lands. After a spell, he approached her – as a young man – introducing himself as a traveler from the Mahruud who had only recently discovered this area (he was assuming she would know most of the locals and was looking for an excuse to have her show him around). As fate would have it, both felt an instant connection, but the sensation was misinterpreted into something else. Seizing the moment, the two forged a fast friendship, and eventually over the next harmon, a romantic relationship. While such relationships were not uncommon in these times, the two of them had no idea of their birthright and the connection that linked them.
As the romance bloomed, Layeth decided that she needed to be honest about her true identity, revealing that she was the daughter of Tiash and a demigoddess in her own right. She assured her companion that while she was giving up her identity for the sake of honesty, it would not change her feelings and she hoped he would feel the same. Brault, stunned by the revelation, immediately revealed his true self as well, wanting her to know that he was also of noble status and the son of Smott. He shared with her that he had in fact met her mother on several occasions, but marveled that they had not had the pleasure of meeting before, likely because he traveled in other realms with his father most of the time. Thrilled with their discovery, the two headed for the great hall to share the news with their parents.
As most daughters do, Layeth had already shared with her mother about her new love, but the look on Tiash’s face when the two showed up in the great chamber was not what Layeth expected. Her emotions were an equal mix of joy and grief – happy that the two had finally met and been reunited, but sad that they didn’t know the truth. Before she could say anything, however, Smott entered the chamber and instantly knew he was looking at his adult daughter.
Dwarves of Dunduar
On Kesero the 11th, year 49, dwarven clan elders Dundrim Umberfist and Uarmil Cragfury founded the mining village of Dunduar.  This village settled in the eastern foothills of the Distancion Mountains on the southern half of the Severed Crease.  There was a great amount of iron ore, with occasional pockets of gemstones, causing the mines to extend beyond the foothills and deep under the mountain.
After years of mining, the dwarves broke way into the Underdark.  Disliking the disturbance, denizens of the Underdark began an assault on the dwarves through a series of battles with drow and illithid (mind flayers).  While the protecting armies of Dunduar were engaged in the many battles in the mines surrounding the village, the illithids began an elaborate plan that would take several stretches to complete.  Through the domination and work of a score of umber hulks, the mind flayers created a large web of layered tunnels beneath Dunduar.  On a day, now known as Morn of Dunduar (the 18th of Hemist, year 59), so much earth had been moved beneath the mining village that it collapsed in ruins some 100 feet beneath the surface, leaving a huge pitted chasm.  
There were only a dozen or so of the villager’s survivors that witnessed that day.  Dunduar’s population was left decimated, save that dozen and the clan members.  The Dundar dwarves, being stubborn as a dwarf is, vowed to create a stronghold that would never fall in such a manner.  And with the village no more, the dwarves moved further into the mountains.  Less than a harmon later, on the 7th of Baegrum, year 59, the new stronghold of Dunduar was founded within the Distancion mountains itself.  The dwarves started by creating interlocking foundational columns and beams that rooted deep into the mountains to prevent any type of repeat collapse.
It took 10 years to complete the foundation and structural settlements suited to house the dwarves of Dunduar.  In the year 69, the new dwarven fortress was on its way and the mining began again.  Dwarves from all over came to join Dunduar and by the turn of the century, Dundar was home to over 5,000 dwarves.
On the 30th of Talil, year 81, the mining dwarves of Dunduar made a huge discovery … an ore with magical properties.  A unique finding, it was only fitting to refer to the new ore as Dunduar Ore.  Great smiths, jewelers and mages would come from all over Myrdae to work with the new ore, and many powerful items were created.
Dundrim Umberfist died of old age in the year 378, having had two children, son Boreth (born in the year 181) and daughter Gloreth (born in the year 185).  Boreth took over as the elder for Umberfist.  Gloreth became an ambassador of the Umberfist clan to dwarven nations of the north, as well as a trader for Dunduar’s mineral riches.
Uarmil Cragfury had died several years earlier in the year 317 from an unknown disease, but left his clan leadership to his only son Saeviul, who was born in 182.  Saeviul and his immediate family were known for taking annual regional trips that lasted a ten-day, to neighboring locations.  In the year of 271, Saeviul Cragfury and his family never returned from their annual outage.
Rise of the Ardent One
According to legend, long ago in the early days of Myrdae, there was a powerful sorceress named Diverra who was renowned for her beauty and grace. She was the daughter of a wealthy noble family from the Bathaen Empire and was known throughout the land for her kindness, wisdom, and benevolence.
As she grew older, Diverra became more and more devoted to the ideals of love and beauty, dedicating herself to spreading those concepts throughout the land. She would often travel from province to province, performing acts of charity and kindness, and inspiring others to do the same.
Over time, Diverra's reputation grew and she became a beloved figure throughout Myrdae. People began to worship her as a goddess of love and beauty, offering up prayers and sacrifices in her name. Her followers built temples and shrines in her honor, and her teachings became the basis for a new religion that spread throughout the land.
There are several versions of the myth on how Diverra became a goddess in Myrdae, but the most common one tells of a tragic event that led to her ascension.
As the legend goes, during one of her travels, Diverra encountered a powerful demon who had been terrorizing the people of a small village called Ulgreer. Despite the danger, she bravely confronted the demon and engaged in a fierce battle with it. Though she fought valiantly, the demon ultimately overpowered her, and she was mortally wounded in the fight.
As she lay dying, Diverra's body began to glow with a radiant light, and she ascended into the heavens, becoming a goddess of love and beauty. It is said that her final act of selflessness and bravery was what earned her a place among the gods, and her legacy continued to inspire generations of worshippers.
Some versions of the myth suggest that Diverra's ascension was also aided by other gods or magical forces, but the central theme of her selflessness and devotion to love and beauty remains constant throughout the stories.
The name of the powerful demon that killed Diverra in some versions of the myth and served the demon lord Di'Belgi is often referred to as Zarak'Nor.  Zarak'Nor was said to be a fearsome and malevolent demon, known for his immense strength and dark powers. His defeat of Diverra was seen as a significant victory for the forces of darkness, and her subsequent ascension as a goddess was seen as a powerful symbol of hope and resilience against the forces of evil.
Ulgreer was a small, peaceful village located on the southwestern edge of the Western Crimson Pines forest, between the town of Ferndale and the city of Brimhaven. The village was known for its stunning views of the sunrise, and the locals took great pride in maintaining the natural beauty of their surroundings. The tragic events that occurred in Ulgreer, including the death of Diverra, have become an integral part of Myrdae's mythology, and the village remains a symbol of bravery and sacrifice to this day.


Oldport & the Smiling Duchess
Legend has it that during a particularly tumultuous period marked by shipwrecks and losses due to the unpredictable currents, monstrous sea creatures, and sudden storms of the Tideless Span, a mysterious woman named Osanna appeared in Oldport. With her serene demeanor and enigmatic smile, she brought with her an air of fortune that seemed almost magical.
Osanna soon became a beloved figure among the traders and sailors. Under her subtle guidance, they learned to read the signs of the sea—when to harness the favorable winds, how to avoid the wrath of sea monsters during their aggressive seasons, and the safest times to sail. Tales of narrow escapes from whirlpools, uncanny rescues from the grips of giant squids, and unexpected finds of floating merchandise became increasingly common, all attributed to the luck that Osanna seemed to carry with her.
Her presence became synonymous with good fortune, and soon, the people of Oldport began to regard Osanna not merely as a bringer of luck but as its very embodiment. They believed that her blessings could sway the capricious moods of the Tideless Span. Before long voyages, captains and crews would visit Osanna, offering gifts and seeking her favor, hoping to secure safe passage and prosperous trades.
When the time came for Osanna to leave Oldport, as mysteriously as she had arrived, the townspeople were struck with a deep sense of loss. In her honor, they started the tradition of the Festival of Fair Winds on the anniversary of her arrival, where sailors, traders, and townsfolk would gather to exchange stories of the sea, share their fortunes, and celebrate the goddess who had once walked among them.
People from across Myrdae would often carry tokens bearing her image as a talisman of luck and good fortune.
The Laztyr & Suthrin
During the hot season of Sultra in 1224, the Year of the Being, a brave group of traveling companions made their way into the village of Bistron. The group’s arrival coincided with that of missing villagers. Rising to the call, the companions began their search for those missing. Their investigation led them to the nearby mines where they discovered the old mining rails had been repurposed by slave traders known as the Barrenscars. These traders apparently lengthened the mines to stretch through the Severed Crease, emerging into the Mahruud where they could quickly provide slaves as fighters for the arenas of that land. The recent activity by the Barrenscars appeared to have set something dire in motion. A group of the slave traders were attacked just before the arrival of the companions to the mines by something of great strength that came from deeper in the mountain.
Near the attack, the adventurers discovered a passage that led them to an ancient cult temple to Brault. This temple had as its centerpiece a very large crystal, which the followers called the Laztyr. The large crystal artifact was discovered by adventurers broken apart into dozens of shards. According to ancient writings found in the temple, the Laztyr was used to recall the exiled anti-hero Suthrin, son of Brault and the demigoddess Tornia. The writings claimed that Suthrin was banished to Stygia, the fifth layer of hell, by the demigod Addan, the Protector. According to the texts, Suthrin was born shortly after his father was thrown from the heavens by the goddess Neera. Raised in the Mahruud, he was known for causing chaos and leaving a path of destruction wherever he went. He was later exiled after spending 5 years on a raging tear through the lands of Myrdae, which he claimed was warranted as payment for Neera’s actions against his father
The adventuring companions made many key discoveries that day. The first item of note was that the shrine had been disturbed by grimlocks. Next, while searching areas around the shrine, they discovered the crystalized artifact known as the Laztyr was extracted from a nearby tunneled mine that was rich in orange opals. But most disturbing, it appears that the attack on the slave traders could have been caused by Suthrin, having returned from the hells and now free on the surface of Myrdae.

Year of Fending Heroes
It is 1226, the Year of Fending.
A band of heroes have successfully taken their airship into the Shadowfell, scrambled their way into Lair of the Immortal Wyrm, Nethyndria.  After a long battle, there was only one hero standing … Abaddon.
To recount it from the barbarian himself …
“I reached in and ripped the dragon’s eyes from her skull.  My raging howls were drowned out by her screams as her body crashed against the frozen floor.  Nethyndria lay lifeless and vanquished forever.”
Abaddon paused, calming his inner demon, and surveying the battlefield before he floated back to the ground. All he could hear was the wind, the cracking of ice, and his own breathing. The battle took each of his companions, their bodies lay broken and defeated, and there was nothing he could think of that would bring them back.
His senses told him Malachia, the traitor, was no longer close or not a current threat to him... but what about Sparrow and the ship. He had to return to ensure they were safe. 
The storm outside had died down a little. Maybe her powers were diminishing across this land. The crew were safe and able to clean off enough ice to take flight. The hull rose from the snow and ice and limped across the plateau to Nethyndria’s lair like a wounded bird.
Abaddon returned to the dome alone. He burned the carcasses of the dragons and their minions in a mighty funeral pyre as he pondered a suitable tribute for his companions. He did not know how long it would take to leave this land and get help or what dangers lay ahead. What to do about his companions.
But first... Treasure! He climbed the roof, dispatched the booty to the ground, and loaded the ship. He would need all this and the sale of the flying fortress to recover his friends (yes, that castle is going on the auction block!)
After contemplating a long while, he decided upon a warrior's farewell for each of his companions. They would be buried here, on this battlefield in a tribute to their valor!!!!!. And he swore an oath to return and restore them. As he laid each one down with their artifacts, he remembered the good times and took a trinket from each body to remember them by.
He left their bodies today but vowed to return.

Dragonborn Origins
Black - Clans Noxari & Miferak
The Noxari Clan are black-scaled dragonborn from Seameet Marsh, north of Nauldeaus near the hills of Wheldrake.
Their ancestry traces back to Aternox the Drowned, an ancient female black dragon who ruled the marsh’s blackwater channels, sunken roads, drowned ruins, and reed-choked pools.
Aternox was not chosen because she was good, merciful, or noble in the way mortals understand those words. She was chosen because she embodied an essential truth of black dragonkind: ruin remembers, rot transforms, and what is buried is never truly gone. She was cruel, patient, and terrible, but she was also a keeper of consequence. She punished arrogance, dissolved false monuments, and forced kingdoms to face the sins they had tried to sink beneath mud and water.
From Aternox’s god-blessed eggs came the first Noxari, who inherited her ties to blackwater, ruins, memory, survival, and judgment. They believe buried things are never truly gone — old sins, old bones, old oaths, and old powers all leave traces.
In a later age, a powerful male black dragon named Miferion, the Growling Mist, came to Seameet Marsh and defeated Aternox. His victory did not create a new bloodline of god-blessed dragonborn, but it did create a rival tradition. Some black dragonborn rejected the old Noxari reverence for Aternox and gathered around Miferion instead, forming Clan Miferak.
This divide still shapes black dragonborn identity in Seameet Marsh. The Noxari see themselves as heirs of Aternox’s sacred burden: to endure ruin, remember what others bury, and keep dangerous things from rising again. Clan Miferak sees itself as heir to Miferion’s conquest, believing that strength, not memory, decides who rules the blackwater.
Blue - Caerath Clan
The Caerath Clan are blue-scaled dragonborn from Dragonstone, a storm-scarred region of Mahruud north of the Dragonspine Mountains.
Their ancestry traces back to Caerulith the Fulminant, an ancient female blue dragon who ruled the desert around Dragonstone. Many legends claim Dragonstone is made from the petrified bones of an ancient dragon, but the Caerath believe the truth is older and stranger: Dragonstone is tied to Caerulith’s power -  a place where lightning, stone, and draconic will were fused into a monument of command.
Caerulith was not chosen because she was kind, gentle, or humble. She was chosen because she embodied an essential truth of blue dragonkind: power must be mastered before it can be rightfully wielded. She was proud, commanding, and terrible, but also disciplined. She believed strength without control was weakness, and that those who claimed authority had to prove they could endure the storm.
From Caerulith’s god-blessed eggs came the first Caerath, who inherited her ties to lightning, desert survival, hierarchy, discipline, oaths, and command. They believe power is not merely taken; it must be declared, tested, witnessed, and upheld.
The Caerath are often seen by outsiders as arrogant, severe, domineering, or overly rigid. Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred burden: to master power before wielding it, to command without becoming tyrants, and to prove that authority is earned by discipline rather than fear alone.
No one has seen Caerulith in ages. Some believe she died beneath Dragonstone and that the so-called petrified bones are truly hers. Others believe Dragonstone is not her grave at all, but her mark upon the world — proof that she still watches from somewhere beneath the storm-scarred desert, waiting for Mahruud to prove worthy of her rule again.
Brass -  Aesvar Clan
The Aesvar Clan are brass-scaled dragonborn from areas around the Sutherford Vec, in southwest Inverland
Their ancestry traces back to Aesratha the Waykeeper, an ancient brass dragon who made her home among the islands off the coast, but whose true domain was the sky above the coastal towns, harbor roads, sea lanes, and trade routes of the Sutherford Vec.  Sailors, merchants, pilgrims, and messengers watched for the warm glint of her wings as a sign that the route ahead was protected.
Aesratha was not chosen because she was quiet, passive, or harmless.  She was chosen because she embodied an essential truth of brass dragonkind: words are water when the road is dry.  Along the coast, that truth came to mean that news, warnings, names, bargains, and stories were as vital as food, water, or safe harbor.  A single spoken warning could save a ship from reefs, a caravan from raiders, or a town from danger moving along the trade roads.
From Aesratha’s god-blessed eggs came the first Aesvar, who inherited her ties to hospitality, conversation, stories, trade routes, safe passage, firelight, memory, and coastal survival.  They believe a shared word can be as precious as a shared drink, and that no traveler should vanish from the road or sea without their name, story, or warning being carried onward.
The Aesvar are often seen by outsiders as talkative, nosy, overly familiar, or impossible to keep secrets from.  Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred duty: to keep the routes alive, preserve the stories of those who pass along them, and offer warmth where the world gives only distance, salt wind, and uncertain horizons.
As for Aesratha - some say she vanished during a terrible coastal storm, guiding ships away from the rocks until her own wings disappeared into the lightning and rain.  Others believe she withdrew to one of the outer islands, sealing herself inside a hidden cavern filled with maps, names, and stories gathered from every traveler she ever protected.  A few coastal elders insist she is not gone at all — that she still flies at dawn or dusk, mistaken for sunlight on brass-colored clouds.  Aesratha did not leave the roads. She became the promise that someone will remember where they lead.
Bronze -  Clan Annagos
Clan Annagos are bronze-scaled dragonborn from Gaelscape, a region west of the Jagged Waste Crags. The town of Annagos was later named for the clan, becoming a lasting sign of their place in the region’s history.
Their ancestry traces back to Annagos the Revered, an ancient bronze dragon who watched over Gaelscape’s storm-worn hills, hard roads, frontier settlements, and crag-shadowed borders.
Annagos was not chosen because he loved war, conquest, or glory. He was chosen because he embodied an essential truth of bronze dragonkind: the shield is only sacred when it is raised before the weak. He understood that strength was not proven by domination, but by the willingness to stand between danger and those who could not withstand it alone.
From Annagos’s god-blessed eggs came the first Annagos, who inherited his ties to protection, courage, vigilance, honorable strength, storm, duty, organized defense, and martial discipline. They believe power becomes noble only when it is used to guard others, and that courage means stepping forward before fear has finished speaking.
The Annagos are often seen by outsiders as stern, dutiful, militant, or overly protective. Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred burden: to defend the vulnerable, hold the line against danger, and ensure that strength is never mistaken for the right to rule over those it was meant to protect.
Annagos has not been seen in many generations. Some believe he died defending Gaelscape from a great threat that came out of the Jagged Waste Crags, and that the town of Annagos was founded near the place where his final stand protected the first bronze dragonborn settlements. Others believe he did not die, but withdrew into the storm-worn highlands west of the crags, keeping watch from a hidden aerie where thunder still gathers before danger moves. Among Clan Annagos, the most sacred belief is that he remains bound to his old charge: not ruling over his descendants, but guarding the borders they cannot yet see.
To the clan, his absence is not abandonment. It is proof that a true shield does not seek praise: it stands where the danger is greatest.
Copper -  Kaendune Clan
The Kaendune Clan are copper-scaled dragonborn from the Blistered Highlands, a desert region of Mahruud.
Their ancestry traces back to Kaelykk the Daring, an ancient copper dragon who hailed from the Withered Spire. The Withered Spire rises from the desert floor as a tall, weather-carved stone formation of copper-stained rock, cracked ledges, narrow climbs, and wind-cut hollows. To the Kaendune, it is not merely a landmark, but a test of courage, cleverness, and nerve.
Kaelykk was not chosen because he was solemn, obedient, or easy to command. He was chosen because he embodied an essential truth of copper dragonkind: the cliff does not yield to strength; it yields to the daring hand. He was clever, irreverent, and bold, but he also understood that survival in the highlands required more than force. It required wit, agility, nerve, and the courage to find a path where others saw only stone.
From Kaelykk’s god-blessed eggs came the first Kaendune, who inherited his ties to stone, desert highlands, wit, daring, freedom, climbing, riddles, clever resistance, and impossible paths. They believe strength without imagination becomes brittle, and that the boldest victories are often won by those willing to leap, laugh, climb, or think where others only push.
The Kaendune are often seen by outsiders as reckless, mocking, unpredictable, or impossible to take seriously. Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred burden: to challenge fear with courage, break oppression with cleverness, and never mistake the obvious path for the only path.
Long ago, Kaelykk was seen circling the Withered Spire, perching on impossible ledges, or appearing to travelers who had become trapped among the highland cliffs. But he has not been seen for some time. Some believe he vanished into a hidden hollow somewhere in the Mahruud, following a route he could not resist exploring. Others think he was defeated by powerful foes.  Among the Kaendune, however, the favored belief is that he is not dead at all — only gone ahead, leaving one last impossible path for his descendants to discover.
Gold -  Clan Òrhael
Clan Òrhael are gold-scaled dragonborn from Caelora, tied to the oldest elven civilization.
Their ancestry traces back to Òryzhael the Radiant, an ancient male gold dragon who watched over the gold dragon eggs when they were blessed by the ancient dragon gods. He was not merely the source of the gold dragonborn lineage; he was the guardian-witness of their beginning, standing watch as draconic essence passed into a mortal-born people.
Òryzhael was not chosen because he was splendid, powerful, or radiant alone. He was chosen because he embodied an essential truth of gold dragonkind: what is sacred must be guarded before it can be inherited. He represented wisdom, divine order, sunlight, radiant fire, moral discipline, and the sacred duty to protect what must outlive the present age.
From Òryzhael’s god-blessed eggs came the first Òrhael, who inherited his ties to sacred guardianship, wisdom, vigilance, radiant fire, ancient culture, and moral responsibility. They are often taught that their bloodline is not a privilege, but a charge: to guard beauty, truth, and order from corruption before claiming them as their own.
Òryzhael is no longer seen in Caelora. Some believe he departed after the blessing, trusting his descendants and the elves of Caelora to carry forward what he had guarded. Others believe he passed beyond mortal sight and remains a hidden guardian, returning only if Caelora’s most sacred inheritance is truly threatened.
Green -  Clan Drathag
Clan Drathag are green-scaled dragonborn from Edgerest Forest, in the Yearning Vale.
Their ancestry traces back to Theldratha the Bitter, an ancient green dragon who ruled the deep woods, root-choked paths, moss-dark hollows, and poison-thick glades of Edgerest Forest.
Theldratha was not chosen because she was honest, gentle, or merciful. She was chosen because she embodied an essential truth of green dragonkind: poison is only a truth the body cannot deny. She was patient, cunning, and cruel, but she also understood that all hidden things eventually work their way to the surface. Lies, secrets, betrayals, and buried motives were no different from venom in the blood — they could be ignored for a time, but not forever.
From Theldratha’s god-blessed eggs came the first Drathag, who inherited her ties to poison, forests, patience, secrecy, bitter memory, hidden paths, bargains, and the dangerous power of truth revealed slowly. They believe a word can be as venomous as a fang, and that the most powerful truths are often the ones no one wishes to taste.
The Drathag are often seen by outsiders as secretive, manipulative, unsettling, or untrustworthy. Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred burden: to understand what others hide, survive what others deny, and know when a bitter truth must be spoken before it poisons everything around it.
Theldratha has not been seen openly in generations. Some believe she withdrew into the deepest reaches of Edgerest Forest, where the trees grow so thick that even sunlight enters only in fragments. Others believe she was slain long ago, but that her blood seeped into the roots of the forest, leaving Edgerest itself poisoned by her memory.
The Drathag tell a more troubling version. They believe Theldratha did not die or flee, but learned to exist through the forest itself — in whispered bargains, bitter dreams, thorned vines, poisoned wells, and the sudden feeling that something ancient is listening from behind the leaves. Among the Drathag, the oldest belief is that Theldratha is not missing -  she has simply become harder to separate from Edgerest Forest.
Red -  Clan Tyralax
Clan Tyralax are red-scaled dragonborn from Mount Wick, the great volcano on Jehsle.
Their ancestry traces back to Rutylaxarion the Molten, an ancient red dragon who ruled the burning slopes, lava-veined caverns, ash fields, and scorched skies around Mount Wick.
Rutylaxarion was not chosen because he was merciful, gentle, or restrained. He was chosen because he embodied an essential truth of red dragonkind: what cannot endure the flame was never worthy of the crown. He was proud, wrathful, and terrible, but he also understood that fire is more than destruction. Fire is trial, revelation, and transformation.
From Rutylaxarion’s god-blessed eggs came the first Tyralax, who inherited his ties to fire, conquest, courage, pride, volcanic trial, visible glory, wrath, and command. They believe power should not be hidden or whispered in shadow; it should be proven openly and endured before others.
The Tyralax are often seen by outsiders as arrogant, dangerous, temperamental, or hungry for power. Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred burden: to become worthy of the fire they inherited, rather than be consumed by it.
Rutylaxarion has not been seen in ages. Some believe he sleeps beneath Mount Wick, coiled in a chamber below the volcano’s molten heart, waiting for the mountain to wake fully before he rises again. Others believe he was consumed by his own fire and became part of the volcano itself, his wrath still felt in every eruption, tremor, and river of lava that scars Jehsle. A few Tyralax elders whisper that he left Mount Wick long ago, not in defeat, but in search of a greater crown worthy of his flame.
Silver-  Nelcrath Clan
The Nelcrath Clan are silver-scaled dragonborn from Dunduar.
Their ancestry traces back to Nelcradia the Bright, an ancient silver dragon who watched over Dunduar. She was remembered not only for her radiance, but for her compassion, restraint, and willingness to stand beside mortals rather than above them.
Nelcradia was not chosen because she was soft, passive, or unwilling to fight. She was chosen because she embodied an essential truth of silver dragonkind: mercy is not weakness - it is strength that refuses to become cruel. She understood that protection without compassion becomes control, and that justice without mercy can become another form of violence.
From Nelcradia’s god-blessed eggs came the first Nelcrath, who inherited her ties to mercy, protection, cold air, bright skies, hidden guardianship, compassion, friendship, watchfulness, humility, and strength restrained by kindness. They believe true strength is shown not only in defeating enemies, but in knowing when to stay the blade.
The Nelcrath are often seen by outsiders as idealistic, overly forgiving, reserved, or quietly intense. Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred burden: to protect without pride, show mercy without surrendering justice, and stand beside those who suffer rather than above them.
Nelcradia has not been seen in her true form for many generations. Some believe she departed Dunduar when she was no longer needed, trusting the Nelcrath to carry her mercy into the world. Others believe she died in a long-ago battle, defending those under her protection.  But the Nelcrath preserve a different belief: they claim she laid aside the terror and splendor of her dragon form and chose to walk among mortals in secret. She may appear as a healer, a quiet traveler, a soldier who refuses to strike a fallen enemy, or a stranger who offers mercy when vengeance would be easier.
Among Clan Nelcrath, the oldest teaching is that Nelcradia is not gone - she is wherever cruelty is refused at great cost.
White -  Clan Kozhakuun
Clan Kozhakuun are white-scaled dragonborn from the Deadwing Mountains, though no one is certain which glacier, pass, or buried valley first held their ancestral home.
Their ancestry traces back to Leukozhar the Deadwing, an ancient white dragon who hunted the frozen peaks, ice-choked ravines, snowblind passes, and hidden caverns of the Deadwing Mountains.
Leukozhar was not chosen because he was gentle, merciful, or wise in the way mortals understand wisdom. He was chosen because he embodied an essential truth of white dragonkind: the cold does not hate; it reveals what cannot survive. He was predatory, patient, and terrible, but he also represented endurance stripped of illusion. In his domain, comfort meant nothing, pride meant nothing, and only what could endure the winter remained.
From Leukozhar’s god-blessed eggs came the first Kozhakuun, who inherited his ties to ice, hunger, endurance, instinct, isolation, hunting, memory, and survival without illusion. They believe hardship does not create truth so much as uncover it, revealing what is strong, what is false, and what must be preserved before the storm takes it.
The Kozhakuun are often seen by outsiders as cold, severe, feral, emotionless, or uncivilized. Among their own people, however, they are taught that their bloodline carries a sacred burden: to endure the long winter, preserve what would otherwise be lost beneath it, and survive without begging the world to become kinder.
Leukozhar has not been seen in ages. Some believe he lies frozen beneath a glacier in the Deadwing Mountains, preserved in ice so ancient it no longer melts. Others believe he still hunts in storms no mortal can cross, appearing only as a pale shape behind the snow before vanishing into the white.
