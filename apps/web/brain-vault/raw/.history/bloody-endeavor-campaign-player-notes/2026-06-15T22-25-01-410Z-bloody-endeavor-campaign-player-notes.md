Escorted to the Great Hall
The morning of the 22nd, a guard comes and offers to escort us to the Great Hall. We walk by the Great Forge and the mines, seeing elevators going up and down into the mines. We approach the Great Hall and see a set of guards. The escort leads us through the corridor into a 200 x 100 room filled with 100 pillars, each with their own designs. Some shaped like dwarves, some made of intricate material. The ceiling looks like waves of precious metals. We hear a hubbub of noise as 2 dozen groups, roughly 100 people, all well dressed and talking. Most are dwarvish, some are drow. They all seem to be talking within their own groups. On the far wall are 2 guarded golden doors to the throne room. We see a hooded individual with their hood down, we assume a delegate of the Dalasor Coast. There are also the 5 short robed figures that Caelion saw at the ring of respect, they aren’t talking with anyone. Caelion checks to see if there are any Monks of Iohir in the room but doesn’t see any. We run into Greythane and he lets us know this is where nobles meet while the Lord Emperor is talking with someone in private. He says he’s meeting with an advisor named Sir Duvall, who visits on occasion. He doesn’t know Sir Duvall well, nor does he know what they’re talking about, but they’ve been talking for about an hour. Pagern and Lucerion recognize the Duvall name, a noble family from a town on the other side of the mountainside called Narzon, known for being great wine traders.  
While we wait for our turn with the king, we choose to eavesdrop a bit on the nearby conversations. Rhody tries to listen in on the Lord’s conversation. He doesn’t hear anything, but notices that the 5 short black robed individuals are paying attention to him. They appear to be armed, and Rhody discerns they don’t like us. We piece together that the taller one they were with earlier was Sir Duvall. Caelion listens in on other conversations going on in the room; the hill dwarves are disgruntled with their lack of representation in Khagdaruhl, the mountain dwarves are talking about the gem trade, and specifically mention getting diamonds with the Dalasor Coast. The Duergar are talking about the nightmares they’ve been having, blaming the other dwarves somewhat for their bad dreams. 
Eventually the gold doors open, and a figure with a black robe and silver linings steps out. Lucerion quickly says a prayer and casts Enhance Ability (Charisma) on Albross, and Caelion casts Guidance. As Sir Duvall exits, the 5 guards surround him. Lucerion bows and greets him, but he is paid little heed. We’re invited inside, offered a private audience. Greythane tells us not to call him king, only to call him Lord Emperor. We enter the throne room, a string of fire warming up the room. We see 3 raised sections with 3-4 dwarves and on the far back wall a silver chair with faint electricity sparking off of it with a black-skinned dwarf. His white beard has rings braided into it, and he wears a black crown shining with gems embedded in it. 
The Lord Emperor greets us and asks what our purpose is for meeting with him. In common, Albross lets him know of the situation in Witguard and how we are ambassadors for the Magistrate. We offer the scroll we got from the magistrate and the Lord Emperor takes it and reads it. He summarizes that much like the Milithia Empire, Witguard wants to ally with Kahgdaruhl. Albross mentions as well that we met with the Runegrip dwarves, and how the piece of the Rimestone that they guarded was taken by the Monks of Iohir. Lucerion notices that the main dwarf of each section are pretty stoic, while the other dwarves chat amongst themselves. Rhody notices that despite his stoic face, his brows furrow as he tries to read Albross’s expression to gauge his honesty. He says that “Master Huldi” did not tell him that last night. He was briefed by Greythane about what we knew, and knows that the relationship with the monks is strained. He lets Master Huldi see the piece of the Rimestone, Rhody speaks suddenly in Dwarvish to say that The Cold Breath is a threat. Albross says that he’s had a vision of the eldritch threat as a result of the Rimestone being taken by Master Huldi. Lucerion whispers to the group asking how the Cold Breath completing the Rimestone to release the eldritch threat is a good thing, but the Lord Emperor hears him and asks what we know about the Rimestone. He explains that it was originally meant to destroy the undead aberration. He brings forward a nerdy duergar, who explains that hundreds of years ago, the Yagvur Sierra were more rolling fields. The dwarves and giants made great cities. The dwarves began using dark magic to dig deeper and deeper, eventually stumbling upon a corpse. Their dark magic eventually awakened it, and it began to destroy their civilization. The mages of the time made the Rimestone to freeze it, then broke it apart and spread their pieces around the mountains. The being has begun messing with the minds of people. 
Caelion asks what the relationship with Witguard and Khagdaruhl could be, he says he would speak with his advisors. Lucerion lets him know of the battles occurring between the two. Lucerion tells that Khagdaruhl is supplying arms and gems to the Dalasor Coast. The Lord Emperor tells us that the scroll offered Khagdaruhl money for the same. As the meeting is wrapping up, he asks how long we intend to say and we let him know we’ll be here for a stretch. We ask to go see the Rimestone as representatives of the Runegrip dwarves. 
To the Rimestone

We go down the stairs, and the dwarf introduces himself as Dalgurn, Watcher of the Stone. He says it’s about a 30 minute walk to the Rimestone.  There is no light, and the ice crunches underneath our feet. It’s about 20 below freezing. We see a frosted over image of mages spewing out energy as the staircase continues down. We enter a 100 x 50 room with 2 to 3 dozen mounds around the room covered in snow. Some of us notice that the mounds look like large dwarves, like a frozen over terracotta army. The temperature is slightly warmer here. Lucerion hears a crack of ice, as a dwarven statue begins to move.
