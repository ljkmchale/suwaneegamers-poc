Overview
Emberstran is a coastal city located in the eastern region of Orbansia, along the shores of the Glimmering Sea and nestled just south of the Severed Crease. The city lies not far from Mount Emberstran, an unrest volcano whose eruption during the Awakening transformed the surrounding landscape. Though once threatened with destruction, Emberstran survived and has since thrived, bolstered by the fertile volcanic soil ideal for grape cultivation. Today, Emberstran is a major producer of wine and a hub for trade, with goods flowing in and out via its bustling port.
The city is divided into two main districts. The southern port district is crowded and lively, packed with warehouses, docks, taverns, shops, and dense housing for the working class. Fog frequently rolls in from the Glimmering Sea and settles here, giving the area a mysterious, moody atmosphere. The northern district is elevated and home to the city’s wealthier citizens, filled with manors, finely kept gardens, and broad cobbled roads. The lord of Emberstran resides in a grand estate on a central hill that overlooks the entire port. Emberstran is protected by a high stone wall, while rural homes, vineyards, and farmland sprawl just beyond the city's boundaries.  


In Emberstran, the scent of salt and wine hangs in the air. The city rises against the coast, framed by the looming Severed Crease to the north and the distant slopes of Mount Emberstran. Outside its tall stone walls, rows of vineyards stretch across the fertile land, dotted with quaint farmsteads. The bustling Port District to the south side of the city hums with trade, fog drifting through tight alleys and along the piers, while the northern district gleams with fine homes and rising terraces. At the heart of it all, a grand estate of the lord sits on a hill.
History
Before the Awakening, Emberstran was known as Winbalt, a growing town nestled near the northeastern stretch of the forested area known as the Baltwood.  Life in Winbalt was humble, shaped by the resources of the nearby forest.
During the Awakening, a series of dramatic natural events reshaped the region. Mount Emberstran, long dormant to the northeast, erupted violently, scarring the landscape and displacing many in the surrounding foothills. Simultaneously, Glimmerstone Deep, a large inland lake to the south, surged and expanded into the newly formed Glimmering Sea, consuming much of the Baltwood Forest and transforming the coast.
As surrounding settlements were destroyed or made uninhabitable, refugees poured into Winbalt. The influx of people brought new trade, talent, and resources, and the town quickly expanded. Recognizing its transformation, the town renamed itself Emberstran—symbolizing both the fiery mark left by the volcano and its emergence as a beacon on the Glimmering Sea.
Over the decades, the city embraced its new identity. The volcanic soil enriched the land, birthing vineyards and agriculture that fueled the city’s prosperity. The port along the Glimmering Sea brought trade and opportunity, transforming Emberstran into a bustling coastal city that continues to grow and thrive in the post-Awakening era. 
Citizenry
Emberstran’s population is diverse, reflecting its status as a bustling port city that prospered after the Awakening. Humans make up the majority of the populace, but halflings, elves, and dwarves are a frequent sight. Tieflings, gnomes, goliaths, gabüks, and dragonborn also call the city home, though they are fewer in number and tend to stand out more in public spaces.
The majority of Emberstran’s residents fall into a middle-class lifestyle, with reliable work related to fishing, farming, wine production, trade, and craftsmanship. The port district contains most of the city’s lower-class citizens and a visible homeless population, especially near the docks and warehouses. In contrast, the elevated northern district boasts estates and refined homes for the city’s wealthiest families and influential figures. The city is generally welcoming, but tensions sometimes flare over class divides or the presence of uncommon races, depending on the neighborhood.
Holidays and Traditions
Emberfast
Each year on the first day of Vaegrum, the people of Emberstran celebrate Emberfast—a holiday marking the transformation of their humble town of Winbalt into the city of Emberstran. Citizens light hundreds of lanterns and fire basins along the walls and streets of the city, casting a warm glow to mirror the fire of Mount Emberstran. Bonfires are lit near the port, where storytelling contests are held about the “day the world changed.” Children wear painted ember masks and carry small bells to “awaken the spirit of the city,” while families share ash-cakes—flatbread charred just slightly at the edges, symbolic of survival and renewal.
The noble families host a Fire’s Ascent Ball, where attendees wear fiery hues, and dancers move in spirals to mimic the rising heat of rebirth. The Lord of Emberstran traditionally offers a speech atop the wall, recounting the legacy of Winbalt and honoring those lost during the Awakening.
Murkroot Eve
Murkroot Eve is a quiet, haunting festival that remembers the deep forests of the Baltwood, much of which was swallowed during the Awakening. Inspired by old stories from woodcutters and druids, this holiday is steeped in superstition and folkloric ritual. On the eve of the first full moon of Endelo’ar in Talil (the 27th), families gather branches of blackthorn or ash and hang them above their doors to keep away “the Murk,” a creeping forest spirit said to search for lost woods to reclaim.
Children don masks of bark, moss, or antlers and parade through the streets in eerie silence, while elders whisper old tales of spirits, dryads, and woodland trials. A common treat is rootcake, a dark, spiced cake baked with root vegetables, mushrooms, and sometimes a bitter herb thought to ward off ill omens.
In wealthier circles, nobles might hire performers to enact traditional Baltwood tales—like “The Birch Maiden Who Wept Blood” or “The Laughing Hart of Hollow Hill.” Some say strange lights can be seen in the sky or drifting across the Glimmering Sea on Murkroot Eve—and few dare to follow them.
Knock Twice for Vine’s Blessing
“Knock once, your wine turns bitter. Knock twice, the vintage flows.”
In winemaking circles, there’s a tradition of knocking twice on a barrel or cask before drawing wine from it, a superstition believed to bless the pour. Some vintners extend this to vineyard gates, wagons of grapes, or even glasses before a toast. It's said the ritual honors ancient vineyard spirits who made the land fertile after Mount Emberstran’s eruption.
Government
Emberstran is a free city governed by an elected official every six years, known as the Lord Protector or Lady Protector. As of the year 1246, the city is under the leadership of Lord Garod Dusklight, a pragmatic and charismatic figure known for balancing the city’s interests with minimal interference.
The Lord Protector is supported by a Council of Five, each responsible for a specific area of city governance:
* The Magistrate – Oversees legal disputes, criminal verdicts, and justice throughout the city.
* The Collector – Manages taxation, trade tariffs, and revenue collection for Emberstran.
* The Advisor – A knowledgeable scholar or mage who provides guidance on historical precedent, magical matters, and arcane affairs.
* The Minister – Handles public relations, religious counsel, and morale among the citizenry, often working with temples and spiritual leaders.
* The Hand – A shadowy but essential figure who maintains a web of informants throughout the city, particularly in the Port District, taverns, and trade networks.

Lord Garod Dusklight


Lord Garod Dusklight is a large man in fine silvery plate mail, black wavy hair swept back and a trimmed beard. Draped across his shoulders is a deep ember-colored cape.
Lord Garod Dusklight is a broad-shouldered, husky man in his middle years. His black wavy hair and full, well-trimmed beard give him a noble presence, complemented by the silver plate mail he often wears—even within city walls. His ember-colored cape marks his office as Lord Protector of Emberstran. Garod is known for his commanding voice, dignified bearing, and no-nonsense approach to leadership. He prefers decisive action and surrounds himself with a trusted council, though he keeps his own counsel close.
No City Militia
Emberstran has no standing army or formal city guard. Instead, the Lord Protector and three appointed captains hire and coordinate bands of mercenaries who enforce the law, patrol the districts, and defend against external threats. These hired swords operate under specific contracts and are rotated based on need and reliability.
Laws
Emberstran operates under a structured set of city ordinances, designed to maintain order and fairness within its growing and diverse population. The enforcement of law is handled by hired mercenaries, who serve under the direction of Lord Garod Dusklight and his three appointed Captains. These mercenaries patrol the city’s districts, maintain peace, and detain those suspected of criminal acts.  
Characters engaging in criminal activity are arrested by the hired mercenary patrol and charged per the City Ordinances.  When an individual is accused of breaking a law, they are not immediately sentenced. Instead, they are brought before an appointee of the Magistrate, who serves as the city’s arbiter of justice. These appointees—trained in local law and authorized to pass judgment—hold short hearings to determine guilt and punishment. Trials are often swift but fair, with more complex cases escalated to the Magistrate directly.
While bribes and influence occasionally sway minor decisions, the general populace sees the system as functional and efficient—especially when compared to cities without any central justice. In cases of repeat or severe offenses, public hearings may be held in the town square as a display of justice, and punishments can include hefty fines, exile, labor assignments, or imprisonment in the city’s fortified holding cells. 
Economy and Trade
Emberstran's economy thrives on its position as a coastal trade hub and its fertile volcanic soil, which makes the surrounding land ideal for vineyards. The city’s prosperity is supported by a wide variety of local businesses, skilled artisans, and merchants, but it is largely structured around three prominent trade guilds that dominate commerce and industry: the Vintner’s Guild, the Shipbuilders' Guild, and the Fisher's Consortium.
Professional Guilds
The Vintner’s Guild benefits from the rich, ash-fed soil around Mount Emberstran, producing wines that are in high demand across Myrdae. They manage vineyard operations, regulate wine quality, and oversee exports, wielding considerable influence over the local economy and seasonal festivals.
The Shipbuilders' Guild oversees the construction and repair of vessels at Emberstran’s shipyards, with designs tailored for both trade and fishing. Their craftsmanship is renowned across the Glimmering Sea, and their work ensures a steady flow of income from merchants and naval interests.
The Fisher’s Consortium maintains the city’s connection to the sea, organizing fishing routes, markets, and preservation practices. They control the majority of seafood trade in the region, supplying not only Emberstran but other settlements along the coast.
These guilds work in coordination with the city's leadership, paying dues and taxes that support infrastructure, defense, and public services. While independent trades still flourish, the guilds provide structure, employment, and economic strength, making them essential pillars in Emberstran’s thriving coastal economy.

Religion
The people of Emberstran honor a wide range of deities, and small shrines to nearly every god of Myrdae can be found tucked throughout the city. However, three gods stand above the rest in local devotion: Crael, Ossanna, and Diverra.
* Crael, the Soulbearer, is revered for his dominion over fate and death—an ever-present reality in a city once nearly destroyed by natural disaster. Many believe Crael’s guidance brought order from the chaos of the Awakening, and his worship is closely tied to Emberstran’s reverence for legacy, remembrance, and the delicate balance between life and death.
* Osanna, Lady Luck, is the goddess of fortune and chance. Her worship thrives in a city reliant on trade winds, open seas, and the ever-unpredictable flow of commerce. Merchants, gamblers, and travelers alike offer her tokens in the hopes of catching her favor—especially in the fog-veiled port district where fortune can shift as quickly as the tide.
* Diverra, the goddess of love and beauty, is celebrated in the vineyards and public gardens of the city, where her blessings are said to influence both fertile lands and hearts. Her presence is most strongly felt during the festival season, when wine flows freely and romantic tales blossom under the moonlight of the Glimmering Sea.

Notable Locations
Anchor Point
Anchor Point is a well-known and frequently visited inn and tavern located on the corner of the Grand Pier in Emberstran’s Docks. Its location offers a prime view of the harbor and serves as a hub for visiting sailors, merchants, and travelers awaiting transport across the Glimmering Sea. The tavern itself is a stout, square structure of salt-stained stone and heavy timber, with broad windows that look out toward the water. The inn extends in two wings—one west and one north—housing nearly two dozen modest but secure rooms. The establishment is run by a former sailor named Calro Tunn, a one-eyed half-elf known for fair prices and a strict policy against dockside brawls. The interior is often filled with the sounds of sea shanties, clinking mugs, and the scent of spiced stew. Anchor Point is a reliable location to gather rumors, overhear sailor gossip, or find passage to distant shores.


The scent of salt and spiced ale greets you as you approach Anchor Point, perched firmly on the edge of the Grand Pier. Its sturdy walls and broad windows overlook the harbor, offering a sweeping view of the ships bobbing in the harbor. Inside the tavern, the air is warm and lively. To the west and north, hallways stretch into the attached inn, where small but comfortable rooms offer a quiet place to sleep above the rhythm of the tides.

	Cemetery
Emberstran’s primary cemetery spans the elevated eastern side of the city. The grounds are well-kept and expansive, filled with rows of graves marked by simple stones, ornate headstones, and family plots. The eastern edge of the cemetery meets the tall stone city wall, while the rest is enclosed by a wrought-iron fence with stone pillars at regular intervals. Lining the perimeter are old, wind-bent trees that offer ample shade and an ever-present rustling sound in the breeze. On the northeastern and eastern sides stand three mausoleums—used by Emberstran’s wealthiest families and historically significant figures—crafted of dark stone and decorated with carvings of flame motifs and ancestral heraldry. The cemetery is often patrolled by hired caretakers, and while it is not officially consecrated, it is regarded as a respectful place where the dead are left undisturbed… most of the time.


The path bends upward to a quiet rise on Emberstran’s east side, where a broad cemetery stretches out. Iron fencing broken by moss-covered stone pillars wraps around most of the graveyard, while tall trees line the borders with rustling leaves that whisper in the sea breeze. Countless graves fill the grounds—some fresh, others ancient and crumbling. To the far side, near the city wall, rise three large mausoleums, their doors carved with intricate symbols.

	Charum’s Oils
Charum’s Oils is a refined bath house nestled in the western side of Emberstran. The street-level structure is circular, with elegant stonework. 
The bathhouse is run efficiently by Charum Iarra, supported by several well-trained attendants. Charum keeps a tight, tasteful operation, known for discretion and soothing service rather than indulgent vice.
The upper floor serves as a lounge and shop, offering an assortment of scented oils, balms, and perfumes made in-house. These oils are used by bathers after a soak to soften the skin, enhance one's natural scent, or impress others with rare and exotic fragrances. 
Inside, a spiral staircase leads down into a wide, naturally-formed cavern below the city where there is a series of three large bathing pools filled with warm water from a natural spring, each carved into the rock and maintained with care. Four private doors line the cavern walls, leading to smaller, more intimate sauna-like baths that require a separate fee. These rooms offer scented steam and enhanced privacy, often used by nobles or wealthy merchants.

	Church to Crael
The Silent Veil serves as the primary place of worship for the god Crael within Emberstran. It sits quietly in the Southeast District, mere steps west of the cemetery, allowing it to serve not only as a place of prayer but as a point of passage for those mourning or tending to the dead. The priests of Crael offer guidance to grieving families, oversee burial rites, and provide spiritual counsel to those contemplating fate, destiny, or the afterlife. City Gates
The Gates of Emberstran are the primary points of entry and regulation into the walled city. Each of the three gates are overseen by mercenary patrols contracted under Lord Garod Dusklight’s authority.  All gates are embedded into Emberstran’s tall stone walls and monitored by rotating bands of hired swords, who maintain order, inspect goods, and track the flow of people entering or exiting. Each gate has a gatehouse, where administrative tasks and minor disputes are handled.
* Western Gate: The busiest and most trafficked gate. It is the main entry point for caravans, travelers, and trade wagons arriving via inland roads. The nearby Headless Hydra Inn makes it a natural waypoint for newcomers and common folk. 
* Upper City Gate: This gate allows controlled access to Emberstran’s elevated northern district, where the city’s wealthiest citizens reside. Entry is highly regulated. It is often used by diplomats, noble families, and approved shipments bound for the noble quarter. Guards at this gate tend to be more experienced and better equipped, often handpicked by Captain Amilla Stassyr.
* Southern Gate: Facing the fertile vineyards and farmland, the Southern Gate is the quietest of the three but still essential for agricultural traffic and seasonal harvest movement. 
All three gates prominently display the Emberstran heraldry above the archways and on guard uniforms. Each is outfitted with an iron-reinforced portcullis, and during times of tension, additional bracing or magical wards may be applied to restrict access.

The Crusty Mug is one of the most popular and frequently visited pubs in southeastern Emberstran, known for its lively crowds, potent drinks, and layered atmosphere. The establishment spans three levels and caters to a wide variety of clientele—from rowdy dockhands and traders to quiet aristocrats looking for a less formal venue.

* Second Floor – A left-side staircase leads to the second floor, where a doorman collects a 5 silver piece access fee. This level features finer wooden furniture, wall-mounted lanterns with colored glass, cushioned seating, and better service. Musicians sometimes perform here, and the patrons are generally wealthier or seeking more privacy.

Access is restricted to those on the list or who know the right words. Velvet curtains, oil lamps, and padded chairs surround tables for dice, cards, and other games of chance.
The pub is operated by a savvy halfling named Redegar Digglepot, who knows how to cater to both the working class and high rollers. Imonorra’s Tower
Imonorra Asuun, the Lord’s Advisor, resides in a tall, four-story tower on the northeastern edge of Emberstran. Her tower mirrors the Lord’s Keep in construction—grey stone walls with black iron accents—but is notably more secluded, surrounded by a dense collection of aged trees and cultivated greenery that give the grounds an arcane serenity. The base of the tower includes a circular receiving chamber with a warded door, leading to spiral stairs that ascend to her study, library, observatory, and private quarters. Imonorra rarely descends into Emberstran, and entrance to the tower is by invitation only. The wizard is assisted by her young elven apprentice, Dathriel Runewish, and accompanied by her longtime familiar, Brunna, a sentient emberling fire elemental. Both maintain the tower’s operation and help manage Imonorra’s research, which spans subjects ranging from planar resonance to elemental channeling.


As you reach the northeastern edge of Emberstran, you spot a towering grey structure rising through a thick patch of trees. Imonorra’s tower, tall and slender with four distinct levels, looks much like the Lord’s Keep—dark stone and black iron detail—but its atmosphere is quieter, almost enchanted. The path to the arched entrance winds beneath lantern-lit boughs and old trees whose leaves cast moving shadows along the ground. The ironwork around the high windows twists into arcane shapes.
