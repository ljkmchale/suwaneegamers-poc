Ahndashere

# Overview

Ahndashere sits where the Northern Stoneflow River empties into the northern coast of the Glimmering Sea. What began as a marsh village before the Awakening has grown into a modest town. The expansion of the Glimmering Sea forced the people to adapt quickly, further securing their homes on stilts and weaving boardwalks above the mire. In time, this necessity drew trade from riverboats and coastal vessels alike.

The town has an air of rugged practicality. Its stilted structures and swaying rope bridges span over brackish waters, with livelihoods tied as much to the swamp as to the sea. Reed cutters, crabbers, salt workers, and sailors all carve out their place here, while smugglers and fortune-seekers move quietly through the channels. Old marsh traditions mingle uneasily with the newer seafaring faiths, as those who honor Villari and Muerg share space with worshippers of Coralei or Osanna. 

Though small compared to cities along the Glimmering Sea, Ahndashere holds a certain rough charm. Its winding causeways, brine-soaked air, and glow of marsh lanterns at night make it both dangerous and welcoming.

## 

## As you approach Ahndashere, the first impression is of two worlds bound together. On the low banks, stilted houses perch above reed-choked channels, their boardwalks zigzagging over the water. But at the town’s heart, green hills rise above the mire, crowned with burrows, gardens, and the steady line of the main road that carries travelers safely through. Boats rock gently at the docks below while smoke curls from hillside chimneys above, and lanterns flicker in the gathering mist.

## History

Before the Awakening reshaped the northern coast, Ahndashere was a humble marsh village clustered around a stone shrine to Torec, the old god of elements. The people who lived there believed the meeting of river and swamp was one of Torec’s chosen convergences. For generations, the villagers relied on his blessing to weather storms, floods, and the restless spirits of the swamp.

When the Glimmering Sea surged inland during the Awakening, the marshes deepened and the shoreline swallowed much of the old village. Yet the rise of solid hills in the heart of the settlement remained untouched by floodwaters. The villagers rebuilt upon stilts and boardwalks where they had to, while many settled into the dry hills, digging burrows and planting gardens.  Ahndashere grew into a small but enduring town as people from the land swallowed by the expansion of the Glimmering Sea came to settle.

## Citizenry

The people of Ahndashere are a blend of swamp-dwellers, riverfolk, and hillstead halflings. Those who live on the boardwalks and stilts tend toward hard pragmatism—fishermen with weathered skin, reed-cutters with calloused hands, and pilots who know every shifting channel of the delta. Life here has taught them caution, patience, and a streak of suspicion toward outsiders, though visitors who prove honest and hardworking quickly find welcome at their tables.

The halflings of the central hills bring a touch of stability and warmth to the settlement. Their gardens and burrows form neat, green neighborhoods that echo the traditions of their homeland, and their presence lends Ahndashere a sense of permanence amid the shifting waters. Known for their hospitality, they often host festivals and communal meals that draw even the swamp-folk to higher ground. Together, the two communities—marsh and hill—make up the heart of Ahndashere.

## Holidays and Traditions

Ahndashere’s calendar is marked less by grand festivals and more by seasonal rites tied to survival, trade, and community.   Among the halfling hills, the Harvest Rest is observed each autumn stretch. The community gathers in gardens and burrow-halls for shared meals of roasted root vegetables, smoked eel, and spiced marsh-carrot bread. It is both thanksgiving and covenant, reminding all that the hills and swamps must provide together if the town is to endure the winter floods.

Smaller customs bind the people day-to-day. Boatmen toss a pebble into the Stoneflow before setting out, a habit traced to Torec’s old shrine. Traders hang ribbon charms from their carts as they cross the main road, seeking Osanna’s favor. And when storms rise, it is tradition for all lanterns in the town—on stilts, in gardens, and along the road—to be lit at once, a sign of unity against the elements.

## Government

Ahndashere’s government is guided not by bloodline or popular vote, but by the wisdom of an elder who appoints the settlement’s leader every six years. Since the Awakening, that role has been held by Myranda Fenberry, a halfling shaman revered as both oracle and caretaker of tradition. Her presence is seen as the voice of the town’s spirit, and her pronouncements are rarely questioned.

The leader she names is formally recognized as Lord or Lady of Ahndashere. While some have been reappointed across multiple terms, the decision always rests with the town elder, who claims to see the shape of leadership in dreams and omens. Four years ago, she surprised the town by appointing Hommof Tsuldor, a young fisherman with little political background. Hommof governs by adhering to the simple, practical laws first codified by the elder council many years ago, relying on tradition rather than ambition. He maintains order through a small guard force led by a captain, who answers directly to him, though the elder also shapes his decisions.

### **Elder Myranda Fenberry**

Myranda is calm, deliberate, and cryptic, speaking in layered sayings that often feel more like riddles than direct commands. She has been elder since the Awakening and is regarded as the town’s true spiritual anchor, though she prefers to guide from the shadows rather than rule directly. Myranda rarely raises her voice, yet when she speaks, her words carry the weight of prophecy, and even the guards fall silent to listen. She values balance and tradition, respects the old powers of Torec, and believes Ahndashere endures because its people accept the roles given to them. 

## 

## Myranda, now just over a century old, wears her age with dignity—her long curly white hair often woven with flowers from the gardens of the hill burrows, her crooked staff adorned with small bones and feathers that clatter softly when she walks.

### **Lord Hommof Tsuldor**

Hommof is earnest and plainspoken, more at ease with nets and boats than with titles or politics. He governs with humility, often leaning on the old rules and Myranda Fenberry’s counsel rather than asserting his own authority. To townsfolk he is approachable and fair, though some see him as inexperienced; to outsiders he is cautious but not unkind, always weighing whether they come to help or to take advantage. Hommof respects tradition, values honesty, and quietly carries the burden of being chosen for a role he never sought, determined not to let the town down.

## 

## Hommof Tsuldor is a broad-shouldered young fisherman with sun-browned skin and hands calloused from years on the nets. His dark hair is tied back with a strip of sailcloth, and he wears simple clothes of roughspun linen, still smelling faintly of river and brine. 

### **Captain Hester Rinby**

Hester takes his duty seriously, but not so seriously that he forgets the people he serves. He is approachable, practical, and fair-minded, often choosing to hand out warnings for small offenses rather than immediate punishment—unless the same face comes before him too many times. To townsfolk, he is both respected and well-liked, though some see his leniency as weakness. With outsiders, Hester is polite but watchful, keen to gauge intentions before trust is granted. Beneath his calm demeanor, he carries a deep sense of loyalty to the community, and he is willing to stand his ground firmly when threats arise.

## 

## Captain Hester Rinby is a sturdy, middle-aged man with weathered features and a trimmed beard gone mostly to gray. His leather jerkin bears the scuffs of long use, and he carries himself with the steady confidence of someone who knows every boardwalk, dock, and back alley of Ahndashere. A short sword rests at his side, though his easy smile and measured tone suggest he prefers words over steel when he can.

## Laws

The people of Ahndashere do not keep thick tomes of law or endless scrolls of decrees. Instead, their way is preserved in sayings—words first spoken by the elder who guided the town through the Awakening, repeated by every Lord and Lady since. The guards and their captain enforce them, but all know the words by heart.

* “*Keep the water clean and the hills green, for both are our lifeblood.*” To foul the river, swamp, or hillside gardens is to wound the town itself.  
* “*The elder’s voice is the river’s course.*” None may dispute the elder’s appointment of a Lord or Lady, for as the river flows, so too must leadership.  
* “*The road must stay clear, the docks must stay open.*” Trade keeps Ahndashere alive, and those who block its flow risk the hunger of all.  
* “*When danger comes, no one stands alone.*” Every hand is called to defend the town, whether against beast, storm, or blade.  
* “*Deal honest measure.*” In coin, in catch, in craft—let no one cheat their neighbor, for distrust rots faster than swampwood.  
* “*Blood only by cause, never by spite.*” Quarrels are to be judged, not slaughtered. Murder brings exile or the headsman’s axe.  
* “*Honor the dead, and leave what is theirs to rest.*” Graves, shrines, and drowned relics are not for plunder. To disturb them is to invite Torec’s wrath.

When punishment is needed, it is as practical as the people themselves: fines, labor, exile, or in the darkest cases, death. In rare disputes where words of law falter, the town still looks to Myranda Fenberry, the elder, whose judgment is said to carry the voice of the elements themselves.

## Economy and Trade

Ahndashere’s economy rests on the meeting of swamp, sea, and hill. The town’s earliest lifeblood came from the marsh: eel traps, crab pots, reeds cut for weaving, and peat dug from the bog. Even as the Glimmering Sea expanded and reshaped the land, these trades endured, providing the staples that still support much of the town’s daily life. Salt-boiling became an early craft, with brine drawn from the sea and boiled in great iron pans to preserve fish and flavor food. 

The rise of the hills at Ahndashere’s heart is a second foundation to its trade. Here the gardens of herbs, root vegetables, and small grain plots provide a reliable supply of food where the swamp cannot. Their gardens are famed for marsh-adapted produce, such as peppery watercress and sweet marsh carrots, which travel well and fetch fair prices. 

The Northern Stoneflow and the coastal road make Ahndashere a minor but essential hub for travelers. River pilots ferry goods upstream while coastal traders stop to resupply. Smugglers, too, take advantage of the twisting channels, slipping contraband into the town’s markets under the guise of honest catch. 

## Religion

Faith in Ahndashere is shaped by its history—rooted in the elemental balance once honored through Torec, but carried forward into the worship of the New Order. In the heart of the town, upon the rise of the hills, stands the weathered Shrine of Torec: a circle of stone markers once tended by priests of the old god of elements. Though nobody actively worships Torec today, the shrine remains a place of quiet reflection. 

The people now look more often to the New Gods, whose mall shrines and altars are scattered throughout the town. Coralei, the Tide Keeper, receives devotion from sailors, pilots, and fisherfolk who depend on the Glimmering Sea’s bounty. Osanna, the Smiling Duchess, is honored by merchants and travelers who seek luck on the road or fortune at market. Those tied to the land and its gardens turn to Villari, the Dawn Princess, whose blessings of growth and renewal sustain the halfling hillstead, while some marsh-dwellers whisper prayers to Muerg, the Earth Father, for strength and endurance against storm or flood.
