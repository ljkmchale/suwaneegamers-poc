# **Overview**

The Abbey of Mont Rest, now commonly referred to as the Abbey of Light, is a sanctuary located south of O’naren, sitting at the summit of a tall foothill descending from the Distancion Mountains into the Borealian Sway. Originally dedicated to the old god Neera, the abbey has since expanded its purpose, becoming a place of study and reverence for all the gods of Myrdae. Built from the region’s natural stone, the abbey has stood for approximately 150 years, housing both devoted scholars of divine lore and an orphanage that provides care for abandoned or displaced children.

A unique and unexplained arrangement exists between the abbey’s inhabitants and a group of Stone Giants who reside in the surrounding foothills. These giants not only aided in the abbey’s original construction but have since remained as its quiet but unwavering guardians. While the specifics of this relationship remain unknown, the bond between the abbey and its giant protectors has endured, ensuring the safety of the site from external threats. Rumors persist that the giants may have been granted some ancient favor, but none within the abbey claim to know the truth. 

The abbey itself is a grand structure, featuring an expansive central hall for worship and study, cloisters for the resident scholars and monks, and a walled courtyard where the orphans play and receive their education. While peaceful, the abbey is no stranger to intrigue—strange travelers, ancient relics, and whispered prophecies all find their way here, drawing seekers of knowledge and faith alike.

## **History**

The Abbey of Light, once known as the Abbey of Mont Rest, was founded roughly one hundred and fifty years ago at the edge of the Distancion Mountains. Built atop a high foothill south of O’naren, its original purpose was as a sanctuary dedicated to the old goddess Neera, patron of wisdom and memory. Constructed from the pale stone of the mountains themselves, the abbey quickly became more than a shrine. In its early years, a group of Stone Giants from the surrounding peaks lent their immense strength to its construction, forging a bond of protection that has endured ever since. Though the nature of this pact remains shrouded in mystery, the giants have stood sentinel for generations, deterring threats both mundane and supernatural.

As the worship of the Old Gods gave way to the New Order, the abbey expanded its devotion. Rather than consecrate itself to a single deity, it embraced all gods of Myrdae, becoming a center of study, diplomacy, and care. The abbey soon grew into four distinct Orders—the Nurturing, Reflective, Protective, and Rising Light—each dedicated to a facet of divine service, from tending orphans and the sick to safeguarding relics and guiding rituals. This structure allowed the abbey to thrive as both sanctuary and scholarly center.

## **Citizenry**

Predominately high elves, humans, and half-elves form its heart, with other species—dwarves, halflings, gnomes, tieflings, and the occasional half-orc or dragonborn—making up roughly a quarter of the residents. 

Almost everyone in the Abbey of Light is tied into its work, prayer and study. The majority are initiated into one of the Four Orders:

* **The Nurturing Light –** The Nurturing Light are caretakers of the orphanage and healers of the body and spirit.  This order oversees the orphanage, infirmary, gardens, and communal kitchens.  
* **The Protective Light –** The Order of Protective Light are defenders of the Abbey, guardians of sacred relics, and enforcers of spiritual boundaries.  This order oversees guarding the people, the sanctum vaults and the outer walls.  
* **The Reflective Light –** The Order of Reflective Light  are scholars, diplomats, and mediators — quietly influential thinkers who support causes like the Tegenwald Phet.  This order oversees the archives, lecture halls, civic correspondence, and public doctrine.  
* **The Rising Light –** The Order of Rising Light are leaders of ritual, memory, and faith — shepherds of transitions and sacred oaths.  This order oversees the ceremonial halls and leading services.

Alongside them, the **Monks of Lighted Breath** carry out their distinct discipline, marrying martial practice with the healing and transformative power of herbs and breath. The Monks of Lighted Breath are a unique tradition within the Abbey of Light, embodying the balance of mercy, discipline, and healing through the mastery of breath and herbcraft. Their philosophy teaches that each inhalation draws in life, while each exhalation carries away suffering; in this rhythm lies the power to restore the body and still the spirit. These monks cultivate and prepare herbs—burned in braziers, brewed into teas, or smoked in pipes—as tools for focus, meditation, and healing. 

A vibrant portion of the abbey’s citizenry is its orphanage, overseen by the Nurturing Light. Children from across the Borealian Sway—those left parentless by famine, plague, or conflict—are raised here with education, guidance, and care. Many eventually take vows within one of the Orders, ensuring the abbey’s future is seeded with its own. 

### **Abbey Attire and Vestments**

The people of the Abbey of Light are easily recognized by their simple yet dignified attire. Most wear garments in soft shades of beige woven from linen and flax fiber. Robes and loose-fitting tunics are the most common, chosen for comfort during long hours of study, meditation, and labor. Belts, boots, and pouches are typically of dark brown or rusty-crimson leather, their practical tones grounding the lighter fabrics in the dust and stone hues of the mountain.

While all share this humble foundation, each of the Four Orders—and the Monks of Lighted Breath—display their own distinct accents, allowing their purpose within the Abbey to be recognized at a glance:

* The Nurturing Light wears dark brown accents or sashes, symbolizing the soil and stability of compassion. Their garments are often reinforced or layered for practical work in the gardens, infirmary, or orphanage.  
* The Reflective Light favors white accents or sashes, representing clarity, truth, and the mirrored light of understanding. Their robes tend to be kept spotless, with subtle embroidery marking their scholarly status.  
* The Protective Light bears rusty-crimson accents or sashes, echoing the color of iron and sacrifice. Members wear heavier fabrics suited for patrols and guard duty, with their sashes often doubled as arm wraps or cinched over cloaks.  
* The Rising Light adorn themselves with golden-yellow accents or sashes, symbolizing enlightenment and divine illumination. Their garments are formal and flowing, suited to ritual and ceremony, and often trimmed with faint gold thread.  
* The Monks of Lighted Breath stand apart in golden-toned attire, wearing knee-length robes over loose, brown baggy pants that taper toward the feet and are wrapped from calf to ankle with linen bindings. Their simplicity and freedom of movement reflect their path of balance and discipline.

## **Holidays and Traditions**

The Abbey of Light has two stand out observances: the Children’s Day of Dawning, which celebrates renewal and the orphans raised within the abbey’s care, and the Silent Breath, a harmon-wide practice of stillness and unity led by the Monks of Lighted Breath.

### **Children’s Day of Dawning**

This spring festival is one of the most beloved traditions of the Abbey of Light, dedicated to the orphans raised within its walls. Overseen by the Nurturing Light, the day begins with simple plays and songs, often retelling stories of the gods in bright, hopeful tones. Games, dances, and storytelling follow, with pilgrims and monks alike joining in celebration. A feast is laid in the Refectory of Warmth, where children are given the first servings as honored guests. 

### **The Silent Breath**

Once per harmon, the Monks of Lighted Breath lead the abbey in a day of silence, known as the Silent Breath. On this day, speech is set aside in favor of mindful breathing, meditation, and reflection. The monks burn fragrant herbs in braziers throughout the cloisters and halls, filling the abbey with calming smoke that encourages focus and tranquility. For the monks, it is both a renewal of their vows and a reminder of life’s balance; for the wider abbey, it is a moment to still the constant motion of service and devotion, returning to the simple rhythm of breath that binds all beings.

## **Government**

The abbey recognizes no worldly lord, though it maintains respectful ties with nearby settlements and the Elle’berian crown. Its autonomy is preserved by the presence of the Stone Giants and its spiritual standing as a neutral sanctuary devoted to all gods. Even kings tread carefully when dealing with the abbey, for its influence stretches through faith, scholarship, and diplomacy alike.

The Abbey of Light is not ruled by a single abbot or abbess but instead operates under the shared guidance of its Four Orders: the Nurturing, Reflective, Protective, and Rising Light. Each Order governs a distinct domain of abbey life. Together they form a balanced council that shapes the abbey’s direction.

### **Stone Giant Pact**

The Stone Giants of the Distancion foothills have stood as silent protectors of the Abbey of Light since its founding. Their immense strength aided in the abbey’s construction, and though the nature of their pact with the clergy has never been fully understood, the bond has endured for generations. The giants do not enter the abbey itself, preferring to keep their long watch from the surrounding hills and high paths, but their presence is unmistakable: towering silhouettes at dawn and dusk, carved runes etched into nearby stones, and the steady tread of their patrols echoing through the valleys. 

## **Laws**

The Abbey of Light is governed not by civic statutes or royal decrees but by a code of conduct rooted in its spiritual mission. These laws, upheld by the Four Orders and the Monks of Lighted Breath, are intended to preserve harmony, safeguard the vulnerable, and ensure that the abbey remains a place of sanctuary. Enforcement falls primarily to the Protective Light, though each Order has authority within its own domain.

1. **Sanctity of Sanctuary –** The abbey is a place of refuge. Violence, bloodshed, or pursuit of vengeance are not welcome within its walls. Those who seek shelter—even fugitives—are afforded temporary protection until their case can be weighed by the council of Orders.  
2. **Oath of Truth –** Deception within abbey business is treated as sacrilege. All who speak in council, archives, or ceremony must give honest word. Those found guilty of lies or false witness face exile from the abbey’s grounds and loss of all privileges of sanctuary.  
3. **Balance of Service –** Every resident—whether orphan, scholar, or monk—must contribute to the abbey’s welfare through labor, study, or devotion. Neglect of duties is corrected first through guidance, then penance, and only lastly through dismissal from the community.  
4. **Reverence of Relics –** Sacred relics and archives are deemed inviolable. Unauthorized access is punishable by confinement, fasting penance, or expulsion, depending on severity.  
5. **Mercy in Judgment –** When discipline is required, punishment is guided by the principle of mercy. The Nurturing Light advises on compassion, the Reflective Light on precedent, the Protective Light on enforcement, and the Rising Light on ritual. Together, they render decisions that aim at correction and restoration rather than cruelty.  
6. **Guardians’ Accord –** The bond with the Stone Giants is sacred. Any attempt to harm, deceive, or exploit the giants is considered treason against the abbey and punished with permanent banishment. 

## **Economy and Trade**

The Abbey of Light sustains itself through a blend of self-sufficiency, donations, and carefully managed trade. Its gardens, orchards, and fields provide much of the daily fare, while herds in the foothills and preserved stores in the mead cellar ensure stability through the seasons. Pilgrims contribute alms and offerings, and noble patrons often send tithes in recognition of the abbey’s spiritual authority. A long-standing arrangement with the Bathaen Empire ensures a steady supply of foodstuffs, clothing, and learning materials for the orphanage—support that both eases the abbey’s burden and maintains goodwill between the abbey and the empire.

The monks of the Lighted Breath have become a quiet but vital contributor to the abbey’s economy. They cultivate and prepare herbs, blending them into teas, poultices, and smokes that are sought across the Borealian Sway. Their remedies ease pain, mend wounds, calm anxious spirits, and aid meditation, making them prized by pilgrims, merchants, and nobles alike. Alongside these goods, the monks also sell simple incense sticks, prayer candles infused with herbs, and salves known for their restorative qualities. Though their focus is mercy and healing, the trade of their craft provides a steady stream of income.

Beyond goods, the abbey offers services. Scholars of the Reflective Light are often asked to copy or preserve records, while the Nurturing Light extends healing to travelers and pilgrims in exchange for donations. The Protective Light occasionally escorts caravans through dangerous routes, their presence seen as both practical protection and a blessing. These activities are carefully managed, for the abbey seeks not to enrich itself but to sustain its mission: caring for the orphaned, preserving knowledge, and serving as a sanctuary of light in the Borealian Sway.

## **Religion**

The Abbey of Light was founded in devotion to Neera of the Old Gods, but over the last couple of decades it has become a sanctuary for the worship of all deities of Myrdae.  The Great Hall of Radiance houses alcoves to many gods, allowing pilgrims, monks, and clergy to honor their chosen faith while still standing in shared fellowship.  Services and ceremonies are conducted in tones of reverence rather than dogma, with the belief that divine light—however it is named—shines upon all.

The Four Orders each interpret faith through their own lens: the Nurturing Light through acts of compassion, the Reflective Light through doctrine and diplomacy, the Protective Light through vigilance against corruption, and the Rising Light through sacred rites and oaths. Alongside them, the Monks of Lighted Breath embody religion in practice rather than scripture, seeking harmony through breath, herbcraft, and merciful discipline.

## **Cloister**

The Cloister of the Abbey of Light lies on a broad, level terrace on the western side of the abbey grounds, enclosed within the outer stone walls. Once a space for scholars and monks to study in quiet contemplation, it has grown into the rhythmic heart of daily abbey life. The quadrangle is surrounded by covered walkways supported by weathered columns, their stone bases carved with symbols of the gods. At the center lies a manicured garden where white lilies, mountain sage, and climbing vines thrive among marble benches and a circular reflecting pool.

During the day, the Cloister is filled with the soft sounds of study and teaching. Members of the Reflective Light conduct lessons here, while the orphans are taught letters, history, and scripture beneath the shade of the arcades. The space is also a common thoroughfare—monks and pilgrims alike pass through between prayer bells, their footsteps echoing softly along the stone paths. At night, lanterns illuminate the covered walkways, and the garden becomes a place for reflection and silent prayer beneath the stars.

The Cloister is one of the few spaces where all of the abbey’s Orders intersect. It is neutral ground for conversation, debate, and counsel. While it feels peaceful, observant travelers may notice subtle divisions—groups of monks and clerics gathering separately according to their Order, or the Reflective Light lingering longer in shadowed corners with quiet words.

## **Great Hall of Radiance**

The Great Hall of Radiance stands at the summit of the Abbey of Light, its pale spires rising above every roofline like the upturned hands of prayer. It is the heart of the Abbey—its central sanctuary and gathering place—where worship, study, and sacred ceremony converge. The hall was built on the highest stone terrace to catch the first light of dawn and the last light of dusk.

The hall’s main doors, heavy oak banded with gold-etched iron, open into the nave, a long and vaulted expanse lined with polished stone columns. The floor is a mosaic of interwoven patterns. To either side, narrow alcoves honor the many gods of the pantheon—each with its own offering bowl and candle stands. The air smells faintly of oil and myrrh, with shafts of colored light streaming through tall, stained-glass windows that tell the stories of creation, sacrifice, and renewal.

At the far end of the nave lies the crossing, where the transepts meet—two great arched wings extending to the right and left. Each transept houses a smaller altar chamber, used for devotion to particular aspects of divine light: one dedicated to healing and compassion, the other to protection and judgment. Straight ahead, opposite the nave, rises the main altar, a simple slab of white marble polished to a mirror sheen. Behind it curves the apse, a semicircular recess that glows faintly even in darkness, its domed ceiling painted with a radiant sun surrounded by thirteen stars—one veiled in shadow.

To the left of the apse, a narrow door leads into a circular stairwell descending beneath the hall into the Solstice Chamber, used for rites tied to celestial alignment. To the right, another stair chamber ascends the Bell Tower of Dawn, a tall spire crowned with a silver bell that marks the harmon’s opening and close.

The Great Hall is overseen by the Rising Light, whose members guide rituals, preside over ceremonies, and interpret omens. While pilgrims and clerics alike see the hall as a place of peace, subtle power flows beneath its serenity. The Rising Light guards ancient texts and the knowledge of celestial conjunctions, and the hall’s architecture itself is said to amplify divine energy on certain days of the year. The space can hold several hundred people during worship, and on solstice evenings, the light from the apse and windows merges into a blinding brilliance that fills the entire chamber—a phenomenon locals call “the Breath of the Dawn.”

## **Rising Hearthlight**

Located within the Abbey of Light’s inner walls, just a short walk from the Great Hall of Radiance, the Rising Hearthlight serves as a warm refuge for pilgrims, travelers, and visiting scholars permitted temporary stay within the abbey grounds. Once a residence hall for wandering sages, it has since been repurposed into a communal lodge where weary visitors can rest beneath the abbey’s protection.

The hall’s construction reflects its age — thick mountain stone, heavy timber beams, and soot-darkened hearths give the place a rustic endurance that contrasts with the abbey’s grander sanctuaries. The great hearth dominates the common room, its flames rising high enough to light the timbered ceiling in a golden glow. Long tables line the floor, where monks, travelers, and orphans often share meals.

Hospitality at the Rising Hearthlight is governed by quiet generosity: food and drink are freely offered, though they vary by season and availability. ## **Warden’s Quarters**

The Warden’s Quarters stands beside the House of Protective Light. The building is sturdier and broader than most, constructed from the same pale brown stone but reinforced with heavier beams and iron bracing around its doors and windows. A short set of steps leads up to the main entrance, while another stairway descends to a lower level that houses the Abbey’s holding cells—used rarely, and only in cases where sanctuary is tested or law is broken.

Inside, the main floor opens into a wide chamber serving as both residence and command hall. To the right stands a circular oak table surrounded by high-backed chairs, used for meetings of the Protective Light and strategic discussions with the Abbey council. The room bears the marks of long service: weapon racks neatly maintained, lanterns burning low, and maps and rosters stacked along the walls. Hanging above the hearth is a portrait of the current Warden’s father, a gift from the House of Reflective Light upon his passing. The resemblance between father and son is unmistakable.

The hearth itself bears several trophies from the Warden’s past campaigns: a massive fang, a jagged black spike, and a broken blade displayed upon a simple mount. None of these are displayed boastfully; rather, they appear to serve as solemn symbols of sacrifice and vigilance.

To the left of the main room are three private chambers—one for the Warden and two for his closest guards, who serve as both protectors and advisors. Their quarters are simple but well-kept, each containing a cot, a chest, and a narrow window. 

Below, the holding cells occupy a reinforced stone chamber accessed through a separate stair and a heavy iron door. The space is dimly lit by lanterns recessed into the walls, with a handful of barred cells built to contain prisoners or those seeking penitence under the Abbey’s authority.
