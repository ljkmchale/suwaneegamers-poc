## About Ys'Nonterien

Ys’Nonterien, the City of Five Chasms, is a drow settlement on the northern shore of Lake Rothenloch in the Upper Underdark. The entire city occupies a single vast obsidian cavern whose jagged walls and ceiling rise hundreds of feet. Five sheer chasms slice the floor into sectors ruled by the Five Houses, connected only by hardened-resin web bridges maintained by Rothenspinner handlers. One chasm opens directly into the black waters of the lake. It is famed for producing the finest magically responsive Rothenspinner silk in the Underdark, often woven with latent enchantments or venomous threads. Graceful spires and balconies of dark stone rise beneath banners of silver and violet, illuminated by pale violet crystals. Visitors require formal sponsorship from one of the Five Houses; the Silent Guard eliminates the unsponsored without hesitation. Beneath the elegant surface lies constant tension from House rivalries.

### **The People**

Society is rigidly stratified under the Five Houses. Priestesses and matrons form the elite ruling tier and control the Matron Council. Skilled artisans, warriors, Rothenspinner handlers, weavers, and the Silent Guard occupy the middle layers. Lower-status drow perform most labor and support roles. Non-drow residents never exceed ten to fifteen percent and consist almost entirely of slaves and servants—primarily svirfneblin and duergar—who tend fungal groves, feed and groom Rothenspinners, clean loom-halls, and haul lake water. These outsiders are forbidden from crossing the web bridges without escort. A tiny number of sponsored non-drow merchants are tolerated under strict House supervision. 

### **Beliefs**

Ys’Nonterien is a theocratic society in which true power rests with the priestesses of “She Who Weaves in Shadow.” These priestesses hold veto authority over any Matron Council decree and interpret omens through spider-silk auguries and temple rituals. The Five Houses govern through the Matron Council, yet open warfare is forbidden by the Covenant of Five Blades. Rivalries therefore manifest through proxy conflicts, sabotage, poisonings, and ritual duels. 

### **Economy and Trade**

The economy of Ys’Nonterien is built entirely on the export of Rothenspinner silk, the finest and most magically responsive silk in the Underdark. This silk is frequently woven with latent enchantments or venom-infused threads. 

### **Traditions**

The drow of Ys’Nonterien observe regular sacrifices to Eredra, the Spider Queen. Each of the Five Houses selects at least one sacrifice per harmon and performs the rite on its own terms and according to its preferred methods. These sacrifices form the primary and most consistent religious tradition practiced within the city. Beyond these observances, the drow of Ys’Nonterien maintain no other widely shared holidays or traditions.

# Dungeon Master’s Reference

Ys’Nonterien is a mid-sized drow settlement of roughly 5,000 souls clinging to the northern shore of Lake Rothenloch in the Upper Underdark. The entire city occupies a single, unnaturally clean obsidian cavern whose jagged walls and ceiling rise hundreds of feet with no stalactites or stalagmites to break the vertical menace. Five sheer chasms slice the cavern floor into five distinct sectors, each claimed by one of the five ruling Houses. One chasm opens directly into the black waters of Lake Rothenloch, allowing cold lake water to flow through all five at a constant 20-foot depth and creating deadly drop-offs along every inter-house border. Hardened-resin web bridges, maintained by Rothenspinner handlers, span the chasms and serve as the only safe crossings.

The city is governed by a Matron Council of the Five Houses, but true authority rests with the priestesses of the settlement’s dark goddess, whose temples and rituals overshadow even the matrons in matters of doctrine and punishment. Ys’Nonterien’s wealth and fame come from its Rothenspinner silk — the finest, most magically responsive silk in the Underdark, often woven with latent enchantments or venomous threads. Visitors are never welcome without sponsorship from one of the Five Houses; uninvited surface-dwellers or rival drow are dealt with swiftly by the Silent Guard. Beneath the surface elegance lies a powder-keg of House rivalries, secret pacts, and looming catastrophe that the party should only uncover through careful play.

Tread carefully; the webs that hold the city together are stronger than they appear, but they tremble at the slightest misstep.

## History

Ys’Nonterien began not as a fortress of drow faith and silk, but as a secretive research enclave founded in the early years after the Fracturing (circa Year 50). A cabal of exiled drow mages and priestesses from deeper Underdark cities claimed the northern shore of Lake Rothenloch, drawn by its unusually stable ley-lines and the rich fungal groves that supported a native strain of massive, silk-producing spiders they named Rothenspinners. The original settlement was a modest complex of obsidian spires and fungal terraces built around a single natural chasm that fed fresh lake water into the cavern. For nearly two centuries the enclave remained neutral — a place of quiet study where scholars experimented with living silk weaves, resonance magic, and controlled breeding of the Rothenspinners for both industry and defense.

All of that changed in the Year of Blood (Year 219). A bloody coup, orchestrated by five ambitious lesser houses, shattered the enclave in a single night of ritual assassination and shadow-magic. The victors — the ancestors of today’s Five Houses — seized control, executed or exiled the original researchers, and deliberately widened the single natural chasm into five perfectly symmetrical fissures using forbidden earth-shaping rituals and enslaved umber hulk labor. Each fissure was claimed by one house as its sovereign sector, with the central chasm deliberately deepened and connected directly to the lake to serve as both a defensive moat and a source of fresh water. The web bridges were woven in the first decade after the coup, their resin hardened by alchemical baths derived from Rothenspinner venom — the same process that would later become the city’s greatest secret and export.

With the chasms carved and the houses entrenched, the new rulers turned their full attention to the Rothenspinners. What had once been wild cave spiders were selectively bred over generations into the semi-sapient war-beasts and silk-livestock that now define Ys’Nonterien. House Yerenduhl took the lead in husbandry, developing the pheromone-laced “command silk” that allows near-telepathic control. By the Year of Convergence (Year 1229\) the city had grown into its modern form, its wealth built entirely on the export of enchanted Rothenspinner silk. The priestesses of the dark goddess rose to prominence during this period, claiming divine mandate over the Matron Council and cementing the theocratic hierarchy that rules today.

The scars of the coup and the forced creation of the chasms still linger in the obsidian walls — faint runes of binding magic pulse faintly at the chasm rims when the moons above Oberra align in Brutali. House rivalries, the secret aboleth pact of House Do’Aethir, and Inidra Maerth’s shadow-colossus ritual are the direct legacies of that violent rebirth. The city’s history is therefore one of transformation: from scholarly haven to silk empire to powder-keg theocracy, all built on the backs of conquered spiders, carved stone, and rivers of spilled drow blood.

## Citizenry

The population of Ys’Nonterien is approximately 5,000 residents, of which at least 80 % are drow born and raised within the city’s five chasms. Drow society here is rigidly stratified: priestesses and matrons of the Five Houses form the elite upper tier (perhaps 10–12 % of the total population), skilled artisans, warriors, and Rothenspinner handlers make up the middle layers (roughly 30–35 %), and the remainder are lower-status drow — laborers, weavers, guards, scribes, and the Silent Guard enforcers.

Non-drow residents are overwhelmingly servants, slaves, or indentured laborers and never exceed 20 % of the population (typically 600–750 individuals at any given time). The vast majority of these are svirfneblin or duergar captured in raids, purchased from slavers, or born into servitude within the city. They are housed in cramped barracks or sub-level warrens beneath each House’s sector, perform menial labor (cleaning loom-halls, tending fungal groves, hauling water from the lake, feeding and grooming Rothenspinners), and are forbidden from crossing chasm bridges without explicit escort or orders. Escape attempts are rare and invariably fatal — the Silent Guard and the Rothenspinners themselves make short work of runaways.

A tiny handful of non-drow merchants (perhaps 8–15 individuals at most) have earned conditional tolerance through long-standing trade relationships or exceptional usefulness (supplying rare surface dyes, metals, or spell components that even House Maerth cannot replicate). These outsiders are always accompanied by a House-sponsored escort, confined to designated market balconies near the lake chasm, and expected to depart within hours of concluding business. Any merchant who overstays, attempts to linger, or is caught speaking privately with a servant is presumed a spy and dealt with accordingly. Surface adventurers or other non-drow visitors are never permitted without formal sponsorship from a matron or high priestess — and even then, their presence is viewed with suspicion bordering on hostility.

## Government

Ys’Nonterien is governed by a Matron Council composed of the five ruling matriarchs — one from each of the Five Houses (Yerenduhl, Do’Aethir, Ssorghess, Taulurae, and Maerth). The Council convenes in a circular obsidian chamber called the Veil Hall, located at the exact geometric center of the cavern where all five chasms converge. Meetings are held under strict ritual protocol: each matron is seated at the point of a five-pointed star inscribed in the floor, with no matron allowed to speak out of turn or directly address another without the High Priestess’s permission. Decisions require at least four votes to pass; a three-to-two split results in deadlock and is referred to the priesthood for divine adjudication.

In practice, however, the Matron Council is a façade of shared power. True authority rests with the priestesses of the city’s unnamed dark goddess (referred to only as “She Who Weaves in Shadow” or “the Mistress of the Web”). The High Priestess — currently a formidable figure named Vaelithra Nyxveil — and her inner circle of temple matrons hold veto power over any Council decree that contradicts divine will, interpret omens from spider-silk auguries, and can declare any matron or House in violation of sacred law. The priestesses maintain their own standing force (the Veiled Watch) separate from the Silent Guard, and their temple complex beneath the Veil Hall contains private chambers where only they may enter.

House-to-House relations are managed through a web of formal alliances, blood-feuds, arranged marriages, and shadow-politics. Open warfare between Houses is forbidden by ancient compact (the Covenant of Five Blades, forged after the original coup), but proxy conflicts, sabotage, poisonings, and ritual duels are commonplace and tacitly encouraged as long as they do not threaten the city’s silk trade or the goddess’s favor. The priestesses exploit these rivalries to maintain balance — no single House is ever allowed to grow strong enough to dominate the others.

This creates a government that appears stable and theocratic on the surface but is riddled with hidden blades, making any prolonged stay in Ys’Nonterien a high-risk game of intrigue where even a minor misstep can ignite a city-wide purge.

### **House Yerenduhl**

House Yerenduhl is viewed as the refined and economically essential house, whose exquisite Rothenspinner silk outfits are coveted throughout the Underdark. Their loom-halls are considered near-sacred craft sites, where spider handlers and weavers labor under intricate rituals meant to ensure flawless silk.

They are respected—sometimes envied—for the prosperity their product brings.

### **House Do’Aethir**

House Do’Aethir is widely recognized as the strongest and most feared house, with unmatched warriors, disciplined priestesses, and prime access to the lake’s resources. They are the de facto power—even if the council is supposedly equal.

Their presence shapes the city’s tone: cold, hierarchical, vigilant.

### **House Ssorghess**

The oldest and most traditional of all houses, Ssorghess claims lineage back to the coup that overthrew the original research enclave and reformed it into the city’s modern religious structure.

They are the keepers of “true drow ways,” staunchly aggressive in doctrine, hierarchy, and ritual purity. They command loyalty among conservative priestesses. 

### **House Taulurae**

Considered the weakest house, Taulurae survives through guile, mastery of toxins, and near-invisible political maneuvers. Their poisons lace the daggers of most enforcers in the settlement, and the Silent Guard buys their concoctions regularly.  Nobody trusts them, but everyone relies on them. 

### **House Maerth**

Known for arcane brilliance, Maerth is respected—and feared—for its magical innovations. Their apprentices and mage-matrons produce spellcasters of unmatched talent, specializing in shadow-weaving, resonance magic, and silent-script ritualism.  They maintain the illusion of neutrality.

### **Silent Guard**

Day-to-day enforcement falls to the Silent Guard, an elite corps of drow warriors and assassins drawn from all five Houses but sworn first to the priesthood. They wear featureless black mail and hooded cloaks, carry poisoned hand crossbows and serrated short blades, and answer only to the High Priestess or her designated proxies. The Silent Guard polices chasm crossings, suppresses open House warfare (while allowing sanctioned duels and assassinations), hunts intruders, and executes those condemned by temple decree. Crossing a chasm bridge without explicit House permission or a priestess-issued sigil is a capital offense; violators are typically bound in command-silk and fed to the Rothenspinners as “sacrificial tribute.”
